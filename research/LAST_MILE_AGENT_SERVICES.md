# Last-mile agent services

**Status:** supporting category essay

**Revised:** 2026-09-01

**Relationship to the product:** this essay explains the demand side of
just-in-time service procurement. [PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md) defines the active
product. The [whitepaper](../AGENTIC_ECONOMY_AUSTRALIA_WHITEPAPER.md) develops the
commercial institution required to support it.

## The boundary inside agent work

An agent can reason only with the models, context, tools, data, permissions and
physical reach available to it. Better inference does not remove the outside
world.

During ordinary work, the agent will encounter a contribution it cannot produce
itself. It may need current inventory, a proprietary record, authenticated
access, specialist judgement, a regional quote, a simulation, a physical action
or a service created after its model was trained.

That missing contribution is a capability gap. The service that crosses it is a
last-mile agent service.

"Last mile" is relative to the next step, not the completion of the project. The
agent may be one hour into a long investigation when it needs a single registry
lookup. It may need one booking confirmation inside a broader travel plan. It
may need a specialist classification before it can continue a supply-chain
decision.

The agent keeps the larger objective. It buys one bounded contribution and
continues.

## A service selected during work

Consider an agent asked to find and book a restaurant that meets a user's
preferences. The agent can interpret cuisine, location, reviews and price. It
cannot see reliable live reservation inventory unless it reaches an outside
booking service.

The installed-connector model assumes that the booking service was selected
before the task began. The agent fills in parameters and calls it. That is useful
software integration, but Provider choice has already happened.

In a service market, the agent can discover several eligible booking Operations
after the need becomes specific. It can compare current coverage, price, terms,
data use, evidence and readiness, then choose within the user's mandate. The
selected Provider returns available slots or creates one reservation. The agent
incorporates the result and continues the user's project.

The distinction is not whether an API was called. It is whether the agent could
allocate the next unit of demand among Providers that were not permanently
chosen by the harness.

## Just-in-time service procurement

Conventional procurement establishes the Provider relationship before software
begins consuming the service. Just-in-time service procurement allows the exact
Provider and Operation to become known after a capability gap appears, then
binds them before Invocation.

This sequence is valuable when the contribution is:

- contingent on facts discovered during the task;
- specialised or regional;
- too infrequent to justify permanent integration;
- newly available;
- fragmented across many Providers; or
- valuable in the moment but too small for a human procurement cycle.

Frequent and strategically important services should still use direct contracts
and installed connectors. The category does not require all software
consumption to become an open market. It requires a meaningful class of needs
for which runtime choice is more useful than permanent preselection.

## The unit the agent buys

The market unit is the Operation.

An Operation is one versioned, callable contribution offered by one Provider.
It states the inputs, result, price, terms, data use, possible effects, readiness
and evidence. It is narrow enough for an agent to compare and for a Seller to
stand behind.

The production of an Operation need not be simple. One customs classification
may require a specialist firm. One data extraction may use private systems and
human review. One physical inspection may require a regional operator and
machine. The contribution is bounded from the consuming agent's perspective,
not by the internal complexity of its Provider.

This boundary allows service companies to participate without turning
themselves into thin API wrappers. They can sell complete outcomes directly and
also provide precise Operations to other agents.

## The principal is the customer; the agent allocates

The Business Principal owns the objective, grants authority and bears the
economic result. The Agent Principal acts for it through an Account and Mandate.

The agent becomes an allocator when it can recognise a capability gap, compare
eligible Operations and commit demand within that authority. It does not need
an unrestricted wallet. A human employee can make economically meaningful
choices inside a policy and budget. An agent can do the same.

The distinction matters because a payment limit is not a purchasing policy. The
Business Principal may care about Provider identity, service category, data
location, external effects, evidence, price and failure treatment. The
Commitment binds those conditions to one Operation before Invocation.

The agent acts. The Business Principal remains responsible. The product must
preserve both facts.

## A market requires more than discovery

A directory answers which services exist. A payment protocol answers how value
can move. Neither guarantees that the Business Principal has made an authorised
and remediable purchase.

For a last-mile service to enter ordinary business, the record must connect:

- the capability gap and considered Operations;
- the Business Principal, acting agent and authority;
- the exact Provider and fixed buyer-facing Seller;
- the service, terms, data use and effects;
- buyer consideration and the separate Provider obligation;
- Invocation and delivery evidence; and
- uncertainty, adjustment, refund or other remedy.

Commercial closure is reached when those facts form one terminal explainable
state. A successful payment can remain commercially open. A failed service can
be commercially closed when the promised remedy has been applied and recorded.

This completed state allows the Business Principal to delegate more than a
wallet transfer. It can delegate a bounded purchase.

## Connectors and markets will coexist

A connector answers, "What has already been installed for this agent?"

A market answers, "What available Operation can provide this missing
contribution now?"

MCP servers, APIs, CLIs, computer use and platform connectors are ways of
delivering supply. They are not alternatives to the market. Agentic Economy can
admit Operations reached through any of them when it can inspect the promise,
control Invocation and support the buyer-facing remedy.

The market will sit behind many agent environments rather than replacing them.
Each harness keeps its planning, memory and relationship with the user. Agentic
Economy owns the moment an outside contribution becomes a purchase.

## How supply responds

Providers make services agent-ready when qualified demand justifies the work.
They do not need to share a theory of autonomous commerce. They need to receive
incremental purchases and clear evidence about which Operations are selected,
delivered, repeated, replaced or remedied.

The possible supply is broad:

- live and proprietary data;
- authenticated access to existing applications;
- specialist models and computation;
- agent-native service companies;
- professional judgement packaged behind a bounded contract;
- inventory and fulfilment systems;
- physical machinery and regional operators; and
- agents that provide specialised work to other agents.

Providers do not need the same implementation. They need a precise Operation
and an upstream arrangement that lets Agentic Economy make the bounded
buyer-facing promise.

## Allocation and closure reinforce one another

Repeated allocation directs demand toward useful Operations. Commercial closure
makes that allocation safe enough for a business to authorise.

Each completed purchase can preserve the original need, viable alternatives,
exact Commitment, delivery, later use and remedy. Where comparable needs recur,
that evidence can improve later selection. Better selection can justify broader
Mandates. Broader Mandates create more qualified demand. Providers respond by
publishing more precise services and accepting common evidence rules.

The process does not compound from call count alone. The market must distinguish
Provider claims from observed facts and buyer reports. It must protect private
business context. An outcome from one task cannot become a universal quality
score. Buyers must permit the relevant evidence to inform later decisions.

## Australia as the first complete case

An Australian business that lets an agent buy an unfamiliar service must still
explain the transaction. It needs attributed parties, authority, consideration,
delivery, tax facts, data handling and retained records. Those obligations do
not disappear because the service cost three dollars or settled through x402.

This makes Australia a useful first implementation. A global Provider market can
sit upstream while Agentic Economy presents one local Seller and one purchase
record downstream. The same transaction core can later receive other
jurisdictional interpretations.

The Australian case changes the ambition. Agentic Economy is not trying to make
more endpoints callable. It is making an unfamiliar outside service purchasable
by software and recognisable by the institution that authorised it.

## What must be demonstrated

The category becomes concrete when an agent encounters a valuable gap, discovers
a Provider it was not configured to use, creates an exact Commitment, buys one
Operation, receives a usable contribution and returns to the market for a
comparable later need.

The company becomes necessary when the Business Principal can increase delegated
authority because Agentic Economy preserves one Seller, one remedy and one
explainable record across those changing Providers.

That is the line between a richer collection of connectors and an economy of
agent-purchased services.
