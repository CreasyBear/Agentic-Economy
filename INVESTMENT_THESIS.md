# Why We Would Invest in Agentic Economy

## The market for just-in-time external work

AI is making reasoning abundant. It is not making external reality freely available.

An increasingly capable agent can analyse a company, underwrite an opportunity, plan a campaign, investigate an incident or negotiate a purchase. But the workflow still stops when the agent needs something controlled by another economic principal: a licensed financial record, a current price, a bespoke dataset, reserved compute, a new observation, a research participant, a credentialed decision or work performed in the physical world.

Superintelligence does not eliminate property rights, data freshness, physical constraints or independent ownership. It increases the number and speed of decisions that encounter them.

Our thesis is that this creates a new market for **just-in-time external work**: transactions in which a business, user or agent acquires a current input, scarce capacity or bounded outcome from another business, user or agent at the moment a workflow requires it.

We would invest in the company that turns those runtime needs into structured purchases, brings external supply to them and makes delivery observable enough for the originating workflow to continue.

## External information and bounded work are already premium markets

Businesses already spend heavily on information they cannot produce internally. FactSet reported $2.406 billion in annual subscription value in FY2025, across 8,996 clients with at least $10,000 in subscription value. Thomson Reuters reported $2.789 billion of recurring revenue from its Legal Professionals segment in 2025. ZoomInfo reported $1.229 billion of subscription revenue and more than 35,000 customers in the same year. These products combine proprietary data, continuing updates, analytics and workflow access rather than selling model intelligence alone. [FactSet FY2025 results](https://investor.factset.com/news-releases/news-release-details/factset-reports-results-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2025/), [Thomson Reuters FY2025 results](https://ir.thomsonreuters.com/news-releases/news-release-details/thomson-reuters-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025), [ZoomInfo 2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794515/000179451526000012/zi-20251231.htm)

Demand for newly produced external work is equally concrete. Upwork reported $4.028 billion in gross services volume and 785,000 active clients in 2025. HackerOne reported $81 million in bounty payouts during 2025. Prolific publishes examples of customers paying to create new participant datasets within hours, including one research programme that recruited more than 76,000 participants for over 91,000 AI conversations in nine weeks. [Upwork FY2025 results](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upwork-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial), [HackerOne 2025 report](https://www.hackerone.com/report/hacker-powered-security), [Prolific research case](https://www.prolific.com/resources/how-a-leading-ai-research-organization-ran-one-of-the-largest-ai-behavioral-experiments)

These figures are evidence of existing paid demand, not a calculation of the Agentic Economy's addressable market. They establish the underlying behaviour: organisations repeatedly pay independent parties for information, access and outcomes that internal software cannot supply.

## The constraint is moving from reasoning to acquisition

The Model Context Protocol gives AI applications a standard way to connect to external data sources and tools. When that connection reaches a system for which the user or business already has credentials and entitlements, no new market transaction has occurred. An employee's agent reading the company's CRM is an intra-business integration, not a market transaction. [MCP introduction](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/getting-started/intro)

The economic boundary appears when the required input belongs to an independent principal.

A useful test is:

> Does one principal need to acquire something from another principal, at the moment a workflow requires it, under predefined authority, budget and delivery conditions?

Five properties define the category:

1. **Inter-party:** buyer and provider are independent economic principals.
2. **Just in time:** the requirement emerges during execution rather than being fully provisioned in advance.
3. **Externally scarce:** another party controls the information, rights, capacity or work.
4. **Bounded:** the request, price ceiling and delivery conditions can be expressed before execution.
5. **Observable:** the system can record what was requested, purchased and returned, and whether stated service conditions were met.

The fifth property matters because reliable output, not an abstract reputation claim, determines whether the workflow can continue.

## Existing procurement was designed for people and predictable demand

Most external services are packaged around accounts, subscriptions, seats, annual contracts, API keys and human procurement. That structure works for predictable, high-volume requirements. It works poorly for the long tail of contextual purchases that appear while an agent is executing a task.

The alternative markets also carry structural limitations:

- API directories expose supply but rarely express the buyer's complete runtime requirement.
- Data subscriptions require buyers to predict which datasets they will need and purchase broad access in advance.
- Freelance markets assume a human will write the brief, compare proposals, communicate with providers and review the result.
- Bounties and contests often pay only after multiple suppliers have performed speculative work, creating review and dispute operations.
- Traditional procurement can handle large contracts but cannot economically approve thousands of small, context-dependent purchases.

Payment infrastructure is arriving ahead of the market. Coinbase's x402 protocol allows a human or machine client to pay for an API response directly over HTTP. Solana's pay.sh similarly wraps paid API calls and presents a catalogue of metered endpoints. These systems reduce account and settlement friction. They do not determine what should be purchased, whether the purchase fits delegated authority, which provider can meet the full requirement, or how the buyer observes execution. [x402 documentation](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/welcome), [pay.sh](https://pay.sh/)

That missing layer is the investment opportunity.

## Early demand aggregation shows the shape of the market

Straitly offers an instructive early example in inference capacity. Its customer application asks buyers to disclose current providers, models, monthly spend, throughput and use case. Its provider application captures the matching supply variables: models served, sustained token capacity, latency, price, uptime, region, data policy and infrastructure.

After a viral launch, Straitly reported 903 applications and $1.11 million per month of "pledged" token spend. The underlying Demand Book labels these figures as self-reported application data and derives spend using the floor of each selected budget band. Public sources do not show signed commitments, converted customers, settled spend, provider payouts or retention. The result therefore demonstrates qualified budget intent, not proven marketplace liquidity. [Straitly launch thread](https://x.com/Mutchtaba2/status/2090107278137692592?s=20), [Straitly provider application](https://straitly.ai/apply/providers)

The important GTM pattern is the construction of a demand book around an existing bill. Straitly did not begin by asking whether people liked an inference marketplace. It asked what they already bought, how much they bought and what capacity they would move.

The business-services analogue should do the same.

## The initial wedge: just-in-time external evidence

We would not begin with a horizontal marketplace for every possible service. The first market should concentrate on **external evidence** required for a high-value workflow to take its next action.

That supply can include:

- a current record or licensed data point;
- a query-specific slice from a proprietary dataset;
- a bespoke dataset assembled against a defined schema;
- a newly collected observation, measurement or participant response;
- a derived result produced from inaccessible source data;
- a credentialed, machine-readable determination.

Financial and business information is a strong hunting ground because buyers already pay for current, proprietary data and much of the output is structured. It is not automatically the winning beachhead: licensing restrictions, regulated uses and incumbent bundling may prevent an open transaction market. The wedge must be selected through evidence of movable, recurring spend rather than catalogue activity.

We would look for a category where:

- buyers already pay external providers;
- requests occur frequently but are difficult to predict individually;
- information loses value quickly as it becomes stale;
- the request and response can be represented in a machine-readable schema;
- delivery conditions can be defined before purchase;
- fulfilment can be observed without a subjective review operation;
- purchases are valuable enough to support a transaction margin but too small or numerous for manual procurement.

The GTM instrument is a **Business Work Demand Book**. Buyers disclose current external spend, unit volume, present supplier, turnaround requirement and demand they would move at stated terms. Providers disclose capacity, price, service conditions and operational constraints against the same unit. A market opens only after one category shows concentrated demand and compatible supply.

## The company we would back

The winning company will not look like a wallet attached to an API directory. It will operate as the demand and execution layer between principals.

The product must:

1. identify the missing external input inside an active workflow;
2. convert that need into an executable purchase plan;
3. discover providers capable of satisfying the complete specification;
4. enforce the buyer's delegated authority, budget and policy boundaries;
5. contract, pay or reserve capacity using the appropriate rail;
6. expose execution status and delivery telemetry;
7. return the result to the originating agent in a usable form.

Value accrues through three potential revenue streams:

- a take rate or negotiated spread on cleared external spend;
- enterprise software fees for authority, policy and observability controls;
- supplier economics from aggregated volume, guaranteed demand or premium placement tied to measurable performance.

The defensible asset is not the payment protocol. Durable advantage should come from embedded demand, normalised purchase specifications, observed provider performance, enterprise policy integration and the ability to aggregate commitments that no individual buyer can negotiate alone.

## Why now

Three enabling layers are becoming available simultaneously:

- models can formulate and execute increasingly complex workflows;
- MCP and tool schemas make external capabilities machine-callable;
- x402, pay.sh and related protocols make request-level payment technically possible.

There is early evidence that businesses are moving from conversational assistance toward tool-mediated completion. Ramp reports that adoption of its MCP and command-line interfaces grew from 275 to more than 2,500 businesses, with 62% of observed usage classified as completion-oriented. Apollo reports 42,000 MCP queries in 30 days, including recurring enrichment workflows. These are provider-reported product signals, not evidence that agents are independently purchasing from external counterparties. [Ramp customer analysis](https://ramp.com/blog/ramp-customers-are-running-month-end-with-ai-assistants), [Apollo MCP analysis](https://www.apollo.io/magazine/the-top-10-use-cases-of-apollo-mcp-based-on-42k-queries)

The market layer remains immature. Public evidence from current agent-service catalogues generally exposes calls, payers or application claims without joining buyer, requirement, payment, delivery and repeat usage. That absence is not evidence of failure, but it sets the present evidence ceiling: the rails exist; autonomous inter-party demand has not yet been publicly demonstrated at meaningful scale.

This creates a window to build the demand, authority and execution layer before payment and discovery become commodity infrastructure.

## What would falsify the thesis

We would abandon or materially narrow this thesis if field evidence shows that:

- agents' economically meaningful external needs are predictable enough to remain subscriptions or pre-negotiated enterprise contracts;
- runtime purchases are too infrequent to create repeat demand;
- the cost of supplier onboarding, fulfilment monitoring and disputes exceeds the available transaction margin;
- buyers will not delegate bounded purchasing authority to agents;
- providers refuse transaction-level distribution or cannot expose machine-readable capacity;
- most outputs require expensive subjective review after delivery;
- incumbent data and SaaS vendors absorb agent demand into existing licences;
- demand fragments across too many incompatible job types to create liquidity in any initial category.

The decisive validation milestone is not registrations, API calls or pledged spend. It is repeated, paid, inter-party fulfilment of runtime needs—with the buyer, requirement, purchase, delivery and downstream continuation observable in the same trace.

## Investment conclusion

The next generation of agents will not be constrained only by what they can reason about. They will be constrained by what they can obtain from the rest of the economy quickly enough to complete the job.

MCP connects agents to known tools. Payment protocols let agents transfer value. Neither creates the market in which independent parties expose current data, scarce capacity and bounded work in a form agents can acquire at runtime.

Agentic Economy can occupy that missing layer: the market for just-in-time external work across businesses, agents and users.

We would invest when a team proves the first dense demand book—one existing budget category, one recurring unit of external work, compatible provider capacity and repeated paid fulfilment. From there, the platform can expand from data calls to bespoke datasets, from datasets to bounded digital services, and eventually to credentialed and physical-world outcomes.
