# Regional agent-services market and wedge hypotheses

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Status:** Decision support for a founder hypothesis; not category authority or wedge authority.  
**Decision posture:** Explore customer demand and lawful source access before implementation.

## 1. Working hypothesis

Agentic Economy should test whether a regional, jurisdiction-specific agent-service market can begin with a repeated buyer job rather than with a data source or a broad category claim.

The current service family can remain **public-document structured extraction**. A separate, testable buyer wedge is **WA property/planning site-constraints screen**. These are different axes:

- **Service family:** the comparable operation supplied to a buyer: retrieve, extract, structure, verify, and package public documents or map-derived constraints.
- **Buyer wedge:** the narrow repeated job, buyer, and decision context that makes the operation worth buying.
- **Region:** jurisdiction-specific data, rules, and provider networks. It does not mean local trades, an Australian-SMB category, or BAS work.

This is a discovery direction, not a frozen product decision. The V1 public-document structured-extraction premise remains intact. [S1][S2][S3]

## 2. Authority boundary

This note records hypotheses and a research plan. It does not establish:

- a market-size claim, category definition, customer traction, willingness to pay, or supplier earnings;
- permission to automate, resell, scrape, redistribute, or use any Landgate product or service for agents;
- planning, legal, title, environmental, mining, transport, or grid advice;
- completeness, currency, accuracy, or legal effect of any dataset;
- hosted production, independent market liquidity, customer value, or production settlement for Agentic Economy.

The platform remains a market and transaction layer for supplier-hosted services. It can verify services before listing them, identify each call, enforce the buyer's permissions, preserve evidence, measure use, reconcile payments, and manage the payment boundary; it does not run supplier services. [S2][S3]

Any pilot must state what was retrieved, from which source, under which access terms, at what time, with what verification and human review. A source owner or licensed professional remains authoritative where the underlying decision requires it.

## 3. Global versus regional market

The global comparison is not “all AI agents.” The useful comparison is a market in which an agent, acting within a buyer's permissions, finds and uses a clearly defined service with a clear input, output, price, evidence trail, and supplier boundary.

A regional version adds constraints that do not travel automatically:

1. jurisdiction-specific datasets and identifiers;
2. local planning, title, environment, mining, transport, and grid rules;
3. source licences, access controls, terms, and redistribution limits;
4. local professional or data suppliers who can verify and interpret results;
5. buyer workflows tied to a regional decision or filing.

The global layer supplies a pattern for agent-ready service discovery. The regional layer supplies the job context and source/supplier graph. Neither layer proves demand. Agentic Market and Coinbase Bazaar show how services can be discovered and used; they are not evidence of a WA market or AE traction. [S18][S19]

## 4. Expansion unit

Use this unit for every expansion decision:

`region × repeated buyer job × source/supplier graph`

Where:

- **Region** names the jurisdiction and its data/rule boundary.
- **Repeated buyer job** names the decision or workflow that recurs often enough to test.
- **Source/supplier graph** names authoritative sources, access/licence conditions, extraction capability, verification capability, interpretation capability, and delivery channel.

Do not expand by country, dataset count, or API count alone. A new region is not an expansion unit until a repeated buyer job and a viable source/supplier graph are evidenced.

## 5. Service family versus buyer wedge

| Axis | Working choice | What must be learned |
|---|---|---|
| Comparable service family | Public-document structured extraction | Which document/map evidence can be retrieved and structured lawfully and repeatably? |
| Candidate buyer wedge | WA property/planning site-constraints screen | Which buyer repeatedly needs the screen, before which decision, and what failure does it prevent? |
| Possible adjacent wedges | Heavy-haul route, environment/clearing, mining title, grid pre-feasibility, cross-council DA monitor, title/encumbrance | Whether each is a repeated job rather than an attractive dataset |
| Platform role | Listing checks, buyer permissions, evidence, usage measurement, payment reconciliation | Whether a supplier-hosted service deserves listing and repeat use |

The wedge should be selected by buyer frequency, decision value, evidence burden, licensing feasibility, and bounded liability—not by the apparent authority or novelty of a source.

## 6. Monopoly-source market test

An authoritative or scarce source is a **gateway**. It is not, by itself, a market.

Test the source and the market separately:

- Can an eligible buyer lawfully access the source?
- Can the relevant facts be extracted into a stable, reviewable output?
- Can an independent party verify the extraction and freshness?
- Is interpretation needed, and who is qualified to provide it?
- Can the result be packaged into a buyer's existing decision or workflow?
- Is the buyer job repeated, urgent, costly, or risk-sensitive enough to pay for?
- Can more than one supplier or capability participate, even if one source is authoritative?

Competition may exist in licensed access, extraction, verification, interpretation, monitoring, workflow integration, or packaged delivery. If the only defensible asset is a monopoly source, the proposition is a source-access dependency, not yet a market thesis.

## 7. Source and licensing realities

The supplied WA sources indicate a mixed source graph: cadastral and title products, planning maps and services, industry and mining layers, network tools, environmental search/layers, and transport services. They must be assessed as separate products with separate terms and operational limits.

A map layer, REST endpoint, search page, certificate, and online enquiry service are not interchangeable evidence. A visible endpoint is not proof of permission to automate, cache, resell, or use through an agent.

For every proposed operation, record:

- source owner and exact product or layer;
- access method and authentication, if any;
- licence, terms, attribution, rate, storage, derivative-output, and redistribution conditions;
- permitted buyer and delivery channel;
- retrieval timestamp, version or query parameters;
- extraction and verification method;
- human review and escalation owner;
- claims the output must not make.

**Landgate boundary:** the Landgate links below are leads for licence and product diligence. This note makes no claim that SLIP, cadastral data, LGATE-217, certificates of title, or Land Enquiry Services permit automation, resale, or agent use. That permission must be confirmed directly from the applicable terms or written source-owner response. [S4][S5][S6][S7][S8][S9]

## 8. Ranked candidate hypotheses

Ranking is a discovery priority, not a market-size ranking. “Incumbent extraction” preserves the current service family; the other rows are buyer-wedge hypotheses.

| Rank | Candidate operation | Repeated buyer job to test | Source/supplier graph | Main uncertainty |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incumbent public-document extraction | Turn a named public document set into a structured, cited pack | Public source → lawful retrieval → extraction → verification → packaged delivery | Whether the job is frequent and valuable beyond a one-off research request |
| 2 | WA property/planning site-constraints screen | Screen a site before acquisition, planning, design, or diligence proceeds | Cadastral/title lead → PlanWA/DPLH zoning → relevant constraints → reviewer/interpreter → decision pack | Buyer, scope boundary, licence, and interpretation responsibility |
| 3 | Heavy-haul route screen | Check an intended movement against route/network constraints before planning | Main Roads HVS/travel layers → route extraction → transport/logistics reviewer → route brief | Actual workflow frequency, currentness, and whether the source supports the needed decision |
| 4 | Environmental/clearing screen | Identify environmental or clearing constraints early in a site decision | EPA proposal search → DWER layer → environmental reviewer → evidence pack | Definitions, completeness, source rights, and professional interpretation |
| 5 | Mining-title screen | Screen an area for relevant tenement/title constraints before a commercial decision | DMIRS tenement layer/MTO → title extraction → mining-title specialist → dated screen | Access terms, title significance, and qualified interpretation |
| 6 | Grid pre-feasibility screen | Gather initial network-context facts before a connection conversation | Western Power NCMT → supplied project inputs → connection professional → pre-feasibility brief | Tool scope, non-binding nature, freshness, and buyer willingness to pay |
| 7 | Cross-council DA monitor | Monitor specified planning/DA signals across councils for a recurring workflow | Council/PlanWA sources → change detection → planner/reviewer → alert pack | Cross-council comparability, lawful monitoring, and signal-to-noise |
| 8 | Title/encumbrance pack | Assemble title-related evidence for a defined diligence checkpoint | Landgate product/access path → licensed retrieval → conveyancing/title professional → cited pack | Landgate permissions, certificate authority, liability, and a repeatable buyer job |

## 9. Recommended discovery wedge (not frozen)

Start discovery with **WA property/planning site-constraints screen** as the leading wedge hypothesis while retaining incumbent extraction as the comparable service family.

The reason to test it first is structural, not evidentiary: it can connect a regional source graph to a concrete site decision, and it can expose whether buyers value extraction, verification, interpretation, or packaged delivery. The supplied material does not yet prove that buyers need it, can pay for it, or will accept an agent-mediated service.

A discovery offer should be described narrowly:

> “For a defined WA site and a defined decision, assemble a dated, source-cited screen of agreed public planning/property constraints, identify uncertainty and review points, and route interpretation to the appropriate human or supplier.”

Do not promise a title search, legal opinion, planning approval, environmental clearance, development advice, or completeness. Do not promise automation or resale of Landgate data. The exact source set and output must follow licence and buyer interviews.

## 10. Typed output sketch

This is a discussion shape, not an implementation contract:

```ts
SiteConstraintsScreen {
  region: "WA";
  subject: { siteReference: string; geometry?: unknown };
  buyerJob: string;
  decisionDate?: string;
  findings: Array<{
    category: "planning" | "property" | "environment" | "other";
    statement: string;
    source: { owner: string; product: string; locator: string; retrievedAt: string };
    status: "observed" | "needs_verification" | "referred";
    confidence: "low" | "medium" | "high";
    reviewBy?: string;
    claimBoundary: string;
  }>;
  licenceBasis: string;
  excludedClaims: string[];
  evidencePack: string[];
}
```

Required behavior for any later design: preserve source locators and timestamps, separate observed facts from interpretation, fail closed when access or licence is unclear, and expose human-review referrals rather than filling gaps with model inference.

## 11. Supplier graph

A viable regional operation is a graph, not a single endpoint:

`buyer job → input/site reference → source access → extraction → verification → interpretation → packaged delivery → evidence/reconciliation`

Potential node types to interview, not assume:

- source owners and licensed data distributors;
- public-document and geospatial extraction providers;
- planners, surveyors, conveyancing/title specialists, environmental consultants, transport specialists, mining-title specialists, and connection professionals;
- buyer-side property, development, infrastructure, logistics, energy, mining, and professional-services teams;
- delivery and workflow systems used by those buyers.

The platform should admit only a bounded operation whose supplier boundary, evidence behavior, authority, and commercial terms are understood. Supplier-hosted expertise may be the scarce part; AE should not silently impersonate it.

## 12. Why not title first

Title/encumbrance is a credible hypothesis but a poor first wedge until discovery resolves several authority questions:

- what exact Landgate product is needed for the buyer job;
- whether the access and licence permit the proposed retrieval, storage, derivative output, redistribution, and agent use;
- whether a certificate or enquiry result is authoritative for the buyer's decision;
- who is qualified to interpret interests, encumbrances, or limitations;
- what review, liability, and escalation path applies;
- whether the job repeats often enough to support a service rather than a one-off search.

Title may become a high-value operation after those questions are answered. It should not be treated as the default wedge merely because the source is authoritative.

## 13. Eight-week discovery and pilot

**Weeks 1–2 — job discovery**

- Interview buyers across property/development, planning, infrastructure, energy, logistics, mining, environment, and professional services.
- Ask for the last real site or decision, the inputs used, sources consulted, elapsed time, rework, and who signed off.
- Capture exact repeated job language; do not pitch a platform first.
- Interview source owners/licensed distributors and potential reviewers about lawful access and output boundaries.

**Weeks 3–4 — narrow the graph**

- Select one buyer job and one WA scope only if the interviews show repetition.
- Write a source/terms matrix for every proposed source.
- Identify extraction, verification, interpretation, and delivery suppliers.
- Define the smallest evidence pack and explicit exclusions.

**Weeks 5–6 — manual, bounded pilot**

- Run a small number of buyer-requested screens manually, using only confirmed access paths and agreed scope.
- Record inputs, retrieval timestamps, source locators, reviewer actions, uncertainty, turnaround, and rework.
- Ask the buyer to use the pack in the real workflow; do not claim a decision outcome not observed.

**Weeks 7–8 — commercial and authority review**

- Compare repeated use, buyer effort saved, review burden, supplier economics, and licence fit.
- Ask whether the buyer would repeat or pay under the bounded scope.
- Decide pass, revise, or kill the wedge; only then decide whether an agent-ready service deserves implementation work.

## 14. Interview targets and questions

**Targets:** recent buyers and operators in property acquisition/development, planning, surveying, conveyancing/title, environmental consulting, transport/logistics, mining, renewable/BESS development, grid connection, and council-facing professional services; source owners and licensed distributors; potential verification and interpretation suppliers.

Ask:

1. “Tell me about the last site/route/title/approval decision you screened.”
2. “What exact job recurs, for whom, and how often?”
3. “Which sources did you use, and which result was trusted?”
4. “What did you copy, structure, verify, or explain manually?”
5. “Where did delay, rework, uncertainty, or missed constraints appear?”
6. “Which output would fit your existing decision pack?”
7. “What may a software or agent service state, and what must a qualified person review?”
8. “Who owns the source rights, and what delivery or storage restrictions matter?”
9. “What would make you repeat this next month, and what would make you reject it?”
10. “Would you pay for a bounded pilot? If yes, for what deliverable and acceptance test?”

## 15. Pass and kill gates

These are proposed decision gates, not observed evidence.

**Pass to a bounded pilot when:**

- at least several independent interviewees describe the same recurring buyer job;
- a named buyer supplies a real, bounded case and acceptance criteria;
- each source has a confirmed access/licence path for the proposed use, or is explicitly excluded;
- extraction can preserve evidence and a qualified reviewer accepts the authority boundary;
- a buyer repeats the workflow or commits to a paid/credible pilot;
- supplier and review effort leave a plausible delivery and reconciliation model.

**Kill or return to discovery when:**

- the need is a one-off dataset hunt with no repeated buyer job;
- source terms or permissions cannot support the intended delivery;
- the output would be mistaken for legal, planning, title, environmental, mining, transport, or grid advice;
- no qualified reviewer or source owner accepts the claim boundary;
- buyers value only an unbounded bespoke service with no repeatable output;
- the buyer will not provide a real case, repeat, pay, or name an acceptance test.

## 16. Unknown / no-claim list

- Landgate permission for automation, resale, caching, redistribution, derivative outputs, or agent use.
- Exact product-level terms, fees, rate limits, authentication, update cadence, and retention rights.
- Completeness or legal authority of any map, layer, search result, REST service, title product, or enquiry result.
- Whether any candidate job is frequent, urgent, valuable, or budgeted by a reachable buyer.
- Which professionals may interpret each result and what liability/insurance boundary applies.
- Whether council, EPA, DWER, DMIRS, Western Power, Main Roads, PlanWA, DPLH, or Landgate sources can be combined lawfully for a delivered pack.
- Whether a regional supplier graph can support competition beyond a single source gateway.
- Whether any candidate service should be listed on AE, and at what price or payment model.
- Any claim of market liquidity, hosted production, customer value, supplier earnings, or production settlement.

## 17. Decision tree

```text
Start with a buyer's recent decision
  └─ Is the job repeated and narrowly describable?
       ├─ No → keep interviewing; do not build
       └─ Yes
            └─ Can each required source be accessed and used lawfully?
                 ├─ No/unknown → exclude or obtain written clarification
                 └─ Yes
                      └─ Can facts be extracted with locators and timestamps?
                           ├─ No → do not promise structured service
                           └─ Yes
                                └─ Is verification/interpretation bounded and assigned?
                                     ├─ No → refer or kill the wedge
                                     └─ Yes
                                          └─ Will a buyer repeat/pay for the evidence pack?
                                               ├─ No → kill or revise the job
                                               └─ Yes → run bounded pilot, then assess listing
```
