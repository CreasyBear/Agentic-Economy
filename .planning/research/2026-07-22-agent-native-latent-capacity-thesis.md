# Agent-native companies and latent economic capacity

**Status:** Idea under exploration  
**Decision affected:** Whether AE should explore market creation, not merely agent-service discovery  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-07-22

## The idea

As companies become agent-native, they can expose more than conventional software APIs. They can make small, bounded units of their knowledge, judgement, access, infrastructure and operating capacity available at the moment another agent needs them.

Much of this capacity already exists but is economically dormant. It may sit in internal databases, specialist workflows, equipment downtime, supplier relationships, local knowledge, compliance expertise, logistics coverage, or the recurring observations produced while running a business. Historically, it was too costly to identify a buyer, define a product, negotiate access and deliver a small transaction.

Agents can reduce those costs. A company may no longer need to sell a database subscription or build a separate SaaS product. It may be able to sell one verified answer, calculation, check, introduction, reservation or action for a specific purpose.

The opportunity is therefore larger than an API directory:

> Make previously invisible business capacity legible to agents, create demand for it, and turn it into repeatable economic activity.

## AE's possible role

Agentic Market is an early discovery and payment surface for services already packaged for agents. AE could explore the market-creation layer before and around that substrate:

1. **Reveal latent supply.** Help companies recognise what they possess that could become a bounded agent-native product.
2. **Reveal unmet demand.** Identify decisions and objectives for which agents cannot currently obtain a satisfactory answer or action.
3. **Form new products.** Connect the latent capacity to a specific customer need, price, permission boundary and observable result.
4. **Create the market language.** Establish categories that let buyers recognise unfamiliar supply and suppliers understand what is being requested.
5. **Make exchange repeatable.** Preserve enough trust, evidence and continuity that both sides return rather than treating the first transaction as a novelty.

AE's defensible asset would not initially be the API, payment protocol or catalogue. It could become the proprietary map between:

> what companies can uniquely provide -> which objectives it improves -> who will pay -> how it must be bounded -> whether it created value

That map compounds only if it is built from real transactions and outcomes. A taxonomy, prompt library or large untested registry does not establish it.

## What latent capacity might look like

- A logistics operator sells a bounded assessment of real delivery feasibility in a difficult region.
- A manufacturer exposes current spare production capacity or a manufacturability check.
- An insurer sells a narrowly permitted risk signal rather than its underlying claims data.
- A specialist firm sells one jurisdiction-specific compliance determination.
- A marketplace sells an aggregated demand or pricing signal that does not disclose participant records.
- A field-services company exposes verified local availability, inspection or fulfilment capacity.
- A research business sells a sourced answer assembled from private information rather than access to the whole database.

The capacity need not be data. It can be judgement, access, coordination, physical availability or the ability to perform an action.

## Australia first: what the economy actually contains

Australia is not simply a “resources economy” or a “services economy.” It is both, in different measures. At 30 June 2025 the ABS counted **2.73 million actively trading businesses**, but only **994,178 employed anyone**. Construction alone contained 462,939 businesses, professional/scientific/technical services 353,261, rental/hiring/real estate 308,127, transport/postal/warehousing 249,289, health/social assistance 213,177, agriculture/forestry/fishing 170,890 and retail 156,169. Mining had only 8,663 businesses. ([ABS business counts](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/jul2021-jun2025))

Business count therefore badly understates concentrated sectors. In 2023–24 mining produced **$312.2 billion of industry value added (IVA)** with 235,000 employees, while construction produced $177.1 billion with 1.29 million employees and professional/scientific/technical services $196.6 billion with 1.32 million. Private health/social assistance produced $152.1 billion with 1.80 million employees. By December 2025, the broader health/social-assistance industry employed 2.48 million people, versus 1.50 million in retail, 1.32 million in construction and 1.30 million in professional services. ([ABS Australian Industry](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/industry-overview/australian-industry/2023-24); [ABS Labour Account](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/labour-accounts/labour-account-australia/dec-2025))

Exports add the third lens. Resources and energy earned about **$385 billion in 2024–25**. Agriculture, fisheries and forestry produced $100.3 billion and exported $75.8 billion; 71% of agricultural production by volume was exported. Education-related travel was Australia's fourth-largest export in DFAT's 2024–25 ranking. Australia's digital activity is economically important but does not sit cleanly in one ABS industry: a 2026 Australian Government review, citing the Tech Council, estimates technology contributes $167 billion and more than 900,000 jobs. Treat that estimate as a broader technology footprint, not as directly comparable to ABS IVA. ([DISR resources](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/resources-and-energy-quarterly-june-2026); [ABARES agriculture](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/abares/products/insights/snapshot-of-australian-agriculture); [DFAT exports](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/australias-goods-services-by-top-25-exports-2024-25.pdf); [Australian Government R&D review](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/strategic-examination-research-and-development))

This creates five practical Australian economies, but industry labels are too coarse to reveal the opportunity. The useful unit is the **record created while a business decides or operates**. The following inventories are hypotheses about products, grounded in records Australian operators are known or required to keep; they are not claims that the records are available for sale.

### What a mining company actually holds

Public repositories show the shape of the private record estate. Geoscience Australia exposes geology, geophysics, geochemistry, boreholes, samples, hydrology and mineral-deposit layers. The commercial opportunity is rarely “sell the drilling database.” It is a narrow answer produced from several objects. Examples: **water-constrained pit viability** (resource model + aquifer model + pump history + water licence); **credible ramp-up range** (mine schedule + fleet cycles + plant recovery + maintenance history); **equipment fit for Pilbara conditions** (failure history + payload/cycle + dust/temperature + parts lead times); and **acquisition liability envelope** (closure criteria + monitoring trend + disturbance geometry + actual rehabilitation performance). Each is a new decision product; each can be useful without transferring the underlying records.

The most valuable combinations cross the project lifecycle: **construction-readiness underwriting** (design maturity + unresolved RFIs + MTO volatility + vendor status + historical rework); **commissioning realism** (system topology + completion status + test/punch history + analogous actuals); **substitution assurance** (specification + process duty + vendor deviations + NCR/operating history); and **early project overrun warning** (estimate assumptions + design churn + RFI/change rate + discipline-hour actuals). These are commercially credible because owners already pay consultants to reconstruct them manually. The hypothesis to test is whether a firm can sell each as a bounded, attributed answer without surrendering client files or accepting uncontrolled design liability.

This makes a more specific Australian thesis: the exportable asset is not generic “mining expertise.” It is the **closed loop between design assumptions and operating truth** accumulated across Australian projects. A mine operator knows what happened; an engineering firm knows why it was designed that way; contractors and vendors know what was actually buildable and available. With permissioned combination, those partial views can form decision products none can offer alone.

The regional pattern matters. WA and Queensland make resource, agriculture, freight and remote-service wedges unusually credible. NSW and Victoria concentrate finance, professional, digital and education capacity. These are starting hypotheses for fieldwork, not claims that those markets already exist.

## The ontology is the latent economic asset

The useful question is not merely “what data does this company store?” It is: **what world does the company know well enough to describe, relate, observe and act upon?** Microsoft's Ontology Playground makes this concrete: an ontology contains typed entities and properties, relationships and cardinalities, and supports natural-language questions that resolve through that graph. ([Microsoft Ontology Playground](https://github.com/microsoft/Ontology-Playground))

For AE, a company is not simply a data supplier. It maintains a partial **operating model of reality**:

- entities: the things it knows about;
- relationships: how those things affect or depend on one another;
- events and measurements: what happened, where, when and under what conditions;
- decisions and outcomes: what was chosen, why, and what followed;
- rights and provenance: where each fact came from and what may be disclosed;
- capabilities: which questions it can answer or actions it can perform reliably.

That last layer is the commercial one. The ontology converts records into capabilities; the market converts capabilities into bounded decision products.

### Engineering services company: the actual delivery graph

An engineering firm holds a different but connected ontology: clients, engagements, projects, sites, assets, systems and subsystems; requirements, design bases, assumptions, constraints, codes and standards; survey/GIS and point clouds; calculations and simulations; CAD/BIM/IFC objects; PFDs, P&IDs, drawings and specifications; equipment tags and datasheets; bills and quantities; vendor submissions and deviations; estimates, schedules, work breakdowns, hours and costs; reviews, approvals, revisions and change requests; RFIs, clashes, defects and nonconformances; HAZOP, LOPA, FMEA and risk registers; inspections, tests, commissioning results, punch lists and as-builts; lessons learned, templates, expert directories and stage-gate workflows.

Its real knowledge lies in the edges:

> this requirement produced this design; this assumption drove this calculation; this equipment item satisfied this specification; this review found this failure mode; this revision resolved it; this supplier deviation caused this rework; this estimated quantity and effort became this actual cost and schedule; this commissioned asset performed this way.

OpenBIM standards already illustrate the structure: IFC represents project and asset information, while information requirements, execution plans, object properties and classification make it exchangeable across a lifecycle. BIM objects can carry structural, thermal and acoustic properties, power needs, manufacturer, cost and maintenance requirements—not merely geometry. ([buildingSMART Australasia](https://www.buildingsmart.org.au/); [NATSPEC BIM](https://natspec.com.au/images/PDF/BIM_Brochure_2023.pdf); [NATSPEC information classification](https://www.natspec.com.au/images/TECHreports/TECHreport-TR-02-Information-classification-systems-and-the-Australian-construction-industry.pdf))

## Services as the first market anchor

Services firms may be the easier starting point than asset owners. Their ordinary work already converts messy evidence into a decision, recommendation or completed action. The latent product is therefore not invented from a database: it is a smaller, repeatable unit extracted from an engagement customers already buy manually.

The following map distinguishes **records held** from a **hypothesised product**. It does not assume a firm owns every client fact or may reuse it.

### What is common across these businesses

The shared asset is not “documents.” Each service firm has accumulated a **decision-and-outcome graph**:

> client condition -> evidence considered -> constraint or rule -> professional decision -> action -> exception -> observed outcome

That creates four recurring products: a **readiness determination** before a commitment; an **exception or gap check** before failure; a **reference-class forecast** grounded in previous outcomes; and an **assured action** whose evidence standard is declared in advance. The agent matters because it can gather the client-specific state, call several narrow capabilities, preserve provenance and return only when human judgement or fresh authority is needed.

Personal information remains bounded by purpose. Under APP 6 an organisation generally may use or disclose personal information only for the purpose collected unless consent or another exception applies; “we can technically derive it” is not a resale right. ([OAIC APP 6](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-6-app-6-use-or-disclosure-of-personal-information)) The tractable first products should therefore use the buyer's own authorised facts, public records, the supplier's own operating data, and carefully tested non-identifying precedent.

### Composite products that no single service firm can answer

| Composite decision product | Capabilities composed | Exact decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Can we sign this project contract and still deliver profitably?** | Legal clause/obligation extraction + insurance coverage-gap check + engineering scope completeness + tax treatment + freight/field capacity | Contractor's commercial director deciding whether to sign and what qualifications, price and contingency are required |
| **Can this imported machine be operating on site by date?** | Engineering specification/substitution + customs classification and permits + international freight + road-route feasibility + installer/maintenance availability + insurance | Operator deciding whether to place the purchase order and promise a commissioning date |
| **Is this property genuinely ready to acquire and operate?** | Title/use constraints + environmental survey sufficiency + building condition/maintenance forecast + insurance availability + tax treatment | SME, investor or lender deciding whether to transact and how much latent liability to price |
| **Is this asset or project ready for finance?** | Accounting close/readiness + engineering maturity and contingency + environmental evidence + insurance terms + legal/title status | Lender or investment committee deciding whether conditions precedent are satisfied and which remain material |
| **Can this regional job actually be fulfilled?** | Current parts/equipment + credentialled field labour + freight/route + site access + insurance/compliance | Seller or public procurer deciding whether to make a binding service promise rather than an indicative referral |

### Initial service wedges, ranked

Scores are directional hypotheses from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), intended to choose fieldwork rather than claim a market.

| Wedge | Demand frequency | Rights tractability | Repeatability | Low marginal cost | Outcome observable | Total / 25 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Freight/customs landed-feasibility and execution** | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | **23** |
| **Facilities/field serviceability and fulfilment** | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **22** |
| **Environmental survey/test sufficiency and procurement** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | **20** |

The strongest first test is not necessarily the most intellectually valuable data. **Freight/customs** and **field service** have frequent decisions, perishable capacity, a visible completion event and records created in structured operational systems. Their product can be an executed outcome—goods released or a fault repaired—rather than a contestable prediction. Environmental testing is a promising second family because method, accreditation scope, sample provenance and report status already provide an explicit evidence contract. Engineering and tax may support higher-value decisions, but liability, client ownership and slow outcome feedback make them better as bounded expert calls after the market has learned how to preserve scope and provenance.

The strongest products may traverse company boundaries. “Can this remote mine water system be approved, procured and commissioned before the wet season?” requires hydrology, engineering, permitting, supplier lead-time and contractor-capacity ontologies. “Can this 80-ton module reach site by date?” requires design dimensions, GIS routes, bridge/load constraints, permits, equipment and schedules. No single database answers either question.

This suggests a sharper market thesis:

> AE need not catalogue companies or APIs. It can map buyer questions onto the smallest authorised traversal across one or more company ontologies, then procure the answer or action.

The defensible IP would be the learned mapping between **decision -> required concepts and relationships -> qualified capability owners -> permissible disclosure -> observed outcome**. Microsoft's tool is useful for expressing an individual ontology; AE's unexplored market layer is resolving a real decision across many independently owned ontologies without requiring their raw records to be pooled.

### Strongest Australian wedges to test

1. **Mining decision products without mine-data resale.** Test water-constrained pit viability, credible ramp-up ranges, condition-specific equipment fit and closure-liability envelopes using rights-cleared derived answers from operators and technical firms.
2. **Export compliance as a callable product.** Turn the repeated biosecurity, certification and market-access work of Australian producers, labs, freight forwarders and advisers into one consignment-specific decision for exporters.
3. **A national market from fragmented tradies and equipment.** Make local construction capacity, lead times and site constraints discoverable enough for builders, insurers and governments to form dependable regional supply.
4. **Export the design-to-operation loop.** Combine engineering design precedent, project-delivery actuals and operating evidence into construction-readiness, commissioning, substitution and overrun decisions while preserving the larger advisory relationship.
5. **Verified remote availability.** Combine local health, freight, accommodation, trades and field-service businesses into truthful answers about what can actually be delivered in regional Australia, when and at what constraint.

The likely unit of trade changes by sector. A miner may sell a derived benchmark from millions of observations; a regional contractor a current equipment slot; a specialist five minutes of rare judgement. The first Australian validation should compare those distinct units rather than assume every opportunity is “data.”

## Literature review: ontology, agent-native firms and market formation

There is not yet a settled academic literature called the “agentic economy.” The useful evidence comes from four older literatures—knowledge representation, transaction-cost economics, electronic markets and service economics—plus recent protocol specifications and first-party startup theses. They support a plausible mechanism, but not yet the claim that a large autonomous-services market will form.

### What YC is actually betting on

YC's 2026 Request for Startups separates three ideas that are often conflated. **AI-native service companies** sell completed work rather than a copilot; YC names insurance brokerage, accounting/tax/audit, compliance and healthcare administration because service spend is large and much of the work is already outsourced. **Software for agents** rebuilds tools around machine-readable APIs, MCPs and CLIs rather than human interfaces. **The AI operating system for companies** makes meetings, tickets and customer interactions queryable so a firm can close the loop from decision to observed result. A related 2025 “Company Brain” request describes a living map of how refunds, pricing exceptions and incidents are handled, not merely enterprise search. ([YC Requests for Startups](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs))

YC is therefore making a three-part economic bet:

1. AI can perform enough of a repeatable service workflow to reduce marginal delivery cost and turnaround time.
2. Selling the outcome captures a larger budget than selling a productivity tool to the incumbent labour provider.
3. Operating the service creates proprietary examples, corrections and outcomes that improve the system, so the service firm becomes a data-and-learning loop rather than a thin wrapper around a public model.

The companies YC has backed show the intended shape. Harper operates the insurance brokerage itself and says its system accumulates broker knowledge, workflows, data and capabilities; Flowscope sells implemented process change rather than a consulting deck. These are first-party company claims, not independent evidence that the economics generalise. ([YC: Harper](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/harper); [YC: Flowscope](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/flowscope))

This is different from AE's more speculative proposition. YC mainly expects new firms to replace or rebuild existing service supply. AE is asking whether incumbents' accumulated knowledge and operating capacity can be exposed in smaller units, composed across firms and used to create transactions that were previously uneconomic.

### The ontology's actual role

In knowledge engineering, an ontology is a shared representational vocabulary—classes, relationships, functions and constraints—that lets systems exchange and reuse domain knowledge. RDF and OWL standardised web-based representation and formal reasoning; modern knowledge graphs combine graph data, schemas, identity, query and deductive or inductive inference over large, changing collections. ([Gruber 1993](https://doi.org/10.1006/knac.1993.1008); [W3C RDF/OWL](https://www.w3.org/news/2004/rdf-and-owl-are-w3c-recommendations/); [Hogan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3447772))

Microsoft's Ontology Playground is a useful teaching and design tool: it models entity types, typed properties, relationships and cardinalities; imports and exports RDF/OWL; and demonstrates natural-language questions over an ontology. It does **not** establish that the model is correct, current, rights-cleared, commercially valuable or safe for an agent to act upon. ([Microsoft Ontology Playground](https://github.com/microsoft/Ontology-Playground))

For AE, ontology is therefore best treated as a **capability-discovery and composition grammar**. It can describe which concepts a decision touches and which organisation may know them. The economic object remains the bounded answer or action, with provenance, permission, freshness, liability and evidence attached. A beautifully modelled graph with no buyer decision is infrastructure without a market.

### Four different things called agent-native

| Category | What changes | Unit sold | Source of advantage | AE relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Agent-native software** | The primary user is an agent; discovery and operation are machine-readable | Software access or usage | Better agent interface, reliability and integration | Makes existing capabilities callable |
| **Agent-native service firm** | The provider owns the workflow and delivers the result using agents and humans | Completed service outcome | Lower delivery cost plus a proprietary decision/outcome loop | Creates new routeable suppliers |
| **Agent-accessible incumbent** | An existing business exposes a bounded part of its knowledge or capacity | Answer, reservation, verification or action | Unique operating history, permissions, assets or relationships | Core latent-capacity hypothesis |
| **Market infrastructure** | Discovery, qualification, authority, payment, evidence and recovery work across providers | Coordination or transaction service | Demand graph, liquidity, reputation and outcome data | Possible AE role; not equivalent to the suppliers |

MCP standardises how a model can discover resources and tools with schemas; A2A standardises discovery and communication between independently built agents; x402 enables automatic per-request payment over HTTP. Together they lower interface, integration and payment friction. None determines whether a service is competent, whether the caller has authority, whether the supplier may use its source records, or whether the result created value. ([MCP specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25); [Google A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/); [Coinbase x402](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/welcome))

### The economic mechanism—and its limits

Coase explained firm boundaries through the comparative cost of coordinating through markets versus inside firms. Malone, Yates and Benjamin later argued that information technology can shift activity toward electronic markets by reducing coordination costs. Agent-mediated commerce research in the 1990s already decomposed buying into need identification, product and merchant discovery, negotiation, purchase and post-purchase evaluation. The new ingredient is not the idea of shopping agents; it is the possibility that models can also perform parts of the supplied service and interpret less-standardised offers. ([Coase 1937](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0335.1937.tb00002.x); [Malone, Yates & Benjamin 1987](https://doi.org/10.1145/214762.214766); [Guttman, Moukas & Maes 1998](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0269888998002082))

Service-dominant logic strengthens the intuition that buyers value the application of knowledge and capabilities, not possession of a data artefact. Modularity theory adds the qualification: exchange expands when work can be separated behind stable interfaces without losing essential context. AI may lower the cost of discovering, specifying and integrating a service module, but professional work that is tacit, tightly coupled or liability-heavy may resist decomposition. ([Vargo & Lusch 2004](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.68.1.1.24036); [Baldwin & Clark 2000](https://books.google.com/books?id=oaBOuo4mId8C))

The literature also argues against a frictionless-agent story. Akerlof's adverse-selection result predicts market failure when buyers cannot distinguish high-quality from low-quality supply. Transaction-cost theory predicts continued integration where specificity, uncertainty and opportunism make contracting expensive. Acemoglu and Restrepo show that automation can displace existing tasks without necessarily creating enough new tasks or broadly shared productivity. AE therefore needs proof of better decisions and incremental exchange, not counts of APIs, agents or automated steps. ([Akerlof 1970](https://doi.org/10.2307/1879431); [Acemoglu & Restrepo 2019](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.2.3))

### Convergence, disagreement and the missing study

The sources converge on one mechanism: machine-readable capabilities, shared semantics and cheaper coordination can make smaller units of work tradable. Repeated operation can turn the provider's context and outcomes into an advantage. They disagree—or remain silent—on the consequential questions: how much professional judgement can be modularised; whether AI-native entrants outperform augmented incumbents; whether lower cost creates new demand or merely transfers revenue; who bears liability; and whether provenance and reputation can prevent a low-quality market.

No located literature demonstrates that exposing incumbent company ontologies causes new market formation or new GDP. No standard provides a portable representation of competence, reliance, permitted purpose, current capacity and observed service outcome together. That gap is closer to AE's research problem than “build an ontology marketplace.”

### Testable propositions and falsifiers

1. **Outcome capture:** buyers will pay materially more for completed work than for software assistance with the same workflow. Falsified if service gross margin does not improve after human exception handling and liability costs.
2. **Granularity:** agents make a useful professional determination economical below today's minimum engagement size. Falsified if scoping, verification and sign-off remain effectively fixed-cost.
3. **Incumbent latent capacity:** an incumbent can produce a saleable answer from rights-cleared operating knowledge without exposing client data or weakening its core service. Falsified if permission and contextualisation consume the economic surplus.
4. **Ontology contribution:** modelling entities and relationships improves cross-provider discovery or answer quality over document search and ordinary API metadata. Falsified if it adds maintenance cost without a measurable decision improvement.
5. **Composition:** a buyer values a resolved cross-company outcome more than separately procuring its component services. Falsified if integration errors, accountability gaps or duplicated verification erase the benefit.
6. **Learning loop:** outcome feedback measurably improves provider selection, scoping or performance. Falsified if outcomes are too delayed, ambiguous or legally inaccessible to learn from.
7. **Demand creation:** lower transaction cost generates purchases that would not otherwise occur, rather than only substituting existing labour or SaaS spend. Falsified if nearly all revenue is displaced incumbent expenditure.
8. **Trust formation:** provenance, scoped responsibility and observed outcomes allow high-quality suppliers to earn a premium. Falsified if buyers cannot distinguish quality and the market converges on cheapest plausible output.

**Evidence ceiling:** this review establishes conceptual lineage, current protocol affordances and YC's stated investment thesis. It does not establish provider economics, autonomous service quality, buyer willingness to pay, rights feasibility, market liquidity, or incremental GDP. Those require transaction-level field evidence.

## Market forms worth testing

These are deliberately broader than a catalogue of APIs:

1. **Reverse capability search.** Demand is published first: “Who can reduce uncertainty in this decision?” Companies discover that an internal capability answers a market need they had never named as a product.
2. **Information by-products.** A business sells a bounded conclusion generated while doing its ordinary work—such as congestion, failure-rate or lead-time insight—without selling the underlying customer records.
3. **Capacity swarms.** Many small operators expose tiny windows of spare equipment, storage, labour, access or local fulfilment. An agent assembles them into dependable capacity no participant could sell alone.
4. **Decision dividends.** Multiple companies contribute privacy-safe signals to a decision product and share revenue when the combined answer is bought. The product exists because the contributors are more informative together than separately.
5. **Expertise unbundling.** A specialist firm sells the narrow judgement hidden inside a larger engagement: not “hire the consultancy,” but “is this clause unusual in this jurisdiction?” The full service remains available where the answer reveals more work.
6. **Cross-tier verification.** Enterprise-scale data says what usually happens; local businesses and specialists sell current ground truth about whether it is happening here and now. The market product is the resolved discrepancy.
7. **Demand observatories.** Unfulfilled agent requests reveal missing products, regions or capacity. Suppliers pay attention to that demand map, then create new offers or deploy assets where demand is credible.

The most interesting mechanism may therefore be **demand-led product formation**, not supply-led listing. Instead of asking companies to upload whatever APIs they already have, observe costly unanswered decisions and recruit the company capability that can make each one answerable.

## Rights boundary

Agent-native does not mean “resell the database.” WIPO identifies processes, test data, supplier and customer lists, pricing and business plans as potential trade secrets; licensing them requires explicit scope, purpose, access, use and disclosure controls. Personal-data risk also depends on the release model and what other information a recipient can combine with the output; aggregation or pseudonymisation is not automatically safe.

The safer design space is often a consented or rights-cleared **derived answer, bounded action or aggregate signal** that reveals less than the source material. Some knowledge should remain internal. A product must establish that the supplier has the right to offer it, the people represented are protected, and the output does not undermine the core business or expose another party's confidential information.

## How to explore it

Start with stories, not infrastructure. Find a small group of companies and ask:

- What do you know or control because of the business you already operate?
- Which parts sit idle, expire, or serve only internal users today?
- Who makes an expensive decision or suffers delay without it?
- What is the smallest useful answer or action you could safely provide?
- Would providing it strengthen or threaten the core business?

In parallel, find buyers with recurring objectives and ask where their agents encounter missing information, unavailable capacity, manual negotiation or dead ends.

The first loop is deliberately small:

> one latent capability -> one real unmet objective -> one bounded offer -> one valuable transaction -> evidence that both sides want another

The exploration succeeds when it discovers supply that was not already sold in this form and creates incremental demand, revenue or productive use. It fails if companies merely repackage existing subscriptions, buyers find the output interesting but not decision-changing, suppliers will not expose the capacity, or both sides bypass the market after introduction.

## Historical grounding and claim ceiling

The historical precedents are consistent: ISBN and the web made fragmented things addressable; search made abundance usable; Sabre made live inventory searchable and reservable; app stores made dispersed software distributable; data exchanges and x402 markets make digital supply purchasable. The larger breakthroughs came from recognising dormant supply and creating a new unit of exchange, not from launching a directory alone.

Billion Dollar PDFs reinforces the mechanism: addressability and discovery precede aggregation, while repeat demand, supplier benefit and transaction improvement determine whether a market forms. Agentic Market shows that agent discovery and per-call payment are becoming available infrastructure.

This note does not establish market size, demand, provider willingness, new GDP, or an AE product commitment. "New GDP" is the ambition: the first proof is incremental economic activity that would not otherwise have occurred.
