# AE becomes the next Plaid

## 1. Verdict

**Analogy strength: 7/10** — AE already has the right *shape* for a Plaid-like consent-and-verification intermediary—bounded authority, verified supply, machine APIs, and evidence—but today those parts are split between a strong target architecture and incomplete runtime enforcement, while local-service transactions are less standardized and less frequent than financial-account connections (`PRODUCT.md`; `.planning/design/JOURNEY-SYSTEM.md:153-154,230`; `src/modules/customer-request/preparation-authority.ts`).

## 2. What made Plaid Plaid

1. **One integration normalized a fragmented two-sided network.** Plaid Link, launched in 2015, gave apps one embedded connection flow across institutions; by 2025 Plaid reported 7,000+ companies, 12,000+ institutions, 750,000 daily connections, and use by more than half of Americans with bank accounts ([Plaid, “A decade of Plaid Link”](https://plaid.com/blog/ten-years-plaid-link/)). The moat was not a prettier bank-login screen; it was removing thousands of bilateral integrations.
2. **Consent and verification became products, not compliance residue.** OAuth lets a user authorize data access at the institution without giving credentials to the app, with expiration and revocation as lifecycle concerns ([Plaid OAuth guide](https://plaid.com/docs/link/oauth/)). Plaid then productized identity, account matching, configurable checks, risk signals, and auditable verification sessions ([Plaid Identity Verification](https://plaid.com/docs/identity-verification/)).
3. **B2B2C distribution made the rail mostly invisible.** An app integrates and pays; the consumer briefly encounters Link at the trust/consent boundary; the institution supplies the permissioned data. Plaid prices connectivity and verification through one-time, per-request, subscription, and enterprise plans ([Plaid billing](https://plaid.com/docs/account/billing/)). **Caveat:** this is structural interoperability, not perfect neutrality—apps are Plaid’s paying customers, and institutions control OAuth access. AE should copy the low-friction intermediary position, not claim fiduciary neutrality.

## 3. 10-star worldline — AE-as-next-Plaid in 2031

**INFERRED target state:** AE is the default Australian **agent-to-SMB trust rail**. A consumer asks any assistant for a local service; the assistant calls AE to discover an admitted business binding, presents an AE-hosted or SDK-rendered consent sheet, receives one-use authority, and gets a cryptographically replayable record. AE is visible for seconds at the permission boundary and backstage thereafter—Plaid Link’s distribution pattern applied to consequential agent action (`PRODUCT.md:23-45,61-69`; `.planning/design/PRINCIPLES.md:64-78`).

**INFERRED 2031 scale targets:** 300,000 C6-admitted Australian SMBs; 2,000 agent/app integrations; 100 million verification/admission decisions per year; 99.99% gateway availability; p95 admission under 300 ms; 100% of admitted actions reconstructable from canonical evidence; fewer than 1 false owner/destination admission per 100,000 decisions.

**Product surfaces:** (1) **AE Connect**, the embedded principal consent/readback; (2) **SMB Verify**, ownership, destination, capability, suppression, and readiness verification; (3) **Agent Authority API**, principal/agent/action/data/recipient/purpose/expiry/cap checks; (4) **Evidence API**, immutable receipt, delivery observation, response attribution, revocation, and dispute replay; (5) owner and operator consoles. These are machine and human projections of the same lifecycle, as AE already requires (`PRODUCT.md:61-69,103-107`; `.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:68-112`).

**Who pays whom:** agent platforms, vertical SaaS products, insurers, property platforms, and enterprise assistants pay AE per verified connection/admission plus volume commitments; basic SMB claim and destination verification are free to maximize coverage; consumers pay AE nothing; the customer pays the SMB directly for real work. AE does not custody funds or take payment authority at R1 (`.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:22-26,174-205`). **UNKNOWN:** AE’s actual monetization is not locked; the Plaid-like payer choice must be validated rather than smuggled in as product authority.

## 4. Causal chain — today’s repo → 10-star

1. **Protect the consent-free entry. `[SUPPORTED]`** Shape D gives a useful shortlist before identity, contact data, or speak-for-me authority; calling, copying, or leaving is success (`.planning/design/JOURNEY.md:1-28`; `.planning/design/JOURNEYS-TO-BUILD.md:13-24`). This earns the right to show an embedded consent step later.
2. **Turn C6 into the network’s hard supply credential. `[DESIGN-SUPPORTED; RUNTIME-CONTRADICTED]`** The target predicate requires published page + verified destination + claimed owner + resolvable notification recipient + no suppression + readiness (`.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:238-260`; `.planning/design/JOURNEY-SYSTEM.md:153-154`). But the current claim path proves an authenticated Clerk user, supplied source references, and duplicate posture—not external ownership—and publication itself flips `claimStatus` to `published` (`src/modules/business/internal/claim.ts:1-90,129-193`; `src/modules/catalog/internal/publish.ts:1-130,181-205`). The inquiry target check tests publication, suppression, service, capability, and launch readiness but not explicit `claimStatus` or a resolvable owner destination (`src/modules/inquiries/internal/commands.ts:957-1020`). The canonical doc explicitly records this break (`.planning/design/JOURNEY-SYSTEM.md:153-154,230`).
3. **Productize verification, rather than treating claim as onboarding. `[GAP]`** Add versioned proofs for legal/business identity, owner authority, destination control, capability conformance, freshness, and revocation; expose a portable verification result without claiming AE verified real-world fulfilment (`PRODUCT.md:50-58,93-107`; `.planning/design/PRINCIPLES.md:40-44`). This is AE’s analogue to Plaid Identity/Auth—not a badge marketplace.
4. **Unify “clearance” around the executable authority object. `[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED]`** Current code has a signed `VerifiedPreparationAuthority` bound to principal, delegated agent, request/revision, fields, recipient bindings, purposes, recipient/exposure/operation caps, grant time, expiry, and revocation, with typed refusal reasons and one-use allocation conflict handling (`src/modules/customer-request/preparation-authority.ts:1-125,230-350,470-525`). Approval grants also admit explicit or standing-mandate authority and are use-limited and expiring (`src/modules/customer-request/public.ts:194-215`). **OBSERVED:** there is no current `src/modules/clearance` module; the Plaid-like mandate layer now lives across preparation authority and routing authorization, so AE must converge those rather than market an absent clearance product.
5. **Make the evidence ledger the verification product. `[DESIGN-SUPPORTED; RUNTIME-PARTIAL]`** The contract requires canonical bytes, commit-time rechecks, one-use keys, append-only events, receipt≠outcome, and deterministic replay (`.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:85-112`; `.planning/design/PRINCIPLES.md:64-78`). Current inquiry records do preserve payload/body hashes, operation keys, audit events, notification attempts, and tombstones (`src/modules/inquiries/internal/schema.ts:120-260,360-490`), but commands still mutate thread projections while appending auxiliary arrays (`src/modules/inquiries/internal/commands.ts:370-464,705-752`). That is useful evidence plumbing, not yet the single replayable ledger the contract demands.
6. **Embed the same gate in every agent surface. `[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED]`** AE already defines signed HTTP/MCP operations—route, authorize, execute, inspect, reconcile, cancel—and separates caller identity from authorization (`PRODUCT.md:61-69`; `src/modules/routing-kernel/contract.ts`; `src/modules/routing-kernel/caller-identity.ts:105-147`; `src/modules/routing-kernel/http.ts:134-205`). The next step is one stable “verify → consent → admit → receipt” SDK/API contract, not separate human inquiry and agent-routing trust models.
7. **Create the two-sided flywheel without ranking capture. `[ARCHITECTURE-SUPPORTED; BUSINESS UNKNOWN]`** More verified SMB bindings improve agent coverage; more agent traffic makes claim verification valuable to SMBs; more ledger evidence improves destination health and fraud detection. Neutrality requires published admission rules, commercial-influence disclosure, portable records, symmetric revocation, and no pay-to-pass verification (`PRODUCT.md:50-58,93-107`; `.planning/design/PRINCIPLES.md:64-78`).
8. **Expand authority only after trust density. `[SUPPORTED]`** R1 remains one send to one business; R2–R4 increase cardinality and structure, while R4 still has no ordering or payment authority (`.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:22-26,160-205`). “Every agent must pass AE” must emerge as a safety/interoperability default, not an early monopoly claim.

## 5. Kill conditions

1. **Supply verification does not clear cold start.** Falsifier: in the first launch category, fewer than 40% of legitimate claim starts become fully C6-admitted within 30 days, or admitted supply covers under 60% of high-intent demand after six months. Earliest metric: `C6-ready claims / legitimate claim starts`, broken down by failed conjunct (`.planning/design/JOURNEYS-TO-BUILD.md:42-46,112-121`; `.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:238-260`).
2. **Agents route around the consent layer.** Falsifier: after three production design partners, fewer than 20% of eligible agent-to-SMB actions use AE admission, or AE consent reduces completion by more than 10 percentage points versus the partner’s direct handoff. Earliest metric: eligible intents → consent opened → authority granted → admitted action, with direct-path comparison (`.planning/design/JOURNEY.md:30-55`; `.planning/design/PRINCIPLES.md:64-78`).
3. **Verification cannot bear dispute-grade trust.** Falsifier: more than 1 false owner/destination admission per 100,000 decisions, any successful revoked/expired/replayed authorization, or under 99.9% deterministic reconstruction across the first 10,000 admitted actions. Earliest metric: false-admission rate, typed-refusal escape rate, and replay-completeness rate (`src/modules/customer-request/preparation-authority.ts:230-350,470-525`; `.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:99-112`).

## 6. Star rating today

**3/10 on the Plaid worldline.** **OBSERVED:** AE has unusually strong consent, typed-authority, machine-gateway, and evidence contracts, plus meaningful implementation primitives; **OBSERVED:** C6 is knowingly unenforced end to end and the inquiry record is not yet the canonical append-only ledger; **UNKNOWN:** no verified two-sided adoption or monetization evidence exists in the inspected authority set. **Single highest-leverage move:** ship one production tracer bullet—**C6 verify one SMB → accept one scoped, expiring, one-use agent authority → append one canonical receipt → replay it through one public agent API**—with real owners and two external agent clients. That simultaneously tests supply verification, consent conversion, API embedability, and dispute evidence without prematurely expanding beyond R1 (`.planning/design/JOURNEYS-TO-BUILD.md:34-46,112-121`; `.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md:31-57,238-260`).
