## Verdict in one paragraph

The thesis survives, but only in a much narrower shape than it was conceived. The evidenced opportunity is **not** a horizontal formation layer, a data marketplace, or an agent-payments play. It is a **vertical answer-products business, entered through buyer-paid demand briefs and services-delivered formation**, restricted to legally clean capacity classes (non-personal, supplier-generated, short provenance chains, private holders), selling into decisions where buyers already spend money on data, verification, or manual research. Agents and LLMs collapse the *cost* of formation; they change nothing about title, liability, trust, channel conflict, or willingness to pay. Every step of the sequence has a named falsifier below.

## Where the five records converge (high-confidence findings)

1. **The horizontal marketplace is dead as a starting model.** All five records independently: no marketplace operator (AWS, Snowflake, Databricks, Dawex, Datarade) discloses GMV; Azure DataMarket died; brokerage scored 15/30 (worst) on the model matrix; Data Republic burned AU$40m+ of bank-backed capital on exactly this shape in exactly this country and sold for AU$3m. [Demand §marketplaces; Models §A; GTM §Data Republic]
2. **The bounded-answer economy is real — vertically.** Plaid, LexisNexis C.L.U.E., CARFAX, Persona, Trulioo, Equifax AU, CoreLogic are large durable businesses selling one bounded answer per decision. But every one is vertical, workflow-embedded, and sells via contracts/credits/commitments — not spot micropayments. [Demand §verification economy]
3. **Agent payment rails are announced, not evidenced.** x402 counts are inflatable and concentrated (Chainalysis); Stripe/OpenAI ACP volumes undisclosed; Monid × akta is a live pricing experiment ($0.125/request) with zero disclosed paying demand or retention. Rails-first is a killed wedge. [Demand §rails; GTM §akta caveat]
4. **Verisk kills the easy-contributory story.** Verisk/ISO is the latent-capacity thesis executed pre-AI (insurer claims → $2.7B+ information business) — and it cold-started through rating-bureau inheritance, insurer ownership and regulatory function, not commercial persuasion. Contributory pooling (E) is the best end-state moat and unavailable as an entry. [Models §Verisk]
5. **The intermediary/formation function is supported; the intermediary *company* is conditional.** dunnhumby, Quantium/CommBank iQ, LexisNexis contributory, FlightAware all show a specialist carrying product, governance, normalization, distribution and P&L. But holders with scale and captive buyer channels keep it in-house (84.51°, Walmart). The third party wins where supply is fragmented and pooling/neutrality adds value. [Supply §intermediary]
6. **What AI changes and doesn't.** Changed materially: schema discovery, extraction, sample generation, small-unit billing — formation labour cost. Changed not at all: title, consent, permitted purpose, liability, channel conflict, holder fear of commoditisation. If extraction becomes a commodity, value moves to rights, unique sources, distribution and outcome validation. [Models §agents table]
7. **Ansoff positioning: new product into existing, costly decisions.** Not "new demand from autonomous agents." The first buyer is a budget owner who already paid for data, verification or manual research in the last 12 months. [Demand §Ansoff]

## Where records tension, and the adjudication

- **Sequence: services-led vs demand-aggregator-led.** Models ranks formation-as-a-service the best entry (22/30) toward a vertical product destination (24/30). GTM scores services-led and demand-aggregator-led equal (18/20) and recommends the combination: a **buyer-paid sourcing brief first** (Neudata pattern — buyer pays to have a missing decision input scouted), then services-delivered formation of exactly the supply that answers repeated briefs. Adjudication: adopt the GTM combination; it is the Models "B in service of F" sequence with a demand instrument bolted on the front, and it inverts the classic failure (forming supply nobody asked for).
- **Recourse as differentiator vs recourse as cost.** The product-design intuition (accountability tiers as AE's moat) survives but shrinks: Legal shows ACL s 18 cannot be excluded, disclaimers only qualify conduct, and unknown downstream machine reliance is hard to insure. "Backed" therefore means: named intended use/audience, disclosed methodology and freshness, correction/re-performance/refund remedy, negotiated aggregate cap — a bounded professional service, never guaranteed truth. Strong recourse on low-priced answers can destroy unit economics; price must carry the assurance cost.
- **The resources example.** Partially survives, redrawn by Legal: confidential exploration results under WAMEX protection are blocked absent express holder authority; selective sale of material results by ASX-listed issuers is dead (LR 3.1/Ch 5/JORC); open-file-derived analysis is clean but commoditises. The clean resource-adjacent classes are **private (unlisted) holders' supplier-generated non-personal observations** — operational, equipment, environmental, geotechnical — with short provenance chains.

## The composite model that survives the evidence

**Shape:** vertical answer-products company (Models rank 1), entered via buyer-paid briefs + manual formation (GTM 18/20 sequence), on legally clean classes (Legal matrix), sold to already-spending buyers (Demand personas), with the intermediary functions the Supply record evidences (rights, normalization, product, distribution, P&L).

**Sequence with pre-committed falsifiers:**

| Step | Action | Falsifier / gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Sell ONE paid sourcing brief to a buyer with an existing data/verification/research budget: name the missing decision input, intended use, willingness to pay, renewal trigger | Buyer won't pay anything for the brief → demand-side of thesis unproven in reach; stop before supply work |
| 1 | Manually form ONE supplier answer for that brief: private holder, non-personal data, uninterrupted reuse-rights chain, derived answer (never raw records) | No holder grants a renewable derived-product right after seeing cannibalisation/liability terms → supply-side blocked |
| 2 | Deliver with the bounded-accountability wrapper (use, provenance, freshness, remedy, cap); charge real money | Buyer finds it interesting but not decision-changing; no repeat within 90 days |
| 3 | Repeat the SAME decision three times paid, ≥2 suppliers or ≥2 buyers | Three repetitions produce three bespoke consulting projects, not a template (>70% step reuse is the bar) |
| 4 | Only then: productize the repeated step; list formed products on third-party rails (AWS DX 3%, Databricks 0%) as channels, never as the business | Vertical shows no path to 5 independent paying buyers, 3 renewable source agreements, >50% contribution margin |

**Base-rate honesty carried forward:** no defensible published base rate exists for this category (the "80% Gartner" claim is an IoT-specific 2017 forecast, not a measured failure rate — do not cite it as one). The observable pattern is dispersion: decades-old vertical, coordinated, or capital-heavy winners; opaque or dead horizontal exchanges. Year-one success is single-digit paid repetitions with repeat intent, and that must be written down before starting.

## What this kills (rollup)

- Horizontal marketplace / catalogue / passive exchange as the wedge.
- Rails-first (x402/ACP) strategy; rails are commodity channels.
- Contributory database as entry (end-state only, needs a coordination mechanism AE lacks).
- Generic resale of personal, sensitive, listed-material, or WAMEX-confidential classes.
- Pure revenue-share compensation for formation work.
- "Companies already have sale-ready data" — they have costly inputs plus unaudited rights chains.
- Marketplace-catalogue-size, x402-count, and vendor-estimate spend figures as demand evidence.

## What remains genuinely open (fieldwork only)

Consolidated from all five records, deduplicated to the load-bearing six:

1. Will an Australian buyer outside investment management pay separately for a sourcing/formation brief?
2. Will a holder sign a narrow renewable right to derive-and-resell an answer, rather than a bespoke analysis?
3. What percentage of candidate datasets survive the rights-chain audit? (Assume attrition; measure it.)
4. Is PI/cyber cover obtainable, and at what cap/premium, for per-answer delivery with downstream machine reliance?
5. Do three repetitions template (>70% reuse) or stay bespoke?
6. Do agents discover incremental demand, or only execute purchases humans already budgeted?

## Relationship to AE's current product

This wedge and the current AE spine are complementary, not substitutes: mandates, receipts, evidence classes and bounded authority are exactly the assurance infrastructure a backed-answer product requires (and the Legal record shows assurance is contractual/procedural, which is what the spine encodes). But the research gives no license to build platform machinery ahead of the Step 0–3 loop; per AE doctrine, horizontal scaffolding waits for a real operation and its acceptance eval.

## Claim ceiling

This synthesis recommends an exploration sequence; it does not select a vertical, name a buyer, commit engineering, or establish that any transaction will occur. Nothing here is legal advice. All five underlying records carry OBSERVED/INFERRED/UNKNOWN labels; this synthesis inherits their weakest link wherever it aggregates.
