# Transaction-cost derivation: what an agentic economy structurally is

**Date:** 2026-07-13 · **Status key:** **OBSERVED** = directly supported by cited source/repo; **INFERRED** = deduction from those observations; **UNKNOWN** = unresolved empirical proposition.

## 1. The economic object

**INFERRED — definition.** An agentic economy is not “a marketplace used by bots.” It is an economy in which principals can continuously expose bounded intent and capacity, and delegated machines can discover counterparties, compose contingent agreements, obtain authority, execute once, and produce disputable evidence at marginal coordination cost below the value of very small transactions. Its new production unit is therefore the **governed, machine-cleared action**, not the listing, lead, chat, or payment.

**OBSERVED — Coase.** Firms replace repeated market contracts when discovering prices, negotiating, and contracting through the price mechanism costs more than direction inside a firm; the boundary lies where the cost of organizing one more transaction internally equals market exchange or another organizer’s cost ([Coase 1937, pp. 390–397](https://faculty.washington.edu/mfan/is582/articles/Coase1937.pdf)). Coase explicitly warns that communications technology may enlarge or shrink firms depending on whether it reduces internal coordination cost or market-use cost more.

**OBSERVED — Williamson.** Governance must align transaction attributes with market, hybrid, or hierarchy. Generic assets and autonomously adaptable work favour markets; asset specificity, incomplete contracts, consequential disturbances, and opportunism favour longer commitments or hierarchy ([Williamson 2009, §§3.1–3.2](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/williamson_lecture.pdf)). Therefore “agents make firms disappear” is not a TCE prediction.

**OBSERVED — search/matching.** In Diamond–Mortensen–Pissarides models, buyers/jobs and sellers/workers do not meet costlessly; a matching function converts searching participants into matches, and congestion, bargaining, entry and matching efficiency jointly determine outcomes ([Nobel Committee 2010, advanced information](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2010.pdf)). Lower search cost increases feasible search and potential market thickness, but does not guarantee efficient matching when participation or information is poor.

**OBSERVED — microstructure.** Liquidity is produced, not free. When counterparties are asymmetrically informed, intermediaries protect themselves through spreads; order flow reveals information and large/faster trading can move prices ([Glosten–Milgrom 1985](https://www.edegan.com/pdfs/Glosten%20Milgrom%20%281985%29%20-%20Bid%20Ask%20and%20Transaction%20Prices%20in%20a%20Specialist%20Market%20with%20Heterogeneously%20Informed%20Trades.pdf); [Kyle 1985](https://www.edegan.com/pdfs/Kyle%20%281985%29%20-%20Continuous%20Auctions%20and%20Insider%20Trader.pdf)). Machine speed can thicken markets while also accelerating adverse selection, congestion and strategic gaming.

### If search, bargaining and enforcement approach zero

1. **Search → 0:** **INFERRED** the relevant market expands from “providers a human can inspect” to all machine-readable, admitted capacity; thin geographic/time slices thicken; latent intent becomes order flow. But ranking, verification and attention become the scarce resources.
2. **Bargaining → 0:** **INFERRED** bespoke terms become viable for low-value exchanges; continuous re-quotation and combinatorial matching replace posted-price-only trade. Preference elicitation and mandate limits—not message generation—become the bottleneck.
3. **Enforcement/verification → 0:** **INFERRED** more work crosses firm boundaries, including smaller and more contingent tasks. Yet physical outcome uncertainty, asset specificity, liability and non-contractible quality do not collapse; firms/hybrids persist where coordinated adaptation still beats market contracting.
4. **All three → 0:** **INFERRED** minimum viable transaction value falls from `coordination cost + production cost` toward production cost. Specialization can become finer, firm boundaries more porous, and temporary multi-firm “virtual production units” form per objective. Viable transaction count rises far faster than average transaction value.
5. **General equilibrium:** **UNKNOWN.** Cheap agents may create a “Coasean singularity,” but current economics explicitly predicts countervailing congestion, obfuscation and platform enclosure, not frictionless welfare ([Shahidi et al., *The Coasean Singularity?*, 2025/26](https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c15309/revisions/c15309.rev3.pdf)).

**OBSERVED frontier evidence.** The recent agent literature reaches the same institutional conclusion: agents reduce search, screening, negotiation and monitoring costs and expand the feasible market-design set ([Shahidi et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c15309/revisions/c15309.rev3.pdf)); DeepMind defines linked agent markets and stresses verifiable identity, reputation, oversight and high-frequency-negotiation risk ([Tomašev et al., *Virtual Agent Economies*, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10147v1)); a 2026 live-agent simulation found trade productive but institutionally fragile, with some transparency/selection interventions worsening performance ([Liu, Shang & Jin, *When Agent Markets Arrive*](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06688v2)). These are early models/simulations, not established macroeconomic facts.

**OBSERVED capability reality.** Anthropic’s November-2025 sample found large estimated speedups but imperfect completion (about 66% on college-level tasks); reliability adjustment cut its projected productivity-growth contribution from 1.8 to 1.0–1.2 percentage points ([Anthropic Economic Index, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/economic-index-primitives)). Cheap cognition is not zero-cost enforcement.

## 3. Why Australia is a structurally strong test bed

**OBSERVED.** At 30 June 2025 Australia had 2,729,648 actively trading businesses, but only 994,178 employers: **1,735,470 non-employing businesses (63.6%)**. It had 822,873 sole proprietors (**30.1% of all businesses**); the household institutional sector held 49.3%. Relevant large counts include transport/postal/warehousing 249,289 and health/social assistance 213,177; construction grew 2.2% that year ([ABS, Counts of Australian Businesses 2021–25](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/latest-release)). “Sole traders are 30.1%” and “non-employing are 63.6%” are different measures and must not be conflated.

**INFERRED.** Coordination-cost tax is highest where supply is fragmented, capacity perishes, work is local/physical, scope is uncertain, and jobs require sequencing: construction/trades; transport and field logistics; property/rental services; home and community health/social care; repair/maintenance and other personal services; agriculture’s seasonal contractors; hospitality events. The claim is structural, not an ABS-measured ranking. **UNKNOWN:** Australian category-level time spent on quoting, scheduling, no-shows, compliance and multi-trade sequencing; AE should measure it before ranking sectors publicly.

## 4. Where value concentrates after coordination collapses

**Steelman — the aggregator wins.** Lower search cost creates more candidates, not less need for choice. Matching platforms internalize cross-side network effects, set participation rules and may subsidize one side while charging another ([Rochet & Tirole 2003/2006 overview](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jep.23.3.125)). App stores and card networks show that reducing interaction friction can concentrate rents at the gatekeeper controlling distribution, identity, defaults and rules. An assistant with principal demand can route internally and commoditize an independent AE.

**Attack.** As discovery and negotiation commoditize, multi-homing agents can query many aggregators. Paid ranking contaminates neutrality; synthetic demand creates congestion; exclusive reputation creates lock-in. Aggregation is defensible only while it owns scarce demand or uniquely liquid supply—neither follows automatically from protocol quality.

**Steelman — the evidence/governance layer wins.** Williamson predicts durable value where credible commitments permit exchange that otherwise fails. Card networks did not merely find merchants; they standardized authorization, records, chargebacks and liability. Containerization’s gains depended on complementary ports, cranes, schedules, standards and inland logistics, not the box alone ([Bernhofen, El-Sahli & Kneller, containerization and trade](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19697/w19697.pdf)). The web commoditized publishing/transport while value concentrated in search, identity, payments and trust chokepoints. Machine-speed exchange increases the value of exact authority, replay and allocable failure because human review cannot scale.

**Attack.** Evidence is a moat only when relied upon: counterparties accept it, disputes use it, insurers/regulators recognize it, and switching loses accumulated trust. Otherwise it is duplicable plumbing or an internal log. AE’s own ceiling correctly sets a kill condition if records are not reopened/exported/cited or do not resolve exceptions ([KERNEL-CEILING.md §6](.planning/design/KERNEL-CEILING.md#L70-L74)).

**Steelman — capacity holders win.** Scarce physical execution, licenses, local presence and trusted relationships remain non-replicable; lower coordination can transfer surplus to differentiated sole traders by filling idle capacity and reducing selling overhead.

**Attack.** Transparent comparison can commoditize undifferentiated providers and let demand aggregators extract surplus. Capacity holders win only where scarcity, quality evidence, switching cost or asset specificity exceeds the platform’s control of demand.

**INFERRED verdict on concentration.** Value divides among three bottlenecks: **demand control** captures matching rent; **governance/evidence** captures risk-reduction rent; **scarce capacity** captures production rent. The protocol itself trends toward commodity. AE’s best defensible position is the evidence-bearing admission and response-history layer, but only if it remains portable and relied upon; otherwise the dominant assistant/marketplace captures the economics.

## 5. Verdict for AE

**Recommended structural claim:** **“AE is building the governed clearing layer through which delegated intent can become a bounded, admitted, duplicate-safe and evidentiary business action across independent counterparties.”** Economically: AE lowers the minimum viable size and raises the feasible complexity of market coordination without pretending that identity proves authority, dispatch proves outcome, or software eliminates physical-world risk.

**Repo fit — OBSERVED.** This matches PRODUCT’s route → exact quote → bounded approval → execute-once → inspect lifecycle ([PRODUCT.md §§Product architecture/Core lifecycle](PRODUCT.md#L21-L46)) and the kernel ceiling’s “clearing envelope for delegated business actions,” explicitly not settlement, payments, assistant or checkout ([KERNEL-CEILING.md §1](.planning/design/KERNEL-CEILING.md#L10-L23)).

**Wedge relationship — INFERRED from repo authority.** Australian local-service R1 is neither the total market nor a disguised lead marketplace. It is the smallest consequential transaction that exercises the future structure: one admitted business, one exact brief, one principal-bound permission, one duplicate-safe send, one durable record, one attributable response. The wedge is the **empirical proof schedule** for whether governance lowers coordination cost and creates relied-upon evidence; R2–R4 add cardinality and contract richness only after supply and unhappy-path proof exist ([WEDGE-LADDER.md §§1–4, 7](.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md#L6-L12)).

**Falsifier.** If admitted supply does not become meaningfully more responsive, users do not reuse/cite records, multi-party work does not clear at lower all-in coordination cost, or dominant assistants refuse portable AE evidence, then AE is a useful local-services workflow—not an economy-level clearing institution. That outcome should be reported, not renamed.
