# Customer confirmation handoff: first-party product patterns

**Question.** What interaction rules can AE take from Airbnb, Uber, Stripe, and Meta when projecting a complex route as a simple customer decision for both humans and external AI?

**Source standard.** First-party product, help, API, and engineering documentation only. Reviewed 14 July 2026. The findings constrain the confirmation handoff; they do not claim that AE has the supply, fulfilment, payment, or dispute powers of these products.

## Executive finding

The comparable companies do not make infrastructure the interaction model. They give the customer one durable object—a reservation, ride request, payment intent, or privacy choice—and expose only the facts and state transitions needed to decide what to do next. Matching, payment orchestration, graph traversal, data lineage, and policy enforcement remain server-owned.

For AE, the public object is the **Request**. A RoutePlan is presented as an option within that Request. Confirmation must bind one exact current option and return a durable confirmation receipt; it must not ask the caller to construct a plan, mandate, digest, graph, or transport command.

## Bounded findings

| Comparable | First-party pattern | Exact implication for AE |
|---|---|---|
| Airbnb | A guest agrees to the displayed total price and reservation terms at booking. The applicable cancellation policy is visible on the listing, at confirmation and payment, and in the confirmation email. Before a trip-change request is submitted, Airbnb shows the original and new total; if cancellation is started, it shows the refund breakdown before final confirmation. ([Terms of Service](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2908), [Cancellation-policy visibility](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4052), [Changing reservation dates](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/913), [Trip changes and cancellations](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1276)) | The confirmation view must restate the selected businesses, customer outcome, maximum cost, expiry, important assumptions, cancellation posture, recovery path, and what will happen next. A material change creates a readable before/after delta and requires a fresh confirmation; it is never silently accepted under the old decision. |
| Uber | The rider enters a destination, compares available vehicle options, selects one, confirms pickup, and only then sends the request to eligible drivers. While matching, the customer sees an estimated wait; after acceptance, the surface changes to driver, location and ETA. The API likewise creates one request from a selected product and fare identifier, returns `202` with a request identifier and `processing` status, and reports expired fares as a conflict requiring a refreshed estimate and confirmation. ([Request a ride](https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/how-to-request-a-ride-?nodeId=67f41961-e0aa-4670-af32-58be02c7c492), [Ride Request API](https://developer.uber.com/docs/riders/references/api/v1.2/requests-post)) | Keep **choose**, **confirm**, **in progress**, and **result** separate. The same selected route reference shown to the person must be relayable by an agent. Confirmation returns a stable receipt immediately, even when later work is asynchronous. An expired or changed option returns the customer to refreshed choices with the Request intact. |
| Uber | A cancellation can have a fee after a driver accepts; the fee appears on the trip receipt. An authorization hold is distinct from the final charge and is canceled when the trip is canceled, subject to timing and cancellation policy. ([Cancellation-fee review](https://help.uber.com/riders/article/review-my-cancellation-fee?nodeId=0c852f97-1ba2-4dce-b899-ff02668aa125), [Authorization holds](https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/%E9%A2%84%E5%85%88%E6%8E%88%E6%AC%BE%E9%A1%B9%E5%B8%B8%E8%A7%81%E9%97%AE%E9%A2%98?nodeId=32bfc371-467f-4f66-9416-a03ac86f7a47)) | “Can stop” is not enough. Before confirmation, AE must say whether cancellation is available, until when, and what effects may already be irreversible. Later receipts must distinguish authority reserved, work released, and final effect rather than flattening them into “confirmed” or “complete.” |
| Stripe | A PaymentIntent is one durable payment objective with explicit states including `requires_confirmation`, `requires_action`, `processing`, `succeeded`, and `canceled`. Confirmation initiates the attempt; success, further customer action, processing, and failure are different outcomes. ([PaymentIntent lifecycle](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/paymentintents/lifecycle), [Confirm a PaymentIntent](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payment_intents/confirm)) | Route confirmation creates bounded authority; it does not claim execution or success. AE needs customer-semantic equivalents of action needed, in progress, succeeded, stopped, definitely failed, and outcome unknown, all resumable from the Request. |
| Stripe | Idempotency allows safe retry: the first begun request's response is retained, exact retries return it, and reuse of a key with different parameters is rejected. Stripe also warns that a success-page redirect is not reliable fulfilment evidence because the browser may disappear; server-side events are authoritative and retried. ([Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests), [Checkout success pages](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/custom-success-page), [Fulfil orders](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment)) | Human and machine confirmation must call one authenticated server command with one idempotency contract. Browser state cannot own authority or completion. Replay returns the same confirmation receipt; changed revision or route under the same key is rejected. Resume reads durable server state after interruption. |
| Meta | Accounts Center presents one place to manage connected experiences, contact information, ad preferences and payments across multiple Meta accounts. Privacy Center lets people review and manage information and activity received from businesses. Meta's underlying Privacy Aware Infrastructure uses data lineage and policy-enforcement systems to propagate and enforce purpose limitations across complex data flows. ([Accounts Center](https://www.facebook.com/help/943858526073065), [Privacy Center](https://www.facebook.com/privacy/center/), [Purpose limitation at scale](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/08/27/security/privacy-aware-infrastructure-purpose-limitation-meta/)) | The customer sees named recipients, purposes, data categories, consequences and controls—not AE's graph or enforcement choreography. The kernel must enforce those disclosed limits across every downstream step. One confirmation surface can span multiple businesses only if each recipient and purpose remains inspectable. |
| Meta | Disconnecting activity explains the customer-visible consequence before the action: history is disconnected, some apps may log the person out, ads remain but may be less personalised, and future activity is a separate control. ([Disconnect past activity](https://www.facebook.com/help/287199741901674)) | Cancellation and recovery copy must state the precise reachable effect, residual effects, and next control. Do not promise generic rollback when an already released provider action or disclosure cannot be reversed. |

## Ticket constraints

1. **One public confirmation command.** Human and external-agent surfaces submit the same semantic fields: current Request reference/revision, displayed route reference, and idempotency key. The server derives generation, internal route identity, budgets, expiry, evidence requirements, recipients, and purposes.
2. **One shared preview projection.** Both surfaces receive the same customer facts: outcome, participating businesses, maximum cost, expiry, material assumptions, recipients and purposes, effects, evidence basis, uncertainty, cancellation posture, fallback, and recovery. Layout may differ; meaning may not.
3. **Selection is not authority; authority is not execution.** Comparing or selecting an option has no effect. Confirmation creates a durable bounded-authority receipt. Later progress and result states come from server-owned orchestration and evidence.
4. **Current-head confirmation only.** A stale revision, expired option, changed price, changed recipient/purpose, changed effect, or changed fallback cannot be confirmed. Return a readable delta and refreshed choice; require a new explicit decision.
5. **Exact replay, no duplicate authority.** The same key and same command return the original receipt. The same key with changed parameters fails. Refreshing, reconnecting, or switching from human UI to agent API resumes the same Request and receipt.
6. **Customer-semantic states, precise machine codes.** Human surfaces say what is happening and what the person can do next. Machine payloads carry stable typed states. Neither surface promotes `processing` to success or treats a missing callback as failure.
7. **Show data like money.** Before confirmation, name every business receiving customer data, the purpose, and the bounded categories. Enforce those facts in the kernel. Technical graph and policy machinery belongs only in progressive technical inspection, never in the primary decision.
8. **Recovery preserves the decision context.** Expiry, refusal, unavailable supply, interruption, or uncertain downstream outcome returns to the same Request with the selected option, changed facts, preserved receipt, and the next safe choices: refresh, retry when safe, choose another option, cancel what remains cancellable, or stop.

## Confirmation handoff in customer language

```text
Request
→ options that could satisfy it
→ choose one
→ review cost, businesses, data, effects, timing and ways out
→ confirm this exact option
→ confirmation receipt
→ progress / action needed / result / recovery
```

The engine may compile a graph, admit a mandate, attenuate step authority, select transports and reconcile evidence. The customer and external AI should not need to know those objects exist to make a valid decision or resume it safely.
