# WA field-study consent, privacy, recording, and outreach protocol

**Owner:** Product
**Status:** Active
**Maturity:** External field
**Question:** What current official Australian and Western Australian requirements and best-practice rules govern consent, privacy, recording, retention, de-identification, and approaching businesses for AE's low-risk customer/provider field study?
**Decision affected:** None
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-07-18
**Review by:** 2026-08-18
**Supersedes:** None
**Superseded by:** None

> This record is a primary-source research synthesis and practical risk-control
> proposal. It is not legal advice. AE should obtain qualified Australian/WA
> legal advice before relying on an exemption, recording without every
> participant's express consent, recruiting a vulnerable cohort, collecting
> sensitive information, or commencing research whose legal or institutional
> status is uncertain.

## Executive finding

The low-risk field study can be designed conservatively without collecting
sensitive information or covertly recording anyone. The practical safe default
is to assess AE's Privacy Act coverage before recruitment, provide a short
participant information notice, obtain voluntary and specific consent, obtain
separate express consent from every participant before audio/video recording,
collect only what the study needs, restrict use and access, de-identify outputs
with a re-identification risk check, publish no attributable record without
separate permission, and destroy identifiable material on a declared schedule.

This is a protocol recommendation, not a conclusion that AE is legally an
Australian Privacy Principles (APP) entity, that the study has ethics approval,
or that any proposed outreach message is outside spam or telemarketing law.

## Scope and source hierarchy

This record addresses interviews, workflow observation, case-artifact review,
and recruitment of adults and independently operated businesses in Western
Australia. It does not address children, health research, sensitive-information
research, covert observation, employee monitoring, clinical research, Aboriginal
and Torres Strait Islander research, overseas data transfer, or access to
government-held datasets.

The sources below are official legislation, regulator guidance, or national
human-research guidance. Where a rule may not legally bind AE, this record labels
its use as a conservative protocol choice rather than a legal conclusion.

## Observations

### Privacy Act coverage is a threshold question

- **OBSERVED:** Most businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are
  not covered by the `Privacy Act 1988`, but specified exceptions apply
  regardless of turnover, including health service providers, businesses that
  trade in personal information, Commonwealth contracted service providers,
  residential-tenancy database operators, credit reporting bodies, certain
  AML/CTF reporting entities, related covered businesses, prescribed
  businesses, and businesses that opt in. [OAIC, Small business](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business)
- **OBSERVED:** If an organisation is an APP entity, APP 3 limits solicited
  collection of personal information to information reasonably necessary for
  its functions or activities. Sensitive information generally requires
  consent, and the OAIC identifies four elements of consent: adequately
  informed, voluntary, current and specific, and given by a person with
  capacity. [OAIC, APP Guidelines chapter 3](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-3-app-3-collection-of-solicited-personal-information)
- **OBSERVED:** An APP entity must take reasonable steps at or before collection
  (or as soon as practicable afterward) to notify the individual of applicable
  APP 5 matters. Those matters include the entity's identity and contact
  details, collection circumstances, purpose, consequences of non-collection,
  usual disclosures, the privacy policy and access/complaint information, and
  likely overseas disclosure. [OAIC, text of the APPs, APP 5](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/read-the-australian-privacy-principles)
- **OBSERVED:** APP 6 generally prevents an APP entity from using or disclosing
  personal information for a secondary purpose unless the individual consents
  or a specified exception applies. [OAIC, text of the APPs, APP 6](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/read-the-australian-privacy-principles)
- **OBSERVED:** APP 11 requires reasonable protection from misuse, interference,
  loss, unauthorised access, modification, and disclosure. When personal
  information is no longer needed for a permitted purpose, an organisation must
  take reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify it unless an exception
  applies. OAIC guidance expects technical and organisational controls and, for
  third-party storage, reasonable verification of destruction. [OAIC, APP Guidelines chapter 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information)
- **OBSERVED:** Removing names alone may not de-identify a record. OAIC guidance
  calls for removing or altering both direct identifiers and combinations of
  characteristics that may identify a person, followed by active assessment and
  management of re-identification risk. [OAIC, APP Guidelines chapter B, paragraphs B.62-B.65](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts)

### WA law makes consensual recording the safe default

- **OBSERVED:** Section 5 of the `Surveillance Devices Act 1998 (WA)` generally
  prohibits using a listening device to record a private conversation even when
  the recorder is a party. The relevant ordinary exception applies where each
  principal party expressly or impliedly consents; another exception concerns
  protection of a consenting principal party's lawful interests. [WA Legislation, Surveillance Devices Act 1998, s 5](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_27928.htm/%24FILE/Surveillance%20Devices%20Act%201998%20-%20%5B02-f0-03%5D.html)
- **OBSERVED:** Section 6 establishes a corresponding restriction for visual
  recording of a private activity and an ordinary exception where each
  principal party consents. [WA Legislation, Surveillance Devices Act 1998, s 6](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_27928.htm/%24FILE/Surveillance%20Devices%20Act%201998%20-%20%5B02-f0-03%5D.html)
- **OBSERVED:** Section 9 separately restricts knowingly publishing or
  communicating a private conversation, its report or record, or a recorded
  private activity. One exception is express or implied consent of each
  principal party, alongside other limited exceptions. [WA Legislation, Surveillance Devices Act 1998, s 9](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_27928.htm/%24FILE/Surveillance%20Devices%20Act%201998%20-%20%5B02-f0-03%5D.html)

### Human-research guidance supplies the ethical floor

- **OBSERVED:** The 2025 `National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human
Research` is intended for researchers conducting human research and became
  effective on 23 June 2026. It says consent must be voluntary and based on
  sufficient information and adequate understanding. [NHMRC, National Statement landing page](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/ethics/national-statement-ethical-conduct-human-research) and [2025 National Statement, chapter 2.2](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)
- **OBSERVED:** The consent information described by chapter 2.2 includes the
  research purpose and methods, demands and risks, voluntary participation,
  privacy/confidentiality protections, withdrawal rights and implications,
  funding and interests, payments, complaints/researcher contacts, and likely
  dissemination. [NHMRC, 2025 National Statement, chapter 2.2](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)
- **OBSERVED:** The National Statement requires institutional processes to
  determine the appropriate level of ethics review based on risk; some
  sufficiently lower-risk work may not require HREC approval but may still
  require another review process. [NHMRC, 2025 National Statement, purpose, scope and limits](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)
- **OBSERVED:** NHMRC compliance is a prerequisite for NHMRC funding. The
  National Statement does not, by its publication alone, establish whether this
  independent commercial field study must obtain institutional ethics review.
  [NHMRC, National Statement landing page](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/ethics/national-statement-ethical-conduct-human-research)

### WA public-sector privacy rules are not a general private-sector regime

- **OBSERVED:** From 1 July 2026, the `Privacy and Responsible Information
Sharing Act 2024 (WA)` privacy obligations apply to WA public-sector IPP
  entities, including government agencies, local governments, government
  trading enterprises, public universities, and some contracted service
  providers. The serious-breach notification scheme begins on 1 January 2027.
  [WA Government, Privacy and Responsible Information Sharing](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/privacy-and-responsible-information-sharing)
- **OBSERVED:** This does not describe an across-the-board privacy regime for
  ordinary private businesses. A government contract can, however, bring a
  contracted service provider within specified obligations. [WA Office of the Information Commissioner, IPP entities](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/office-of-the-information-commissioner/what-ipp-entity-and-who-does-the-pris-act-apply)

### Outreach channel and purpose change the applicable rules

- **OBSERVED:** ACMA distinguishes research/survey calls from telemarketing, but
  requires both research callers and telemarketers to follow calling-time and
  identity rules: identify the caller/employer/client, state the purpose, show a
  return caller-ID number, and end the call if asked. ACMA lists research-call
  hours as 9:00 am-8:30 pm weekdays and 9:00 am-5:00 pm weekends, excluding
  national public holidays. [ACMA, Dealing with telemarketing](https://www.acma.gov.au/say-no-to-telemarketers)
- **OBSERVED:** Business phone numbers cannot ordinarily be added to the Do Not
  Call Register, although mixed personal/business numbers may qualify where
  personal use exceeds 50%. [ACMA, Do Not Call Register](https://www.acma.gov.au/do-not-call-register)
- **OBSERVED:** An electronic message is commercial under the `Spam Act 2003`
  where one purpose is to offer, advertise, or promote goods, services, a
  supplier, or a business opportunity. [Federal Register of Legislation, Spam Act 2003, s 6](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A01214/latest)
- **OBSERVED:** ACMA says marketing email/SMS requires consent, sender identity
  and contact details, and a functional unsubscribe facility; a message cannot
  be sent merely to ask for that consent. [ACMA, Avoid sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/Industry/Marketers/Anti-Spam/Ensuring-you-dont-spam/key-elements-of-the-spam-act-ensuring-you-dont-spam-i-acma)

## Inferences

- **INFERRED:** AE should comply with an APP-level protocol even if a documented
  coverage assessment concludes that the small-business exemption applies.
  This reduces participant risk and prevents the study design from depending on
  a potentially changeable exemption. It must not be represented as proof that
  AE is or is not an APP entity.
- **INFERRED:** Express consent from every person is the defensible field-study
  rule for audio/video recording. The study has no need to test the boundaries
  of implied consent, lawful-interests exceptions, or public-interest
  exceptions.
- **INFERRED:** Consent to participate, consent to record, consent to retain
  attributable material, and consent to quote or share with a business are
  different permissions. They should be separately recorded because recording
  and later communication engage distinct WA provisions and participant
  expectations.
- **INFERRED:** Recruitment should be factual and study-specific. If an email or
  message also promotes AE, supplier participation, or future commercial
  opportunity, treat it as a commercial electronic message and do not send it
  without a documented lawful consent basis and compliant unsubscribe
  mechanism. A published business email address alone should not be treated as
  blanket consent.
- **INFERRED:** Results should be aggregated by default. Describing a rare role,
  locality, unusual incident, quoted phrase, or distinctive business process
  can re-identify a participant even after names are removed.

## Practical protocol requirements

These are conservative entry controls for the Wayfinder #182 workbook and field
work. They are not retrospective evidence that any participant has consented.

### Before recruitment

1. Name the legal entity/data controller, accountable field owner, privacy
   contact, study purpose, participant populations, data systems, processors,
   and any overseas storage.
2. Complete and retain a Privacy Act coverage assessment, including turnover
   and every OAIC small-business exception. Repeat it if AE's entity,
   contracts, information practices, or turnover changes.
3. Exclude minors, sensitive information, covert observation/recording, health
   or tenancy records, and government-held personal data from this protocol.
   Stop for legal and ethics review if any enter scope.
4. Determine and record whether an institutional or other ethics-review process
   applies. Do not describe internal product approval as HREC or institutional
   ethics approval.
5. Use a factual recruitment script that names the researcher and AE, explains
   why the person/business was approached, states time commitment and any
   payment, says participation is voluntary, and provides a no-further-contact
   path.
6. For calls, apply ACMA's research-caller identity, purpose, caller-ID,
   calling-time, and immediate-stop rules. For email/SMS/instant messages,
   obtain legal review of whether the message is commercial; if it is or may
   be, require documented Spam Act consent and compliant sender/unsubscribe
   elements.
7. Keep a suppression list containing only the minimum information necessary
   to honour no-further-contact requests. Do not re-approach suppressed people
   through another channel or colleague.

### Consent and session opening

8. Give each participant a plain-language notice before collection covering:
   controller/contact; purpose and methods; requested data; voluntary nature;
   risks and expected benefit; payment; use, access and disclosures; recording;
   retention/de-identification; withdrawal and data-removal limits; complaints;
   and intended dissemination.
9. Record affirmative, current, specific participation consent. Silence,
   attendance, failure to opt out, or a manager's permission is not treated as
   an individual's consent.
10. Obtain a separate affirmative answer from every person before starting any
    audio, video, screen, or AI-generated transcript recording. If anyone says
    no or later withdraws recording permission, use contemporaneous
    non-attributable notes only and stop/delete the recording according to the
    declared procedure.
11. At the start of each recorded session, restate who is present, the recording
    types and processors, and the permitted uses. Do not allow an unconsented
    observer, meeting bot, or automated transcription service to join.
12. Obtain additional, specific permission before sharing an attributable
    quote, recording, transcript, screenshot, case artifact, or identifiable
    account with a participating business, another participant, the public, or
    a model/service not named in the notice.

### Collection, storage, and analysis

13. Collect the least detail that can answer the preregistered measures. Do not
    copy full customer files when timings, categories, and redacted evidence
    will suffice. Participants must redact unrelated third-party information
    before transfer; the researcher must not assume a participant can consent
    for those third parties.
14. Assign random participant/case IDs. Store the identity key separately from
    recordings, transcripts, notes, and analysis. Restrict raw-data access to
    named field staff with a need to know; enable access logs and encryption in
    transit and at rest.
15. Before a quote, vignette, or row leaves the restricted workspace, remove
    direct identifiers and assess combinations that could enable
    re-identification. Generalise or suppress rare dates, places, roles,
    incidents, amounts, and distinctive wording. Record the assessment.
16. Do not use field data for product training, model training, sales, public
    claims, provider evaluation, or automated decisions unless that use was
    specifically disclosed and consented to, or a qualified adviser confirms
    another lawful basis.
17. Record incidents and suspend affected processing immediately. Determine
    legal notification duties from AE's actual entity/contract status and the
    laws in force; do not assume the WA public-sector breach regime governs AE.

### Retention and withdrawal

18. Put dates, not “as needed,” in the workbook. Proposed conservative schedule:
    delete raw audio/video and AI transcripts within 30 days after a participant
    validates the study notes, or 60 days after the session if validation is not
    obtained; delete the re-identification key within 90 days after the cohort
    decision; retain de-identified measures and decision evidence for 12 months
    after the decision, then reassess and destroy or justify a new period.
    These periods are protocol choices, not statutory retention periods found
    in the cited sources.
19. State the last point at which already-collected data can practicably be
    removed. Honour withdrawal from future participation immediately. Before
    irreversible aggregation/de-identification, remove the participant's
    identifiable source data when requested unless a documented legal
    obligation requires retention.
20. Destruction must cover primary storage, exports, local copies, meeting
    platforms, transcription tools, backups when practicable, and service
    providers. Record what was destroyed, when, by whom, and how third-party
    deletion was verified.

## Decision impact

Do not treat Wayfinder #182 field evidence as admissible for task selection until
the workbook passes the acceptance checklist. The research requires a protocol
and workbook update but does not itself choose the task, cohort, provider, or
implementation slice. It does not require an ADR unless later legal advice or
field design changes a public contract, authority boundary, or canonical data
model.

## Current-versus-target check

- **Current evidenced behavior:** Official-source requirements and conservative
  controls have been identified. No participant consent, compliant recruitment,
  ethics determination, field data, task selection, customer value, provider
  value, or independently operated supply is evidenced here.
- **Target behavior informed by this research:** A low-risk study in which
  participants understand and control participation and recording, data use is
  bounded, outputs resist re-identification, and outreach respects channel
  rules.
- **Claims this research does not authorize:** Legal compliance, legal advice,
  HREC approval, Privacy Act exemption, production readiness, independently
  operated supply, fulfilment, or customer/provider value.
