# Australian loyalty microtransaction flywheels

**Date:** 2026-08-25\
**Question:** What have successful Australian loyalty programs actually built,
why does accumulating tiny rewards work, and what should Agentic Economy copy
for atomic Operations?\
**Source policy:** Primary sources only: program terms and support material,
company reports and filings, accounting standards, and regulator publications.

## Executive conclusion

The user's aggregation thesis is right, with an important refinement:

> **The product is not the point awarded on one transaction. The product is a
> ledger that preserves many economically funded fractions until they become a
> salient, low-cost reward.**

Australia's strongest schemes combine five things:

1. **High-frequency earn:** grocery shops, card purchases, flights, fuel,
   health actions, or business bills repeatedly add to one balance.
2. **A coalition balance:** spend across many businesses or brands converges
   into one useful currency instead of dying in disconnected merchant wallets.
3. **A visible, memorable burn:** $10 off, a free flight, a fuel discount, a
   gift card, or a service benefit. The earn can be microscopic; the redemption
   must feel real.
4. **Multiple funding pools:** merchant margin, partner acquisition budgets,
   interchange and annual fees, subscription or insurance economics, controlled
   reward inventory, affiliate commissions, and breakage. No durable large
   scheme depends on an unexplained per-transaction giveaway.
5. **A liability engine:** points are not merely UI. The issuer defers revenue
   or records the obligation, estimates redemption and breakage, reverses
   rewards on refunds, and protects the pool from abuse.

The most direct Australian precedent for Agentic Economy is not a supermarket
program in isolation. It is a combination of:

- **Flybuys / Everyday Rewards:** one cross-partner balance built from routine
  low-value earns, with a clear cash-like redemption floor;
- **Qantas / Velocity:** partners buy access to a desirable currency whose
  redemption inventory can cost the issuer less than its perceived value;
- **Cotton On Perks Payday:** cumulative spend becomes a short-lived, closed-loop
  voucher that causes another purchase;
- **Medibank Live Better:** reward verified productive behaviour, not merely
  payment volume;
- **pay.com.au:** an Australian B2B platform can turn otherwise unrewarded
  routine transactions into a valuable aggregate, although in this case the
  user explicitly pays much of the reward cost; and
- **Loyalty Now:** the infrastructure is a real-time eligibility, receipt,
  partner-billing, reward-ledger and notification system—not a points screen.

For AE, every successful Qualified Operation should accrue an exact fractional
entitlement in a single **Reach balance**. Do not round at the Operation level.
Auto-redeem that balance against later Operations or inference so repeated
fractions eventually produce a genuinely free action. Fund each fraction from a
named source: supplier campaign budget, realized execution saving, or a capped
share of AE gross profit. Keep the launch currency non-transferable and
non-cash; a cash-out model introduces a much heavier cost and regulatory shape.

## Program profiles

### 1. Flybuys: coalition density and a concrete redemption floor

Flybuys is a true coalition program. Coles' privacy material describes it as a
coalition across retail, financial services, travel, utilities and health;
Flybuys says it contracts with each program participant for loyalty services.
It is owned 50/50 by Coles and Wesfarmers and had 9.9 million active members at
30 June 2025. ([Coles privacy policy](https://www.colesgroup.com.au/public/),
[Flybuys FY24 statement](https://register.flybuys.com.au/cdn/pdf/Flybuys_Modern_Slavery_Statement.pdf),
[Wesfarmers FY25 report](https://www.wesfarmers.com.au/docs/default-source/sustainability/sustainability-2025/2025-annual-report.pdf))

The base retail proposition is deliberately small: at Coles and Liquorland a
member generally receives one point per dollar, and 2,000 points can produce
$10 off. That is a **0.5% base return** before bonus offers. The balance is
useful across Coles, Bunnings, Kmart, Target, Officeworks, Reddy Express and
other partners. Points remain active if the member collects or redeems at least
once in 12 months. ([Coles Pay with Points](https://www.colesgroup.com.au/news/2026/media-releases/?page=coles-helps-customers-take-100-dollars-off-their-in-store-shop-with-20-000-flybuys-points),
[Flybuys partners](https://help.flybuys.com.au/hc/en-gb/articles/9103611834383-Where-can-I-collect-Flybuys-points),
[Flybuys expiry](https://help.flybuys.com.au/hc/en-gb/articles/9103578419983-Do-my-points-ever-expire))

Coles reported Flybuys active members up 4.4% and customers redeeming points at
Coles up 13.3% in FY25. It also said more than half of members who shopped in
store in the prior 12 months had received more than $40 off through $10
redemptions. This shows how an unexciting half-cent-per-dollar base rate becomes
salient after repeated shopping and targeted bonuses. ([Coles FY25 report](https://www.colesgroup.com.au/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/ir5sKeTxxEOndzdh00hWJw/file/Annual_Report.pdf),
[Coles Pay with Points](https://www.colesgroup.com.au/news/2026/media-releases/?page=coles-helps-customers-take-100-dollars-off-their-in-store-shop-with-20-000-flybuys-points))

However, “large and useful” is not the same as “standalone profit machine.”
Coles' FY25 accounts show a $5 million share of loss from Loyalty Pacific and a
$20 million addition to its investment. This implies strategic data, retail and
partner benefits can justify a coalition even when the operator itself is
investing or loss-making. ([Coles FY25 report, note 5.1](https://www.colesgroup.com.au/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/ir5sKeTxxEOndzdh00hWJw/file/Annual_Report.pdf))

**AE implication:** a coalition balance becomes valuable when it can be earned
across many suppliers and burned on the next useful thing. Supplier coverage
matters more than a heroic base earn rate.

### 2. Everyday Rewards: progress, personalisation and cross-category burn

Everyday Rewards has the same concrete base floor: at least one point per dollar
and 2,000 points for $10 off. Members may instead Bank for Christmas or convert
2,000 Everyday Rewards points into 1,000 Qantas Points. Points expire after 18
months without earn or redemption. ([Everyday Rewards choices](https://www.everyday.com.au/rewards/how-it-works/your-rewards-choices.html),
[program terms](https://www.everyday.com.au/terms.html))

Woolworths' 2025 results provide unusually good evidence about the aggregate
effect. Roughly 750,000 members redeemed almost $90 million through Bank for
Christmas in FY25. Its investor presentation says customers more deeply
connected to its digital experiences and offers have progressively higher
approximate share of wallet, reaching 8x for the deepest-connected cohort
relative to active members. This is company-reported association, not proof
that points alone caused the increase. ([Woolworths FY25 results](https://www.woolworthsgroup.com.au/content/dam/wwg/investors/reports/f25/f25/2936263.pdf))

The personalisation loop is explicit. Everyday says Boost offers are created
for the individual and become more personalised with shopping behaviour;
Woolworths also treats Everyday, data/AI and Cartology retail media as connected
strategic capabilities. ([Everyday app](https://www.everyday.com.au/rewards/everyday-inspiration/more-value-with-the-everyday-rewards-app.html),
[Woolworths FY25 annual report](https://www.woolworthsgroup.com.au/content/dam/wwg/sustainability/reports/f25/Woolworths%20Group%20Annual%20Report%202025%20.pdf))

The linked fuel proposition demonstrates cross-category economics: a $30
eligible supermarket shop generates a 4c/litre discount usable at participating
Ampol/EG locations within 28 days, capped at 150 litres. At EG, a further $5
in-store purchase can add another 4c/litre. The reward is designed to move a
shopper from groceries to fuel and then into the convenience store, not merely
to rebate the original basket. ([Everyday fuel terms](https://www.everyday.com.au/terms.html),
[EG Ampol offer](https://www.everyday.com.au/rewards/partners/eg-ampol.html))

**AE implication:** show progress continuously, let suppliers sponsor Boosts,
and allow earn in one capability category to burn in another. Cross-category
continuation is more flywheel-like than rebating the same call.

### 3. Qantas and Velocity: the loyalty currency itself is a B2B product

Qantas Loyalty is the clearest proof that aggregate micro-entitlements can
become a substantial business. In FY25 it reported:

- 17.6 million members;
- 222 billion points earned and 185 billion redeemed;
- $2.863 billion revenue;
- $556 million underlying EBIT at a 19.4% margin; and
- $3.556 billion of unredeemed Frequent Flyer revenue on the balance sheet.

([Qantas FY25 annual report](https://investor.qantas.com/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/doLLG5ufYkCyEPjF1tpgyw/file/annual-reports/2025-Annual-Report.pdf))

The accounting describes the actual business model. Qantas typically receives
consideration for issuing points before redemption. It recognises the marketing
component as the partner service is supplied, but defers the redemption
component using a relative standalone selling price. Revenue is recognised when
points are redeemed; breakage and weighted redemption value require actuarial
estimation. Qantas' auditors treat the valuation and expected non-redemption of
points as a key audit matter. This is infrastructure, finance and forecasting—not
decorative gamification. ([Qantas FY25 annual report, notes 20, 35 and auditor report](https://investor.qantas.com/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/doLLG5ufYkCyEPjF1tpgyw/file/annual-reports/2025-Annual-Report.pdf))

Velocity reports the same two-performance-obligation structure: participation
partners pay fees for points; the expected award cost plus margin, adjusted for
breakage, is deferred, while the residual marketing service is recognised as
points issue. FY25 Velocity underlying revenue was $450 million and underlying
EBIT $127.3 million at a 28.3% margin, with external billings up 14% and active
members up 12%. ([Virgin Australia FY25 annual report](https://www.virginaustralia.com/content/dam/vaa/documents/investor-centre/vgn-annual-report-fy25.pdf))

The perceived value of an airline point can exceed its cost because reward
flights are capacity controlled and other redemptions may use negotiated partner
inventory. Neither Qantas nor Velocity promises a stable cash value for every
point. This gives the issuer an economic lever that a pure cashback program does
not possess.

**AE implication:** “free Operation” inventory can have a face value greater
than its cost when a supplier has idle capacity, when AE batches or caches work,
or when a supplier funds acquisition. AE should sell funded Reach credits to
partners, not attempt to create all reward value from its own rake.

### 4. Cotton On Perks Payday: the exact cumulative-spend pattern

The likely meaning of “Payday points” is **Cotton On & Co. Perks Payday**. Its
terms say purchases are cumulative: $100 may be spent once or as $20 across five
shops, across the group's brands, to trigger a $10 voucher. The voucher expires
after 45 days and requires at least $20 spend. Sale items, donations and gift
cards do not earn; the reward-funded part of a later purchase does not earn;
returns reverse the points; vouchers cannot be cashed or transferred; and the
program can reject suspected resale or commercial use. ([Cotton On Perks terms](https://cottonon.com/AU/perks/perks-terms.html),
[Cotton On general terms](https://cottonon.com/AU/terms.html))

This looks generous at 10%, but the economic object is a **closed-loop voucher**:
it drives another purchase, cannot pay out cash, has a short expiry, requires
incremental spend, and is funded against Cotton On's product margin rather than
the face value of cash. The program also spans group brands, making otherwise
separate small purchases converge.

**AE implication:** preserve every tiny earn, but convert it into a “free next
Operation” only when the balance can cover a useful action. The next action
keeps the agent in the marketplace, just as the Payday voucher returns the user
to a Cotton On brand.

### 5. Credit-card points: a multi-revenue product, not free interchange

Australian bank cards typically award points on each eligible dollar and offer
cashback, gift cards, travel or partner transfers. Current examples include:

- CommBank Awards: one point per eligible dollar on the base card up to a
  statement threshold, then 0.5, with cash, gift-card, Myer and travel burns;
- NAB Rewards Signature: 1.5 points per eligible dollar to $15,000 in a monthly
  statement period, then 0.5;
- Westpac Altitude: up to three points per eligible dollar depending on card and
  category, with cash, retail and airline burns; and
- Amex Platinum Membership Rewards: 2.25 points per ordinary eligible dollar and
  no expiry while the account remains enrolled and in good standing.

([CommBank Awards](https://www.commbank.com.au/credit-cards/awards.html),
[NAB Rewards Signature](https://www.nab.com.au/personal/credit-cards/nab-rewards-cards/signature-card),
[Westpac Altitude](https://www.westpac.com.au/personal-banking/credit-cards/reward/altitude-rewards),
[Amex Membership Rewards](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-au/credit-cards/membership-benefits/the-platinum-card/membership-rewards/))

Westpac's FY25 accounts make the funding/accounting connection explicit: card
transaction revenue includes interchange net of scheme charges, but a component
is deferred because Westpac has a future service obligation under its card
reward programs; loyalty costs are recognised when points are redeemed. Annual
and program fees and other card economics also contribute. ([Westpac FY25 annual report](https://www.westpac.com.au/content/dam/public/wbc/documents/pdf/aw/ic/wbc-annual-report-2025.pdf))

Interchange is constrained in Australia. At present, designated consumer credit
schemes operate under a 0.5% weighted-average benchmark and 0.8% cap. From
1 October 2026, the RBA will lower domestic consumer credit interchange to a
0.3% cap and remove the benchmark; commercial cards retain a 0.8% cap. The RBA
found the current average consumer credit interchange rate was about 0.47%.
([RBA regulatory framework](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/credit-cards/regulatory-framework.html),
[RBA 2026 conclusions](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/interchange-fees.html))

There is another crucial microtransaction wrinkle. Amex's Australian terms say
each purchase is rounded down to the nearest dollar, only whole points are
awarded, and fractions are discarded per purchase. A stream of sub-dollar
transactions can therefore earn nothing even though their aggregate spend is
large. ([Amex Membership Rewards terms, clauses 4.1–4.3](https://www.americanexpress.com/content/dam/amex/en-au/company/terms-and-conditions/personal-cards/MembershipRewardsTermsandConditions-Nov2023.pdf))

**AE implication:** copying card points literally would break atomic-operation
rewards. AE should ledger fractional AUD value across Operations before
conversion. Also, AE should not assume card interchange can fund rewards; its
own measured gross profit and supplier budgets are much larger and more relevant
potential pools.

### 6. CommBank Yello: the live move from transaction loyalty to relationship loyalty

CommBank is closing CommBank Awards on 29 September 2026 and launching its
reimagined Yello program from 1 October. Instead of rewarding only card spend,
one visible Yello balance can be earned across eligible credit and debit cards,
home loans, savings, insurance, travel and third-party offers. It can be burned
on shopping, travel, gift cards, partner currencies or cash. Relationship tiers
change earn rates; eligible customers generally need a transaction account and
five eligible transactions per month. CBA says more than nine million enrolled
customers are in scope. ([CommBank Yello announcement](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2026/08/commbank-yello-reimagined.html),
[Awards transition details](https://www.commbank.com.au/credit-cards/card-updates.html))

CBA explicitly connects the redesign to the RBA's interchange changes, saying
whole-relationship rewards are intended to sustain customer value beyond
traditional card spend. Launch partners include Everyday Rewards, Origin, Myer,
Velocity, DoorDash, bp and Qatar. Existing Awards balances convert to Yello by
7 October for eligible users, with cash alternatives during transition.

**AE implication:** this is fresh domestic confirmation of the thesis. Do not
make the reward system depend on a single per-Operation rake. Reward the whole
agent relationship—qualified routing, retained work, supplied capability,
reproducible routes and sponsored partner actions—into one balance.

### 7. Medibank Live Better: reward the outcome, not the transaction

Medibank's Live Better rewards program pays for health and wellbeing actions,
approved claims and partner purchases. Only eligible policyholders in good
standing can redeem. There are annual action caps, per-transaction caps,
verification rights, reversal rights and a 24-month activity expiry. Rewards
include third-party merchandise, gift cards and insurance-related benefits and
cannot be directly exchanged for cash. ([Live Better terms](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/rewards/terms/),
[earning policy](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/rewards/connect/))

This is not small theatre. Medibank reported 931,000 participants and $32.8
million rewards redeemed in FY25, with 52% of policyholders engaging in a health
and wellbeing service. In HY26 it reported one million members. It also discloses
commercial arrangements under which the Live Better company may receive partner
commissions. ([Medibank FY25 annual report](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/newsroom/download/yQ9Tr5nxJRYJdWMQQnAiu),
[Live Better partner earn](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/rewards/earn/shop/),
[Medibank HY26 results](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/newsroom/post/2026-half-year-results))

The broader economic rationale—retention and potentially better health
outcomes—belongs to the insurance relationship, while merchant-funded partner
rewards add another pool. Medibank does not publicly attribute exact claim or
retention savings to each rewarded action, so those savings should not be
assumed.

**AE implication:** a call should not earn the richest reward merely because it
happened. Pay more for independently verifiable Qualified Use, downstream
continuation, successful replay, or organic return. Put annual/campaign caps
around behaviours whose causal economics are not yet known.

### 8. Cashback: aggregation works, but the funding source can still fail

ShopBack describes its Australian model as sharing a merchant affiliate
commission with the buyer. Cashback progresses from tracked to pending to
confirmed only after the store validates the purchase; returns, cancellations,
ineligible codes and broken attribution can reject it. A member needs $10 of
confirmed cashback to withdraw, and withdrawals are capped at $300 per day.
([ShopBack cashback model](https://www.shopback.com.au/guide/basics/what-is-cashback),
[tracking states](https://support.shopback.com.au/hc/en-gb/articles/360039122194-Cashback-Tracking-Top-Tips-and-What-to-Expect-While-You-Wait),
[withdrawals](https://support.shopback.com.au/hc/en-gb/articles/360039161474-Withdrawing-your-Cashback))

The Australian failure case is equally informative. ANZ closed Cashrewards in
September 2025 as part of exiting non-bank activities that “lack economic or
strategic rationale” and recognised a $78 million pre-tax goodwill impairment.
([ANZ FY25 annual report](https://www.anz.com/content/dam/anzcom/shareholder/2025-annual-report/anz-2025-annual-report.pdf))

**AE implication:** confirmed-after-settlement and a minimum external withdrawal
are excellent controls. But a cashback-only business financed by thin merchant
commissions is not a sufficient model. AE needs strategic supply, routing and
execution economics, not an affiliate portal attached to agents.

### 9. pay.com.au: the closest Australian B2B “every operation earns” analog

pay.com.au routes business expenses—including supplier invoices, tax, payroll,
super and rent—through its platform even where the recipient only accepts bank
transfer. It says more than 100,000 Australian businesses have used the platform
and billions of dollars of routine payments have been processed. Customers can
earn normal card points and optionally add PayRewards. ([pay.com.au overview](https://pay.com.au/earn-rewards-on-every-business-payment/),
[company page](https://pay.com.au/about-us/))

The reward is largely user-funded: one PayRewards point per dollar currently
costs a 1.0% fee and two points cost 1.8%, separate from card processing and any
subscription. Users may still perceive positive value because the platform
unlocks card acceptance, float, accounting integrations, two stacked reward
currencies and higher-value airline/hotel redemptions. ([PayRewards fees](https://help.pay.com.au/hc/en-au/articles/14199849479439-Do-I-pay-a-fee-to-earn-PayRewards),
[platform pricing](https://pay.com.au/earn-rewards-on-every-business-payment/))

**AE implication:** it is possible to make “boring mandatory transactions” feel
productive. But if AE charges extra to create the reward, the UI must show the
net economics. User-funded points are a purchasable reward package, not free
value.

### 10. Loyalty Now: what the infrastructure actually looks like

Australian company Loyalty Now exposes the less visible infrastructure layer:
card/member registration, transaction matching, merchant offer creation,
eligibility logic, immediate earn notifications, reward ledgering, merchant
reporting, automated partner billing and cash collection. Its APIs can issue
points or cashback, and its merchant portal exposes usage and transaction data.
([Loyalty Now card-linked technology](https://www.loyaltynow.com/card-linked-technology),
[operator product](https://www.loyaltynow.com/operator))

Medibank uses Loyalty Now as its card-linking vendor. It receives redacted card
and purchase data after Visa or Mastercard transactions, but notes that EFTPOS
routing or third-party payment intermediaries can prevent automatic earning.
This is a concrete reminder that attribution quality is part of the reward
product. ([Medibank card-linking FAQ](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/app/mym-faqs/))

**AE implication:** the agent-facing interface should expose machine-readable
eligibility and effective cost before routing, then a signed receipt with exact
earn, funding source, state and reversal conditions afterward. Supplier billing
and reward reconciliation are first-class platform services.

## Why micro-rewards aggregate successfully

### 1. The ledger removes the minimum viable transaction problem

A half-cent reward cannot be paid to a bank account economically and is not
psychologically salient. One shared internal balance can preserve it at near-zero
marginal ledger cost until it becomes $10 or a free service. Flybuys and Everyday
Rewards use 2,000-point conversion thresholds; ShopBack waits for $10 confirmed
cashback; Cotton On accumulates $100 across purchases before issuing a voucher.

The threshold is not merely friction. It:

- avoids external settlement cost on each earn;
- makes reversals possible while an event is pending;
- creates a progress gradient toward a comprehensible reward; and
- keeps value inside the network when redemption is closed-loop.

### 2. One currency makes weak individual merchants useful

A single API supplier may never issue enough value for an agent to care. A
coalition lets a fraction earned on search combine with fractions from inference,
data, identity and automation. This is why Flybuys and airline programs recruit
partners, and why Qantas measures both points earned and redeemed across its
portfolio.

### 3. Perceived value can exceed issuer cost

$10 cash costs nearly $10. A $10 retail voucher may cost less because it brings a
customer back and is redeemed against gross margin. An airline reward can use
controlled capacity. A free agent Operation can use supplier-sponsored trial
inventory, idle capacity, a cached response, a batch discount or a wholesale
commitment. This value gap is the key economic lever.

### 4. Targeted bonuses spend money only where behaviour may change

Everyday Boosts, Flybuys personalised offers, fuel cross-sell and card-linked
merchant offers do not increase the base reward for everyone. They concentrate
budget on a category, cohort, threshold or next action. AE should copy this with
new-agent trials, missing-capability exploration, continued use of a result, and
return after a lapse.

### 5. Accrued liability also produces float and data—but is not free profit

Qantas held $3.556 billion of unredeemed Frequent Flyer revenue at FY25. The
program benefits from receiving partner consideration before redemption, but it
also owes future rewards and must forecast cost and breakage. The balance enables
planning and investment; it cannot be treated as earned platform revenue on day
one.

### 6. The loop improves as transaction density grows

More eligible transactions create:

- faster progress and greater user salience;
- better targeting and causal measurement;
- more attractive distribution for supplier-funded campaigns;
- more predictable aggregate demand for negotiating supply;
- more opportunities to batch, cache or route efficiently; and
- better estimates of redemption cost and fraud patterns.

This is the actual aggregation flywheel. The reward does not need to be profitable
at the atomic level if the cohort and funding pool are profitable on aggregate.

## Why schemes fail or become extractive

1. **Fractions are discarded.** Amex's purchase-level rounding illustrates why
   naive integer points fail for sub-dollar Operations.
2. **A balance never becomes useful.** Too many disconnected supplier wallets or
   a high redemption threshold produce orphan value.
3. **Cash costs equal face value.** Pure cashback has less room than negotiated
   inventory or a closed-loop service reward.
4. **One thin commission funds everything.** Cashrewards' closure is a warning
   against mistaking transaction volume for sustainable margin.
5. **Rewards reward circular volume.** Card programs exclude cash equivalents,
   self-owned merchants and refunded transactions; AE must similarly exclude
   self-dealing, recursive calls and wash traffic.
6. **Expiry and devaluation destroy trust.** The ACCC recommends clear terms,
   prominent expiry notice, fair changes and timely notice before reducing earn
   or redemption value. ([ACCC loyalty guidance](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/customer-loyalty-schemes),
   [ACCC final report](https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/customer-loyalty-schemes-final-report))
7. **The scheme depends on interchange that regulation compresses.** Australia's
   October 2026 consumer credit cap makes this especially current.
8. **The operator books issued credit as revenue.** AASB 15 says a material right
   to future discounted goods can be a performance obligation and customer
   prepayments remain a contract liability; expected breakage is recognised in
   proportion to rights exercised only when the estimate is supportable.
   ([AASB 15, B39–B47](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-may-2022))

## Concrete design for Agentic Economy in Australia

### The product: Aggregate Reach, not “points”

Use a single exact internal balance across all Operations. The balance is an
earned entitlement to AE-delivered Operations, inference or protection—not a
transferable token.

```text
Operation cost                                A$0.023700
supplier campaign earn                        A$0.001200
realized routing/caching surplus share         A$0.000430
AE gross-profit loyalty allocation             A$0.000240
Reach accrued                                  A$0.001870
Reach balance after Operation                  A$0.184360
next eligible Operation                        A$0.171000  -> FREE
remaining Reach                                A$0.013360
```

The user/agent receives “free shit” periodically, while the system preserves the
economic truth at every atomic step.

### Precision and rounding

- Store value in integer micros or a similarly exact fixed-point unit; never use
  binary floating point for the liability.
- Calculate earn on the exact settled Operation amount or verified outcome.
- Do **not** round per Operation. Round only when presenting or settling an
  external instrument.
- Carry every remainder forward.
- Reverse the exact original earn if the Operation is refunded, invalidated or
  determined to be abusive.

This is more microtransaction-native than Australian card programs.

### Redemption

For launch:

- no cash purchase of Reach;
- no cash-out, withdrawal, transfer, secondary market or external spend;
- automatically apply Reach to the next eligible Operation when it can make
  that Operation fully free, unless the principal opts to save it;
- optionally allow partial application if agents optimize strict effective cost;
- show both exact balance and progress toward a concrete next free action; and
- avoid expiry while the account remains active; if expiry is introduced, use an
  inactivity rule with prominent advance notice, not silent per-credit decay.

A $10 consumer threshold is inappropriate for agents. The meaningful threshold
is **the cheapest useful complete Operation**, not a round fiat amount. Keeping
redemption internal means AE can redeem sub-dollar balances without bank payout
fees.

### Funding hierarchy

Each reward receipt should name one of these sources:

1. **Supplier-funded campaign** — a supplier pre-funds acquisition, Qualified
   Use, continued use or return-use credits.
2. **Realized execution surplus** — verified saving from supplier competition,
   batching, caching, wholesale commitment or a cheaper equivalent route.
3. **AE gross-profit allocation** — a fixed, capped share of realized platform
   gross profit, calculated after supplier cost and refunds.
4. **Explicit launch budget** — temporary and separately labelled; never mixed
   into claims of self-sustaining economics.

Do not fund the base promise from payment-card interchange. The Australian pool
is too small and declining. Do not fund it by silently adding an inference markup
and calling the difference a reward.

### Supplier economics

Copy coalition and card-linked-offer infrastructure:

- supplier declares eligible Operation, principal cohort, reward rate, outcome
  condition, maximum budget, start/end time and clawback window;
- AE quotes **effective cost after eligible earn** before routing;
- Qualified Use produces an immutable pending reward receipt;
- continuation or return transitions the receipt to confirmed;
- supplier is billed from a pre-funded budget or accrued settlement;
- routing quality has a non-negotiable floor and sponsorship is labelled; and
- supplier receives aggregate conversion and retention reporting without raw
  private agent traces.

This gives suppliers an acquisition product analogous to Qantas selling points
or Loyalty Now selling verified merchant outcomes.

### Anti-abuse

- reward settled, independently attributable Qualified Uses, not call attempts;
- exclude principal-to-self, common-control and circular routes;
- cap rewards per principal, supplier, work reference and time window;
- use a pending state until refund/failure/continuation windows close;
- reverse all dependent rewards when the underlying charge reverses;
- do not award on the reward-funded portion of a transaction;
- require independent reproduction before route residuals begin;
- make self-trading economically negative after all fees; and
- reserve suspension, clawback and termination for fraud with clear appeal and
  evidence procedures.

These controls recur across Cotton On, Amex, Flybuys, ShopBack and Medibank.

### Liability and Australian structure

ASIC's 2026 instrument declares qualifying loyalty schemes not to be Chapter 7
financial products where the dominant purpose is promoting purchases/services,
credits arise from those purchases/services, and credits buy goods/services or
another benefit. ([ASIC Instrument 2026/167 and explanatory statement](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00318/latest/text))

That does not remove accounting, consumer-law, privacy or tax work. Under AASB
15, the right to future discounted AE services may require allocating transaction
consideration to a performance obligation and carrying a contract liability.
Operationally AE should maintain, at minimum:

- issued, pending, confirmed, redeemed, reversed and expired balances;
- funding source and restricted campaign balance;
- expected redemption cost and timing;
- outstanding face value and accounting liability reconciliation;
- expiry and breakage cohorts;
- supplier receivable/payable reconciliation; and
- privacy-safe attribution evidence.

Breakage may improve economics, but should be an observed accounting estimate,
not a product strategy. The ACCC has already identified unclear expiry,
devaluation and data practices as loyalty-scheme harms.

## Recommended first experiment

Run the smallest experiment that tests aggregation rather than merely testing a
promotion.

### Cohort and duration

- one fungible capability family;
- at least three suppliers;
- 20–50 agent principals or harnesses;
- four weeks; and
- a holdout cohort receiving prices but no Reach accrual.

### Reward design

- base earn: 10–20% of AE's realized gross profit, not 10–20% of GMV;
- one supplier-funded Qualified Trial campaign;
- one continuation bonus funded only after downstream consumption;
- exact fractional accrual on every eligible settled Operation; and
- auto-redemption when balance covers the next useful Operation.

### Measure

- time and number of Operations to first free action;
- share of agents reaching a first redemption;
- incremental routed Operations versus holdout;
- organic repeat use after reward exhaustion;
- supplier cost per Qualified Use and retained principal;
- AE gross profit before and after rewards;
- outstanding liability, redemption rate and reversal rate;
- concentration of rewards by principal/supplier;
- abuse loss; and
- percentage of rewards by funding source.

### Kill rules

Stop or redesign if:

- fewer than 25% of active principals reach a meaningful redemption during the
  test;
- reward cost exceeds incremental gross profit plus explicit supplier campaign
  funding;
- rewarded usage does not continue organically;
- one supplier or principal captures the pool through circular volume;
- agents optimize reward face value at the expense of output quality; or
- liability and supplier settlement cannot reconcile exactly.

## Decision

Build the aggregation ledger and machine-readable reward receipts into the
agent-first transaction model. Do not launch a generic consumer points program.

The durable Australian-shaped promise is:

> **Every Qualified Operation contributes an exact fraction toward a later free
> Operation. Fractions never disappear at the transaction boundary, and every
> fraction names the supplier budget, realized saving, or gross profit that
> funded it.**

That is the part of Flybuys, Payday, airline miles, credit-card points and usage
loyalty worth cloning. The points branding, artificial integer scale and
breakage theatre are not.
