# Agent-native economic behaviours — AE capture test (2026-07-13)

## 1. Contract and test

**Question.** Which behaviours become economically viable only when software can monitor, decide, contact, and transact continuously at near-zero attention cost; which need AE’s neutral kernel + admitted registry; and which a platform can internalise?

**Labels.** **OBSERVED** = primary source says shipped/in use; **DEMO/PREVIEW** = announced, beta, waitlist, or illustrative; **INFERRED** = projection from observed primitives; **UNKNOWN** = no reliable evidence found. “Agent-native” does **not** mean humans never delegated these jobs; brokers, service contracts, standing orders, and procurement agents are old. It means the behaviour’s *granularity, persistence, or frequency* was uneconomic before machine attention approached zero.

**Neutral-layer test.** AE has structural leverage only when an action spans independently controlled principals, assistants, businesses, or systems **and** needs portable admission, scoped/revocable authority, exact-action digest, duplicate safety, and replayable evidence. If discovery, mandate, counterparty, execution, and record all live inside one platform, that platform captures it.

**Repo boundary.** AE’s target is a natural-language request → registered capability bindings → inspectable signed route quote → bounded approval → execute once → Root Run/evidence ([PRODUCT.md §§Product architecture/Core lifecycle](PRODUCT.md)). Current wedge is only one consented inquiry to one claimed, admitted business; no autonomous negotiation, booking, ordering, payment, portfolio, or recurring-work claim is allowed ([WEDGE-LADDER §§1–3, 5–7](.planning/design/WEDGE-LADDER.md)). The kernel’s honest ceiling is a neutral clearing envelope for delegated business actions—admission, mandate, canonical digest, idempotency, evidence/replay, temporal truth—not assistant, workflow, settlement, or outcome adjudication ([KERNEL-CEILING §§1,3,7](.planning/design/KERNEL-CEILING.md)).

**Finding.** The first shipped agent-native economy is not autonomous multi-agent bargaining. It is (a) persistent conditional mandates inside vertically integrated platforms, (b) proactive information, and (c) always-on business response/booking. Cross-platform agent↔agent negotiation remains **UNKNOWN** in the cited product evidence.

## 4. Frequency verdict

**Steelman YES.** Agents can turn local services into continuous *coordination*: monitor assets and deadlines, refresh evidence/quotes, expose expiring capacity, tender portfolios, and reconcile records. Coase’s core point is that using the price mechanism has search/negotiation/contracting costs ([Coase, “The Nature of the Firm,” 1937](https://faculty.washington.edu/mfan/is582/articles/Coase1937.pdf)); reducing those costs makes smaller and more frequent market comparisons rational. The household may generate **monthly/weekly machine events** even while buying only a few physical jobs.

**Attack NO.** Physical failure/maintenance rates, travel, skilled-labour scarcity, diagnosis, minimum call-out economics and trust do not collapse with software transaction costs. Most projected events are low-value observations, not transactions. Always-on tendering can create spam, adverse selection, race-to-the-bottom pricing, quote fatigue and contractor refusal. A platform with calendar + CRM + payment + customer relationship can execute more cheaply than a neutral hop. Thus “2–6 jobs/year becomes a continuous economy” is true only if the unit is *governed coordination event*; it is false or unproven if the unit is paid service job.

**First credible post-R1 high-frequency wedge: expiring capacity + always-on quote refresh, narrowly paired.** A business publishes a signed, short-lived cancellation/availability slot and constraints; previously consented consumers/property managers may receive or request a refreshed, scope-stable quote; any booking remains business-confirmed. Why first: it creates daily supply events, directly extends admitted registry + R1 brief/response lineage, avoids pretending annual maintenance itself is high-frequency, and tests cross-party temporal truth/idempotency. Gate it behind R1 proof and then R2/R3 structured-response readiness—current ladder explicitly forbids surfacing it now.

**Second, stronger moat but later:** warranty/compliance evidence chains. They exercise the kernel uniquely and resist assistant capture, but produce trust/retention value rather than an obvious demand-acquisition loop.

## 5. AE position: steelman, attack, decision

**Steelman.** AE is unusually aligned to behaviours where no actor should own the whole record: portfolio tenders, insurer↔policyholder↔trade dispatch, pooled demand, cross-assistant negotiation, and portable compliance. Its admitted capability binding plus exact mandate, temporal truth, duplicate-safe execution and evidence replay are the missing shared grammar. A registry can make ephemeral capacity and evidence portable rather than trapped in one CRM.

**Attack.** Those same behaviours need density, structured supply, calendar/work-order integration, liability allocation and accepted schemas—none follows from having a kernel. Amazon proves integrated mandate execution; Meta owns enormous business-thread distribution; ServiceTitan already owns trade capacity and booking. UCP/AP2 or assistant-native audit logs may commoditise AE’s primitives within 12–24 months ([KERNEL-CEILING §3](.planning/design/KERNEL-CEILING.md)). Neutrality is not a moat unless independent counterparties actually demand AE receipts and portability.

**Verdict.** AE is **not uniquely positioned** for proactive detection, reordering, deal watching, one-business booking, or machine payments: platforms capture those. It is **architecturally well-positioned but commercially unproven** for cross-party recurring coordination and portable evidence. The falsifiable test is not feature breadth: can AE get independently controlled assistants/business systems to accept the same mandate digest, capacity assertion, response schema and replay record without proprietary translation? If not, remain an adapter/private network; do not claim “the” agentic-economy layer.

## 6. Immediate falsification metrics

1. **Frequency:** per active principal, measure machine coordination events *and separately* physical jobs; reject any thesis that conflates them.
2. **Cross-party necessity:** share of useful runs spanning ≥2 independent systems/principals; one-platform runs do not validate neutrality.
3. **Supply freshness:** percentage of admitted capacity/quote assertions updated before expiry and honoured by businesses.
4. **Governance value:** duplicate/refusal/drift incidents prevented; records reopened/exported/cited; disputes resolved by replay.
5. **Capture test:** integrate one consumer assistant, one trade CRM, and one independent business endpoint against one unchanged envelope. Proprietary per-platform semantics = no neutral moat.
6. **Wedge threshold:** do not advance from R1 until real claimed businesses reliably reply; then test capacity/quote refresh in one dense category/geography before portfolio, pooling, insurance, or negotiation claims.
