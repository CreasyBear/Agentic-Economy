# Minimum participant materials for a low-risk WA commercial field study

**Owner:** Product  
**Status:** Active  
**Maturity:** External field  
**Question:** What minimum content and separation should AE use for participant
information, participation consent, recording/transcription consent,
attributable publication consent, and withdrawal/complaints instructions?  
**Decision affected:** None  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-07-18  
**Review by:** 2026-08-18  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Superseded by:** None

> This is a conservative primary-source synthesis for an adult, low-risk
> commercial field study in Western Australia. It is not legal advice, ethics
> approval, or a finding that AE is or is not an APP entity. Qualified
> Australian/WA advice is required before fieldwork if the entity, ethics
> pathway, data processors, participant population, or proposed uses remain
> uncertain.

## Finding

**INFERRED:** Use five visibly separate parts, even if delivered in one packet.
Each permission must be independently answerable and recorded. A participant
must be able to join an unrecorded session and refuse attribution without losing
the ability to participate. Do not use one signature to bundle participation,
recording, third-party transcription, and publication.

This separation follows the OAIC's warning that bundled consent may undermine
voluntariness and its expectation that consent be adequately informed,
voluntary, current, specific, and given with capacity ([OAIC APP Guidelines,
Chapter B, B.38 and B.45-B.54](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts)).
The National Statement separately requires sufficient information, a clearly
established voluntary decision, withdrawal information, and disclosure of the
likely form of dissemination ([NHMRC National Statement 2025, 2.2.2-2.2.7 and
2.2.19-2.2.20](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).

## 1. Participant information notice

**OBSERVED:** Participation must be voluntary and based on adequate
understanding of the purpose, methods, demands, risks, and potential benefits.
Participants should also receive researcher and complaints contacts; privacy
and confidentiality protections; withdrawal rights, implications, and whether
data can be withdrawn; funding/interests; payment; and likely dissemination
([NHMRC 2025, 2.2.2 and 2.2.6](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).
If AE is an APP entity, APP 5 additionally requires reasonable steps at or
before collection (or as soon as practicable afterwards) to notify applicable
matters including entity identity/contact, collection circumstances and
purposes, consequences of non-collection, usual disclosures, privacy-policy and
access/complaint information, and likely overseas recipients
([OAIC, APP 5](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/read-the-australian-privacy-principles)).

**INFERRED — minimum notice content:**

- version/date; study name; AE legal entity or clearly identified responsible
  person; researcher, privacy, and complaints contacts;
- why the person was invited; purpose; activities; duration; data and case
  artefacts requested; voluntary nature; ability to skip questions; reasonable
  risks, burdens, expected benefits, payment, funding, and relevant interests;
- actual collection methods; named recording/transcription types; processors,
  access recipients, storage countries, security approach, retention/deletion
  dates, de-identification approach, and intended uses;
- ordinary disclosures, proposed reporting/publication and whether a results
  summary will be offered;
- consequences of declining each optional permission; withdrawal process,
  deadline and limits; privacy access/correction and complaints routes.

The notice informs; it does not itself grant permission.

## 2. Participation consent

**OBSERVED:** The participant's voluntary decision must be clearly established;
no person should be coerced or pressured, and refusal should cause no
disadvantage ([NHMRC 2025, 2.2.7, 2.2.9 and
2.2.19](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).
OAIC says express consent may be oral, written, electronic, or voice agreement
and recommends recording consent
([OAIC APP Guidelines, B.39 and B.45](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts)).

**INFERRED — minimum participation declaration:** record participant ID, notice
version, date/time, consent method, and an affirmative declaration that the
adult has read or received the notice, had questions answered, understands
participation and withdrawal, participates voluntarily, and consents to the
specified study activities and necessary data collection. Include separate
`yes/no` choices for any optional case artefacts or follow-up contact. Silence,
attendance, a pre-ticked box, or a manager's permission is insufficient for the
individual.

## 3. Audio, video, and transcription consent

**OBSERVED:** WA's `Surveillance Devices Act 1998` generally prohibits a party
from recording a private conversation or private activity. The ordinary
party-recording exceptions include express or implied consent from each
principal party ([WA Act, ss
5(1)(b), 5(3)(c), 6(1)(b) and 6(3)(a)](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_27928.htm/%24FILE/Surveillance%20Devices%20Act%201998%20-%20%5B02-f0-03%5D.html)).
Section 9 separately restricts knowing publication or communication of a
recorded private conversation/activity and includes an exception for consent
from each principal party ([WA Act, s
9](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_27928.htm/%24FILE/Surveillance%20Devices%20Act%201998%20-%20%5B02-f0-03%5D.html)).

**INFERRED — minimum recording choices:** obtain an affirmative `yes/no` from
every person present, before starting, separately for audio, video/screen
capture, and automated or human transcription. Name the recording purpose,
who/services will receive it, whether processing or storage occurs overseas,
who may access each derivative, retention/deletion dates, and the unrecorded
alternative. Record session ID, every principal party, consent time, exact
choices, and the recorder. Stop recording if any party refuses or withdraws;
do not admit an undeclared observer or meeting bot.

**INFERRED:** Sending audio/video to a transcription service is a communication
of the record, not merely a recording setting. Treat that disclosure as a
specific permission and disclose the actual processor. Consent to transcription
does not grant model-training, product-training, evaluation, sales, or public
use.

## 4. Attributable quotation and publication consent

**OBSERVED:** Publications must prevent a participant's identity being
reasonably ascertained unless the participant agreed to identification
([NHMRC 2025,
3.1.41](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).
Researchers should distinguish disclosure to named third parties, sharing with
researchers, and public disclosure, and clarify the permission applying to each
([NHMRC 2025,
3.1.58-3.1.60](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).
WA Act s 9 creates a distinct publication/communication restriction for
recording-derived material.

**INFERRED — minimum publication permission:** default to de-identified,
non-attributable reporting. If attribution is genuinely necessary, seek fresh
permission after the exact quote or extract exists. Show the participant the
verbatim text/context, proposed name, role and business attribution, named
recipient or publication channel, purpose, edit rights, publication timing, and
the practical deadline for revocation. Record a separate `yes/no` for each
quote/extract and each channel. Permission to record, quote anonymously, name
the individual, name the business, share privately, and publish publicly are
not interchangeable.

## 5. Withdrawal and complaints instructions

**OBSERVED:** Participants may withdraw at any stage and must be told before
consenting about consequences and whether data can be withdrawn
([NHMRC 2025, 2.2.6(g) and
2.2.20](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).
OAIC says consent withdrawal should be easy and accessible; past consent cannot
then authorise future use/disclosure
([OAIC APP Guidelines,
B.54](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts)).
The National Statement expects an accessible complaints recipient and
publicised handling/resolution procedure
([NHMRC 2025,
5.7.1](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)).

**INFERRED — minimum instructions:** give one plain-language email/phone route,
participant/study identifiers needed, a no-reason/no-disadvantage statement,
immediate cessation of future participation/recording/use, what identifiable
source material can be deleted, the exact cutoff after which irreversible
de-identification, aggregation, completed disclosure, or publication cannot
practicably be undone, response timeframe, and confirmation of action. State
separately how to complain, the accessible complaints contact, escalation
route, expected acknowledgement/resolution timing, and that complaining will
not disadvantage the participant. The field researcher should not be the only
complaints route.

## Field-entry blockers

The materials cannot be truthfully finalised until these are resolved:

1. **UNKNOWN:** AE's responsible legal entity, APP coverage, accountable field
   owner, privacy contact, and complaints contact/escalation.
2. **UNKNOWN:** Whether institutional, partner, funder, or publication-venue
   ethics review applies and who has determined that.
3. **UNKNOWN:** Exact study activities, requested fields/artefacts, risks,
   participant payment, funding and researcher interests.
4. **UNKNOWN:** Exact recording/transcription vendors, subprocessors, storage
   locations, access roles, security controls, model-training settings, and
   verified deletion paths.
5. **UNKNOWN:** Retention dates, the workable withdrawal/data-removal cutoff,
   publication channels, and the person authorised to execute deletion and
   complaints handling.
6. **UNKNOWN:** Whether case artefacts or sessions include unconsented third
   parties; one participant cannot provide recording/publication consent for
   another principal party.

Until those blockers are answered, these findings support drafting only. They
do not prove consent, legal compliance, ethics approval, recruitment, customer
or provider value, independently operated supply, fulfilment, or production
readiness.
