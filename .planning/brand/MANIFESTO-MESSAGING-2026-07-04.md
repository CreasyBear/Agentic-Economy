## 1. THE MANIFESTO

Everyone knows the afternoon. The hot water's gone, or the switchboard keeps
tripping, or someone you love needs a hand you can't give on your own. So you ask
— the assistant on your phone, the search bar, the tool that was going to change
everything. It hands you a tidy list: the best businesses in your area. Then it
goes quiet.

And you're back where you always are. Ten tabs open. Three quotes that don't
measure the same thing. A half-typed message to a mate who knows a guy. The
deciding, the calling, the chasing, the working out of what it actually costs —
all of it lands back in your lap.

An answer is not an outcome. A list is not a decision. Somewhere between the
question you asked and the thing you needed done, the help just stopped.

That's the part we're building. You ask once, in your own words — and it goes out
in writing, to the businesses that actually do the work, and stays a record you
can point to. No phone tag. No wondering what was said. No starting over.

Ask once. It gets sorted.

real, written approach to the businesses that actually do the work, and it stays a
record you can point to. No phone tag. No guessing. The exhale when it is finally
sorted, on purpose, every time.

## 3. MESSAGING HIERARCHY

**Master promise:** Ask once. It gets sorted.

Each pillar carries a household framing and its business-owner mirror. The
business lines together are the ROI pitch: work arrives in writing, no lead fees,
you own your page.

### Pillar 1 — Ask once, in plain language.
- **Household:** Say what you need the way you would say it to a friend. One ask,
  not ten tabs and a group chat.
- **Business owner:** The people reaching you already know what they want. Fewer,
  clearer inquiries, in your words and theirs.

### Pillar 2 — Everything in writing. *(ROI mirror)*
- **Household:** One clear ask goes out, one written record comes back. No phone
  tag, nothing you have to remember or re-explain.
- **Business owner:** Work arrives in writing, direct to you. No lead fees, no
  resold leads, no paying to talk to your own customer.

### Pillar 3 — Own the ground you stand on.
- **Household:** Compare who actually does what, side by side, on pages built to be
  read by you and your assistant alike.
- **Business owner:** Your page is yours. Claim it free, keep it right, and get
  found by people ready to ask.

## 5. VOICE CONTRACT

Ten rules. The public register is the master-promise register — plain confidence,
household language, show don't announce. On the receipt and the inquiry that
confidence becomes exactness and momentum, never caveats. The founder's
future-tense conviction (the BONES §2 "we will build it" north star) is the
internal and founder register only — see the register-scoping note.

**Register scoping (per `COUNCIL-CONVERGENCE-2026-07-05.md`):** the **public**
voice — manifesto, campaign, and product surfaces — is the master-promise
register: plain confidence in household words, "show, don't announce." Completion
language ("done," "sorted") lives here as the promise, not a claim. The swagger —
the future-tense "we will book, we will charge, we will confirm" declaration — is
**internal and founder register only** (BONES §2 / founder notes), never public
manifesto, campaign, launch post, hero, ad, or product copy. On shipped product
surfaces (inquiry, receipt, listing),
state plainly what happens today: the ask goes out, in writing, and you keep the
record. The full 5-register matrix lives in `.planning/brand/VOICE-2026-07-05.md`.

1. **Conviction over hedged maybes — plain confidence, not throat-clearing.**
   - Do: "Ask once. It gets sorted."
   - Don't: "We hope to eventually help connect you with some local businesses."

2. **First person plural. We do things; we do not "aim to."**
   - Do: "We close the distance between asking and done."
   - Don't: "The platform aims to facilitate connections between parties."

3. **Name the enemy by showing it — the lived afternoon, not a battle cry.**
   - Do: "You asked once and got 'the best businesses in your area.' Then silence."
   - Don't: "We're here to end the halfway."

4. **Concrete nouns carry the swagger on product surfaces. No adjective doing a verb's job.**
   - Do: "One ask out. One written record back."
   - Don't: "A truly seamless, best-in-class inquiry experience."

5. **Receipts read as momentum, not apology. State what happened, then point forward.**
   - Do (receipt): "Your inquiry is written and ready to send to {business}." (the
     contract line stays where required: "AE has not booked, charged, or confirmed.")
   - Cut: any sentence that turns the receipt into a liability lecture or a list
     of powers AE lacks.

6. **Contract facts are small. One clean line where the contract needs it, never a defensive drumbeat.**
   - Do: "The business confirms timing, quote, and availability."
   - Cut: disclaimer blocks, warranty language, and stacked lists of everything
     AE has not done.

7. **Never fabricate what we have not shipped. Ambition is trajectory; capability is only what is real today.**
   - Do: "We are building toward where answers become action."
   - Don't: "AE books your plumber and pays them for you." (not shipped, so never say it)

8. **Household-real, not economist. Speak the afternoon, not the market.**
   - Do: "Ten tabs, three quotes you can't line up, and voicemail."
   - Don't: "AE optimizes matching efficiency across the domestic trade sector."

9. **Kinetic verbs, short lines. Cut the throat-clearing.**
   - Do: "Ask once. It's sorted."
   - Don't: "In order to get started, you may wish to consider submitting a request."

10. **No hollow marketing words. If a competitor could paste the line onto their site unchanged, delete it.**
    - Do: "Work arrives in writing. No lead fees."
    - Don't: "A revolutionary, world-class platform that empowers unique, seamless experiences."
