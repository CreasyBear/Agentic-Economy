# Territory C — The Real Marketplace

One-line emotional bet: Agentic Economy feels like real local businesses and real people being handed across a warm-but-strict trust desk, not an abstract AI tool.

## Palette

- Bone `#F6F1E8` — primary page/background warmth.
- Linen `#FFFDFC` — clean product surface and docket cards.
- Stone `#D8D0C2` — neutral field, dividers, inactive material.
- Ink `#191816` — primary type, logo, high-trust CTAs.
- Slate `#6F6A62` — secondary text and quiet labels.
- Clay `#A85C3A` — the one warm editorial accent for stamps, trail dots, and source cues.

## Type direction

Refined grotesk, high-contrast product typography with tight tracking for headlines and clear readable body copy. Compact monospaced numerals only for receipt IDs, dates, and source-trail metadata.

## Logo concept + construction

A refined registry stamp: a threshold arch and two docket lines form a subtle AE/belonging mark. The mark reads as “listed in the registry” rather than a corporate shield; the negative space implies the handoff path.

## Motif system

- Provider storefront heroes with fictional local business names.
- Flat inquiry receipt / docket cards with hairline rules and mono IDs.
- Source/freshness stamps using “business supplied”, “last checked”, “published”, and “listed”.
- Proof spine: Published → Freshness → Inquiry sent → Business reply.
- Service geography panels with quiet map-line texture and restrained clay markers.
- Owner-confirmed notes that make providers proud without implying booking, payment, dispatch, reviews, or availability confirmation.

## Imagery treatment rules

Use warm editorial, tactile, local-commerce imagery: tradespeople, storefronts, service areas, tools, and owner pride. Keep imagery color-graded into bone/stone/ink with clay as the only accent. Avoid purple AI gradients, glassmorphism, blobs, star reviews, real brands, fake verification marks, booking/payment/dispatch scenes, or any completed-transaction cues.
