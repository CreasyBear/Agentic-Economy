# AE CONSUMER NAME — Round 2 (2026-07-05)

Authority: `COUNCIL-CONVERGENCE-2026-07-05.md` — founder took **Option B** (re-run
naming, new brief). This file supersedes `CANDIDATES-2026-07-04.md` (round 1).

**What round 1 got wrong (unanimous council reject, Aeco 3–4/10):** it chased
*category compression* — the name had to "own the category by being derived from
it" (AECO ← Agentic ECOnomy). The council killed that logic outright:

- The category-claim job is **already done** by the corporate name *Agentic
  Economy*. The consumer name has one job: the household mouth.
- Compression is a *reward for winning*, not a *strategy to win* — "Google"
  earned its verb after a billion sessions; you cannot assign yourself the
  compressed form of a phrase no household has ever said (Strategist, KILL 1).
- Every scarce awareness dollar then teaches two things at once — "Aeco means
  Agentic ECOnomy" *and* "no, not eco / not echo / not the energy company."

**Round-2 mandate:** the consumer name lives in the **master-promise register** —
the *"Ask once. It gets sorted."* family: **outcome, completion, relief,
momentum.** Not the category. The exhale.

This is internal naming strategy. The verb-tests below describe the *target*
behaviour the promise register points at (ask once, it's sorted) — not a
booking/payment feature shipped today. That honesty rail is unchanged.

## 4. Final ranking — Top 5

Weighing the six R2 filters **and** collision reality. **Sorted** and **Settled**
fall out (HIGH, sector-adjacent AU incumbents on the exact word). **Breeze** falls
out (crowded, low distinctiveness). **Aeco** fails the new brief. What remains is
the recommendable set:

### #1 — **Cinch**
> *"I cinched a plumber — sorted by lunch."*

The strongest **real word** with **zero pronunciation ambiguity, no
eco/echo/energy pull, AU-English mouth-feel, and no AU services collision.** One
syllable; Dave and Margaret read *and spell* it right the first time, no guide,
no decoder ring. It is not fake-ocker, but it is pub-clean in Australia: short,
hard-edged, and already understood in "it's a cinch" / "cinched it." It lives
dead-centre in the master-promise register and spans **two** of its four notes at
once — *"it's a cinch"* (relief) and *"cinched it"* (completion / secured).
Crucially the verb is **earned, not assigned**: "cinch the deal / cinched it" is
already idiomatic English, so it clears the exact kill that sank Aeco's invented
"I Aeco'd it." Collision is **MODERATE and non-adjacent** — the AU holder is a
Western-apparel label (Class 25); the used-car cinch is UK-only; the
services-adjacent *Cinch Home Services* is US-only (the AU-first screen surfaced
no local services collision; a future US move needs a look). Launch posture:
`getcinch` / `trycinch` / `cinchit` / `cinch.au`, confirm at registrar.

### #2 — **Handled**
> *"Reckoned I'd be on the phone all arvo. Handled it in one message — sorted by lunch."*

The strongest **fallback** in the set: a completion-register word with a native
idiom — *"consider it handled"* — instant read, instant spell, and no eco/echo
drag. Two marks keep it at #2. First, an exact **Handled AU** household-management
brand means the ownership story is merely manageable, not clean. Second, the verb
attaches to a **task**, not a **person** — "I handled it / handled the job" is
perfect, "I handled a plumber" wobbles against the pub sentence-test the brief
prizes. Promote it only if a Class 35/42 search snags Cinch.

### #3 — **Onto**
> *"I got onto a plumber — sorted by lunch."*

The most **AU-authentic** line of all — "get onto someone" *literally means
contact them*, which is exactly what the product does; the defunct UK *Onto*
namesake never touched AU. But you cannot build a defensible mark on a
**preposition**: it's near-impossible to trademark, impossible to own the exact
domain, and low-distinctiveness. Wonderful phrase, structurally weak asset — a
tagline waiting to happen more than a name.

### #4 — **Doddle**
> *"Finding a sparky used to be a nightmare. Doddle now — sorted by lunch."*

The **purest master-promise-register word** in the whole field: *"it's a doddle"*
= dead easy, the exhale itself. Reads and (mostly) spells clean; charming AU/UK
mouth. Held back by a **weak verb** ("I doddled a plumber" doesn't survive the
sentence test) and a **global-corp-owned brand** (Blue Yonder/Panasonic) sitting
on the same word in logistics — non-adjacent to AU consumer, but a bigger shadow
to launch beside than Cinch's apparel label.

### #5 — **Sorto**
> *"I Sorto'd a plumber — sorted by lunch."*

Highest on **register + AU mouth-feel**: it *is* "sorted," clipped into the
servo/arvo/rego/bottle-o "-o" pocket, and it verbs in the native AU clip. But two
real drags: a **sector-adjacent namesake** (Sorto = property maintenance/repairs
software) sits in AE's lane, and the **"sort of"** shadow leaks the certainty the
register is built on. The best *coinage* option; not the cleanest option.
