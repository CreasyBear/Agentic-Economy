# AE CONSUMER NAME — AECO-family candidates (2026-07-04)

Authority: `BONES-2026-07-04.md §6` (name architecture). Direction **locked by
founder**: the consumer name is a *category compression* of "Agentic Economy" —
AECO, Aeco, and kin. The name must OWN the category by being derived from it.
"Agentic Economy" stays as the corporate/category frame; this file names the
thing a household *says*.

This is internal naming strategy. Nothing here claims shipped capability — the
verb-tests below describe the *target* behaviour (ask once, it's sorted), not a
booking/payment feature that exists today.

## Filters (from BONES §6)

| Code | Filter | What wins |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **SYL** | ≤3 syllables | fewer, punchier |
| **SPELL** | pub-test spellable | a punter can write it after hearing it once, loud room |
| **VERB** | verb-able | "I ___'d a plumber" survives |
| **MOTION** | completion / motion energy | reads as momentum, not a soft noun |
| **AU** | AU mouth-feel | lands in the arvo / servo / rego / bottle-o clip pocket |
| **AGENT** | agent-era signal | visibly derived from *Agentic Economy*; reads new-world, not legacy |

Scored 1–5 each, /30 total. `KILL` = removed before shortlist, one-line reason.

## 3. Final ranking — Top 5

Weighting the BONES filters *and* collision reality. Aecon (HIGH, construction
giant, trades-adjacent) and Aecos (verb-hostile plural) and Ageco (semantic
"age" flaw) fall out of the recommendable set; they were screened for
completeness but a challenger brand shouldn't launch into a sector-adjacent
giant or a name that reads "aging".

### #1 — **Aeco**

*"Just Aeco it." · "I Aeco'd a plumber — sorted by lunch."* Clips exactly like
"I Ubered / I'll servo it": the "-o" tail drops it straight into the Australian
mouth (arvo, servo, rego, bottle-o), and it's the **only** candidate whose
spelling visibly *is* the category — A-E-C-O reads back as *Agentic ECOnomy*, so
owning the word owns the claim. It verbs cleanly, carries motion, and is the
founder-locked root, so strategy and score agree. Collision is **MODERATE, not
fatal**: the sharpest thorn is AECO Energy (AU energy-tech), but energy
brokerage vs a consumer commerce assistant sit in different trademark classes —
coexistence is plausible pending a Class 35/42 screen. Exact-match domains are
gone, but the verb-prefix estate (`getaeco.com`, `useaeco.com`, `tryaeco.com`,
`heyaeco.com`) is **open**, which is the standard modern launch posture anyway.
It owns the category because it *is* the category, compressed to four letters.
**Lock the pronunciation as "AY-koh"** (not "EE-ko") — it maximises the AU clip
and walls the word off from "eco/echo".

### #2 — **Aeko**

*"I Aeko'd it."* The cleanest respelling in the set — a punter spells it right
first try, and "AY-ko" is friction-free in an Australian mouth. But it pays for
that cleanliness twice: an **existing AU consumer brand** (the Aeko hat label,
pronounced "eh-co") already owns the exact word and `aeko.co` in-market, and the
"eko/eco" spelling drags the meaning toward *green/eco-friendly* rather than
*agentic economy*. Great phonetics, compromised ownership. A strong fallback
only if the Aeco TM path snags.

### #3 — **Aecho**

*"I Aecho'd a sparky."* The most *evocative* option — "echo" carries reach,
resonance, an answer coming back — and it keeps the AEC-→-Agentic-Economy stem.
But the meaning drifts to *sound/repetition*, the Amazon-Echo halo makes people
default-spell "Echo", and **Aecho.ai** (an AI company, no less) already holds the
exact word and the .ai. Distinctive, but the collision and the semantic pull are
both working against it.

### #4 — **Agenco**

*"I Agenco'd it"* — clunkier (3 syllables + the "'d"), but this is the candidate
that shouts **agent** loudest: "Agen-co" front-loads the agent-era signal the
bones ask for, and collision is the lowest of the leaders (one small Canadian
namesake). Held back by the "age-" front deadening the momentum and the extra
syllable blunting the pub-clip. A credible pick if the priority swings from
category-ownership to *agent* legibility.

### #5 — **Aecoa**

*"I Aecoa'd it?"* — the sentence itself exposes the weakness: nobody's sure how
to say or spell it on first hearing. Its one genuine advantage is a **clean,
buyable exact-match .com** (`aecoa.com` is parked/for-sale) and the lowest
collision profile in the set (a Portuguese training org, different everything).
But the soft "-oa" tail leaks the momentum out and the pronunciation is
ambiguous — it's the safe-domain option, not the strong-brand option.

## 4. Recommendation

**#1 — Aeco.** It is the founder-locked root, it tops the filter score, it clips
like a native Australian word, it verbs, and — decisively — it is the only
candidate whose four letters *are* the category ("Agentic ECOnomy" → AECO), so
the name does the positioning work for free. It's an ACTION word, not a trust
badge, exactly as the bones demand.

Operational notes to carry into the next stage:

1. **Pronunciation lock: "AY-koh."** Kills the "EE-ko / eco-friendly / echo"
   ambiguity and maximises the AU mouth-feel. Bake it into the wordmark/audio.
2. **Verb form: "Aeco'd" / "to Aeco."** The category verb is the whole game —
   design campaign copy around it ("Aeco it", "Aeco'd by lunch").
3. **Domain strategy:** exact `aeco.com`/`.com.au` are taken, so launch on
   `getaeco.com` / `useaeco.com` (both open at probe time); pursue `aeco.au`
   / `aeco.com.au` acquisition opportunistically, not on the critical path.
4. **Trademark:** run a proper Australian TM search vs **AECO Energy** in the
   relevant Nice classes (35 advertising/business, 42 tech) before commit —
   sector separation looks workable but must be confirmed, not assumed.
5. **Do not use "Aecom / Aecon / Aec"** in any treatment — each collides with a
   large incumbent.
