# AE Reference Map: Real-World Parallels

Grounding source: `.planning/brand/GROUNDING.md`.

AE is not inventing a new behavior from scratch. People already compare local providers, read answer-engine summaries with cited sources, trust official records, keep receipts, and use comparison surfaces to narrow choices. AE's brand opportunity is to combine those familiar patterns into one stricter object: **a sourced comparison that ends in one qualified inquiry receipt, not a booking/payment/dispatch promise**.

Territory shorthand:

- **A — Proof Ledger:** evidence, receipts, source trails, official records, auditability.
- **B — Living Handoff:** routing, qualified inquiry flow, guided next step, status handoff.
- **C — Real Marketplace:** human/commercial browsability, provider pride, editorial comparison.

Source notes: web verification was used for current-state facts where especially relevant: per-lead/commission models for service marketplaces; citation/source behavior for answer engines; official-record data patterns; receipt contents; comparison-surface fee transparency. Brand-visual references are strategic pattern reads from widely visible product/brand behavior, not claims about internal design systems.

## 1. Local-services marketplaces & inquiry / quote flows

These are AE's closest behavioral category, but also the category AE must most carefully **not become**. The incumbents teach demand capture, provider profile anatomy, quote collection, and local intent language. Their biggest brand risk is that many monetize provider attention through opaque lead-selling, shared leads, booking implication, review theater, or hidden true acquisition cost.

| Reference | Current-state pattern worth knowing | Borrow for AE | Reject for AE | Territory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Thumbtack** | Pros pay for leads when new customers contact them; lead prices vary by job value, pro supply, and market; pros can set lead prices. Sources: Thumbtack help on paying for leads and lead-price setting. | Make the customer request feel concrete before handoff: service category, location, timing, project details, and a clear “this is the inquiry being sent” preview. | Do not create a pay-per-lead auction where providers feel charged for low-intent or duplicated contacts; do not optimize for lead volume over inquiry quality. | **B** |
| **Angi / HomeAdvisor** | Ads-and-leads are core revenue; contractor lead fees are commonly discussed as pay-per-lead and sometimes shared among multiple pros. | Borrow the homeowner-friendly intake ladder: structured questions that make vague needs comparable. | Reject lead resale, multi-pro spray-and-pray, and any UX that implies the “best” provider is objectively chosen while the ranking is commercially shaped. | **B** |
| **TaskRabbit** | Taskers set hourly rates and receive their hourly rate/tips; clients see platform trust/support fees before confirming; platform is task completion oriented. | Borrow the legibility of a human service card: person/business, category, rate/price cue when supplied, availability boundaries, trust/support explanation. | Reject completed-task framing: AE must not imply scheduling, task acceptance, dispatch, or platform-managed fulfillment. | **C** |
| **Airtasker** | Taskers bid/offer on posted tasks; service fees are tiered and decrease with completed work; platform moves toward transaction completion. | Borrow the “job post as structured artifact” idea: the user's need becomes a reusable, reviewable packet. | Reject reverse-auction dynamics that push providers to underbid, and reject “accepted offer” language unless a business actually replies outside AE. | **B** |
| **hipages** | Australian tradies commonly pay subscriptions/lead credits; web research cites credits around A$30–A$80 depending on trade/job and shared leads sent to multiple tradies. | Borrow the AU-local-services category grammar: suburb/service/trade language, tradie trust signals, and practical inquiry detail. | Reject opaque credit expiry, hidden true cost per booked job, and the feeling that one consumer need is being sold to a pack. AE's “one qualified inquiry” must be a brand promise. | **B** |
| **Bark** | Professionals buy credits to respond to leads; lead credit cost varies by service and job size; Bark states no commission after the response fee. | Borrow the easy top-of-funnel breadth: many service categories can be expressed through one inquiry schema. | Reject “credits to unlock a lead” as the central trust object; the AE trust object is the evidence/inquiry receipt, not a paywall around customer contact. | **B** |
| **Checkatrade** | UK trade directory with membership plans and strong review/trust positioning; public pricing commentary notes monthly membership plus lead economics. | Borrow the vetted-directory feel: business profile as something an owner is proud to keep current. | Reject review theater, long-contract pressure, and “trusted” as a generic badge without a named standard or dated evidence. | **C** |
| **Houzz** | Home-improvement inspiration plus professional profiles; Houzz Pro extends into proposals, estimates, CRM, scheduling, invoices, and payments. | Borrow the provider-pride storefront: imagery, past work, specialties, service area, owner voice, and a profile that feels worth sharing. | Reject collapsing AE into an all-in-one vertical SaaS or project-management suite; AE routes the inquiry and records proof, not the full job lifecycle. | **C** |
| **Yelp** | Yelp Request a Quote lets users message businesses via category-specific questionnaires; Yelp also now promotes AI Assistant flows that can reserve/book/request quotes. | Borrow category-specific quote questions and business messaging language that feels familiar to consumers. | Reject the slide from inquiry into booking/reservation unless AE can truthfully support it; avoid star-rating dominance and engagement-ranked review theater. | **B** |
| **Google Business Profile / Google Local Services** | GBP makes businesses visible in Search/Maps; quote-request features have shifted over time, while Local Services Ads and competitive quote surfaces sit near business profiles. | Borrow the canonical local fact card: hours, service area, website, phone, location, reviews, photos, freshness. | Reject black-box local ranking as brand; AE must expose sources/freshness and avoid implying Google-scale coverage or verification. | **C** |

**Implication for AE:** local-service marketplaces prove the need is real, but they also define the trust scar. AE should feel like the antidote: **one inquiry, facts first, no hidden lead auction, no booking implication**.

## 2. Answer engines / agent-readable discovery with sources

These references teach how people now expect AI-assisted answers to behave: concise synthesis, visible sources, and a path from answer to action. AE should borrow source-forward summarization, but reject source-washing and generic AI confidence.

| Reference | Current-state pattern worth knowing | Borrow for AE | Reject for AE | Territory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Perplexity** | Citation-first answer engine: answers are organized around linked sources with inline citation markers and source panels. | Make every business comparison feel source-backed: visible evidence chips, inline citations, and a source drawer that shows where each claim came from. | Reject “citation as decoration.” AE must not cite stale, weak, or irrelevant pages just to make an answer look rigorous. | **A** |
| **ChatGPT Search** | Search-enabled responses can include inline citations users can hover/click; not every response is web-grounded. Source: OpenAI help on ChatGPT Search citations. | Borrow the conversational bridge from vague need to sourced answer: clarify, summarize, and route. | Reject hidden mode switching where users cannot tell whether facts are live-sourced, owner-supplied, or inferred. | **B** |
| **Google AI Overviews** | AI summaries synthesize multiple sources at the top of search and show linked source cards. | Borrow the compact “answer before list” layout for high-intent local needs: lead with the useful comparison, not a directory dump. | Reject SEO-style authority opacity and overconfident synthesis when source facts disagree or are incomplete. | **A** |
| **Arc Search / Browse for Me** | Builds a clean generated page from multiple sources and links the sources. | Borrow the “assembled brief” feeling: one custom page for a user's need, not a generic search-results page. | Reject disposable generated pages that obscure provenance or make the assistant look like the source of truth. | **B** |
| **Bing Copilot / web answers** | AI answers often combine web search with citations and suggested follow-up prompts. | Borrow follow-up affordances that narrow the need before handoff. | Reject endless chat loops; AE should converge on either “compare facts” or “send one qualified inquiry,” not keep the user in assistant theater. | **B** |

**Implication for AE:** answer engines give AE its discovery grammar, but AE needs a more material trust object than a cited paragraph: **the comparison must harden into an inquiry receipt**.

## 3. Registries, records & directories with an authoritative “record” feel

These references are about institutional confidence: stable identifiers, dated filings, public records, versioned metadata, and canonical profile pages. AE can borrow their record-like gravity without pretending to be a government registry.

| Reference | Current-state pattern worth knowing | Borrow for AE | Reject for AE | Territory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Companies House** | Official UK company register; free company information includes registered office, filing history, accounts, officers, charges, and business activity. | Borrow the record spine: stable entity page, dated filings/updates, source-of-record hierarchy, and “last filed/last checked” discipline. | Reject government-register cosplay. AE is a commercial trust layer, not a state authority; “verified” needs named standards. | **A** |
| **Crunchbase** | Company profiles aggregate funding, people, growth signals, news, and proprietary scores. | Borrow the profile-as-intelligence dossier: one page that gathers scattered business facts into a coherent record. | Reject opaque scoring and growth/status badges that users cannot audit or challenge. | **C** |
| **npm Registry** | Package pages expose names, versions, maintainers, publish dates, dependencies, readme, and install command; metadata is developer-readable. | Borrow machine-readable record design: stable IDs, version/freshness metadata, and a profile that humans and agents can both parse. | Reject developer-only coldness; AE must remain warm and local-commerce legible to nontechnical customers and owners. | **A** |
| **WHOIS / RDAP lookup** | Domain records expose registrar, dates, nameservers, and sometimes redacted owner/contact data due to privacy. | Borrow the idea of a lookup as a bounded truth object: “this is the record; these fields are private/redacted/unavailable.” | Reject leaking sensitive owner details or implying privacy redaction is suspicious. | **A** |
| **Clearbit / Breeze Intelligence** | B2B enrichment APIs provide firmographic/technographic attributes from many sources, with freshness/coverage claims. | Borrow enrichment as augmentation: AE can reconcile public business facts into a better owner record. | Reject invisible enrichment that creates facts owners cannot see, dispute, or correct. | **A** |
| **OpenCorporates** | Public company data search with jurisdiction/company-number emphasis. | Borrow jurisdiction/entity disambiguation: same business name can map to different legal/local entities. | Reject drowning local service users in legal data when the next step only needs practical provider facts. | **A** |

**Implication for AE:** AE should feel less like “search results” and more like **a living business record with a visible evidence spine**.

## 4. Receipts & tangible trust objects

These are AE's most important emotional references. A receipt makes an invisible action feel real. AE's receipt is not proof that the job will happen; it is proof that AE sent one bounded inquiry with specific content, source context, timestamp, and status.

| Reference | Current-state pattern worth knowing | Borrow for AE | Reject for AE | Territory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stripe receipts / paid invoices** | Stripe receipts can include itemized line items, discounts, taxes, browser-view links, and unique receipt numbers. | Borrow the durable receipt URL + unique ID + itemized detail: “what was sent, to whom, when, under which boundary.” | Reject payment-complete semantics; AE receipt must not look like money moved or a service was purchased. | **A** |
| **Stripe Dashboard payment timeline** | Payment objects expose status, events, metadata, logs, and dispute/refund trails to operators. | Borrow the operator audit timeline: every inquiry/proof event has a timestamp and actor/source. | Reject making internal logs public; customer-facing receipt should be calm, not an ops console. | **A** |
| **Square digital receipts** | Digital receipts show merchant information and transaction details such as item/quantity, tax, discounts, date, amount. | Borrow merchant + line-item clarity: business identity, inquiry details, status, and support/reference ID. | Reject POS checkout cues that imply AE sold the service or is the merchant of record. | **A** |
| **Wise transfer confirmation** | Completed transfers can produce PDF confirmations that work as receipts, with sender/recipient, amount/currency, status, timestamps, and references. | Borrow status language: created, sent, delivered/received/pending, plus references that can be audited later. | Reject money-transfer metaphors unless payment exists; AE is transferring an inquiry, not funds. | **B** |
| **Airline boarding pass / e-ticket** | E-tickets and boarding passes are compact permission/status objects with passenger, flight, time, gate/seat, barcode/reference. | Borrow the compact handoff card: one object you can save, reference, and show to the provider. | Reject “boarding pass means confirmed access” semantics; AE cannot imply the provider accepted the job. | **B** |
| **DocuSign Certificate of Completion** | Certificate of Completion records envelope details and recipient/signature events with times and identifying information. | Borrow the completion-certificate seriousness: event log, participants, timestamps, and tamper-evident feeling. | Reject legal finality for an inquiry; AE's certificate proves send/receipt state, not contractual acceptance. | **A** |
| **Amazon order confirmation** | Order confirmations center an order number and link to order summary/history; item detail may live behind the account page. | Borrow the familiar “confirmation number” UX and post-action retrieval path. | Reject marketplace order semantics: no cart, no purchased item, no delivery promise, no seller-of-record confusion. | **B** |
| **UPS / FedEx tracking page** | Tracking pages make status progression concrete: label created, picked up, in transit, delivered, exception. | Borrow the proof spine as a status progression: inquiry drafted → sent → business reply pending → reply received / expired. | Reject false precision; do not show fake route steps or time estimates AE cannot know. | **B** |

**Implication for AE:** the receipt is not an afterthought success toast. It is the **brand artifact**: the thing that makes AE feel strict, real, and commercially useful.

## NORTH STARS

### 1. **Perplexity — source-forward answer as default**

AE should emulate Perplexity's core expectation: a synthesized answer is not credible unless the sources sit close to the claim. AE's version should be stricter: citations are tied to business-published facts, freshness, and inquiry eligibility, not just web links.

- **Borrow:** Inline source/freshness chips and a source drawer for every meaningful provider claim.
- **Why:** AE lives or dies on proof being visible at the moment of comparison.
- **Territory:** **A — Proof Ledger**.

### 2. **Stripe receipts / dashboard objects — tangible commercial proof**

Stripe makes payments feel real through object IDs, receipt URLs, itemization, status, and event trails. AE needs the same seriousness for a non-payment event: the qualified inquiry.

- **Borrow:** Unique receipt ID, itemized payload, timestamp, participant identities, and status/event timeline.
- **Why:** The receipt is AE's moat made visible; it turns “we sent a message” into an auditable object.
- **Territory:** **A — Proof Ledger** with **B — Living Handoff**.

### 3. **Google Flights — fast, explainable comparison without owning inventory**

Google Flights is useful because it normalizes messy supplier data into filters, sort logic, and clear tradeoffs while usually sending the user elsewhere to complete the transaction.

- **Borrow:** Explainable comparison controls: best fit vs nearest vs freshest vs most complete; transparent “why this result” logic.
- **Why:** AE must compare local providers without pretending to control provider inventory, price, or availability.
- **Territory:** **C — Real Marketplace**.

### 4. **Companies House — record gravity and dated public facts**

Companies House shows how confidence comes from a stable record, dated filings, and named fields rather than marketing language.

- **Borrow:** Stable entity page, dated updates, source hierarchy, and record-like confidence.
- **Why:** AE provider pages should feel like living commercial records, not cards in a lead-gen carousel.
- **Territory:** **A — Proof Ledger**.

## Anti-references: what AE must not become

### 1. **Angi / hipages / Bark-style lead marketplace economics**

- **Do not become:** A hidden lead-selling machine where one customer request is monetized repeatedly and provider trust erodes.
- **AE counter-position:** One qualified inquiry, owner review, visible receipt, no spray-and-pray.

### 2. **Booking.com-style urgency and availability theater**

- **Do not become:** A marketplace UI that manufactures scarcity, implies availability, or nudges commitment before facts are confirmed.
- **AE counter-position:** Useful comparison, honest uncertainty, business confirms later.

### 3. **Generic AI answer UI / source-washing**

- **Do not become:** A polished AI summary with decorative citations, vague “verified” badges, and no durable proof object.
- **AE counter-position:** Every confident claim has a source/freshness basis; every handoff creates a receipt.

## Sharpest brand insight

**AE should not brand itself as “AI that finds local services.” It should brand itself as “the receipt-backed handoff layer”: the place where sourced business facts become one bounded, auditable inquiry.**

That single move distinguishes AE from three crowded categories at once:

- not a lead marketplace selling attention;
- not an answer engine summarizing the web;
- not a booking platform pretending availability is confirmed.

The brand should make the user feel: **I understand my options, I can see the proof, and the next step is real but not over-promised.**
