## Decision

Agentic Economy will add a points and rewards system after it proves the current
Treg-shaped x402 start line.

- Eligible provisional settlement earns **pending points**; final settlement and
  reconciliation release them as spendable points.
- Points aggregate in one account balance across eligible activity.
- The account owner redeems points for desirable **rewards**.
- Operations are earn events. They are not rewards and must not be described as
  “free useful Operations.”
- The currency has no brand or product name yet. Product language uses only
  `points`, `points balance`, `earn`, `boost`, `reward`, and `redeem`.

The chosen product shape is a coalition rewards marketplace. A narrow catalogue
is sufficient for the pilot, but the system preserves documented extension
points for future earn partners, reward partners, supplier-funded boosts, and
varied reward categories. The pilot does not implement a generalized coalition
framework.

## Problem Statement

Agentic Economy can become a useful transaction path for agents without giving
the economic principal a cumulative reason to prefer that path again. Atomic
transactions are too small for an ordinary per-transaction rebate to feel
meaningful. If each transaction rounds its incentive away, the reward is
invisible. If AE merely discounts later Operations, the mechanism is mistaken
for the merchandise.

The product must preserve the value created across many small transactions,
express that value as points, and let the user redeem those points for things
they actually want. The resulting system must increase productive routed
activity without issuing unfunded value or allowing rewards to distort
transaction quality.

## Demand Evidence

- Existing Australian programs prove that small earn events become meaningful
  when one balance aggregates across a network and can be redeemed for legible
  rewards.
- Everyday Rewards separates earning from reward choice: members can take money
  off shopping, save for Christmas, or convert to Qantas Points.
- Flybuys supports Flybuys Dollars, travel, a rewards store, gift cards, and
  conversion to Velocity Points.
- Qantas and Velocity extend redemption across flights, upgrades, hotels,
  products, gift cards, car hire, wine, and experiences.
- Qantas and Velocity demonstrate that partners buying points and funding bonus
  issuance can be a major B2B business, not merely a consumer discount expense.
- Agentic Economy has evidence that paid x402 supply exists. It has not yet
  proven an unfamiliar external agent can complete AE’s transaction loop or
  that points cause incremental repeat activity. Those remain explicit tests.

This is precedent for the mechanism, not direct proof of AE demand.

## Status Quo

- The active start line is one unfamiliar agent discovering, paying for, and
  receiving one independently supplied x402 Operation with durable readback and
  recovery.
- AE already has exact money events, settlement and refund semantics, budgets,
  durable invocation records, Qualified Use receipts, and supplier economics.
- Existing work is cloning the Treg product foundation while using Whop as the
  quality bar for machine-readable agent entry.
- No points liability, earn rules, reward catalogue, fulfilment flow, redemption
  authority, or campaign funding contract exists yet.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

There are two actors, and the product must not confuse them.

| Actor | Job |
|---|---|
| Agent | Route eligible activity, inspect the earn offer, receive machine-readable points and balance readback, and optionally suggest rewards. |
| Account owner | Own the economic account, choose reward goals, and authorize or perform redemption. |

The first wedge is:

1. A settled eligible transaction earns points under a published rule.
2. Every fractional remainder is preserved until it becomes a whole spendable
   point; transaction boundaries never erase value.
3. The agent receives the points earned and updated balance in its receipt.
4. The user sees progress toward a selected reward.
5. The user redeems from a deliberately small Australian catalogue.
6. A holdout cohort receives the same transaction prices without points.

The pilot catalogue should contain three emotionally distinct reward types:

- a broadly useful digital gift card;
- a high-perceived-value digital subscription or software benefit;
- an aspirational but operationally simple digital reward, such as a fixed-value
  travel, entertainment, or premium-brand gift card.

The catalogue is small to make the experiment legible, not because the long-term
product is a three-item store.

## Constraints

- Do not name or brand the points currency yet.
- Do not call an Operation a reward.
- Points are account-bound and non-transferable at launch.
- Points cannot be purchased, sold, withdrawn, or redeemed for cash at launch.
- Rewards may include third-party gift cards or benefits, but their fulfilment,
  expiry, exclusions, and availability must be explicit before redemption.
- Every issuance must trace to a funded pool: realised AE gross-profit
  allocation, supplier campaign money, realised procurement savings, or a
  capped promotional budget.
- A supplier boost may change the effective incentive but cannot override the
  quality, privacy, jurisdiction, or reliability policy used to route activity.
- Points remain a liability until redemption, expiry, or valid reversal.
- Refunds and transaction reversals reverse the points originally issued for
  that event, including their preserved remainder.
- The pilot must not delay or widen the live x402 start-line proof.
- The implemented Australian terms and money flow require fixed-scope legal and
  accounting review before public launch.

## Premises

1. The agent is the earning interface; the economic principal is the redemption
   owner.
2. The product is a points balance plus desirable reward choice, not cashback
   disguised as points and not discounted Operations.
3. Exact aggregation makes atomic activity economically perceptible.
4. Base earning establishes the habit; supplier-funded boosts create the B2B
   monetization surface.
5. Partner issuance, catalogue spread, negotiated fulfilment cost, unredeemed
   balance economics, and incremental routed activity matter more than a
   mandatory per-use rake. Accounting breakage is not recognized without an
   approved policy and adequate evidence.
6. The strongest long-term moat is concentrated, measurable agent-driven demand.
   Points make that demand sticky; the points balance is not itself the moat.
7. The mechanism fails if points do not create incremental productive activity
   after reward cost, abuse, reversals, and liability are included.

## Approaches Considered

- **Fixed-value rewards:** fastest pilot, but too close to cashback and too weak
  as the long-term partner platform.
- **Coalition rewards marketplace:** selected because it joins automatic earning,
  desirable redemption, partner-funded boosts, and catalogue economics.
- **Transfer currency:** preserve as a later redemption option once AE has enough
  demand to negotiate with established programs without surrendering the user
  relationship.

## Recommended Approach

### 1. Points ledger

The ledger is exact, append-only, and attributable. Entries do not change state.
They are immutable events from which balance buckets are derived.

The pilot event types are:

```text
earn_pending -> earn_release -> reserve -> capture
     |               |            |
     +-> reverse     +-> reverse  +-> release

remainder_allocate records sub-point carry crossing a point-micro boundary.
operator_adjustment is exceptional, separately authorized, and audited.
expire is reserved for a later policy; pilot points do not expire.
```

Every event records:

- account and economic-owner reference;
- source event and prior event reference where applicable;
- earn rule and rule version;
- raw qualifying amount or qualifying event;
- source-level rational entitlement numerator, denominator, allocated
  point-micros, and residual numerator;
- signed point-micros changed by the event;
- funding source, funding-pool reference, and pool reservation;
- idempotency key;
- campaign, supplier, and work references where relevant;
- recorded-at and effective-at timestamps;
- actor, authority, and reason code.

One point equals 1,000,000 point-micros. All arithmetic uses signed integers,
never floating point. Earn rules store a rational numerator and denominator.
The calculation floors only at the point-micro boundary and carries any smaller
rational remainder against the account and rule version.

Illustrative arithmetic, not the pilot earn-rate decision:

```text
published rate                      4 points per A$1
eligible settled amount             A$0.023700
raw earn                            0.094800 points
ledger event                        94,800 point-micros
display                             0 whole points + 9.48% to the next point
```

The derived balance is split into `pending`, `available`, `reserved`, `spent`,
and `deficit`. Conservation must hold for every account and funding pool. A
reservation moves value from available to reserved; capture moves it from
reserved to spent; release restores it to available.

Account equations use the signed sum of immutable events:

```text
spendable balance = max(0, available - deficit)
pending progress  = pending
goal distance     = max(0, reward price - spendable balance)
lifetime earned   = sum of positive earn_pending point-micros
lifetime reversed = absolute sum of reverse point-micros
net earned        = lifetime earned - lifetime reversed
```

`pending`, `available`, `reserved`, and `spent` are mutually exclusive buckets;
moving points between them does not create earnings. Positive or negative
`operator_adjustment` events are reported separately and never folded into
`lifetime earned`.

Receipts and UI never use the ambiguous term `total balance`. They show
`spendable balance`, `pending progress`, and `goal distance` separately.

Point conservation and monetary funding are separate ledgers. Point-micros never
appear in an AUD equation. The point ledger maintains:

```text
outstanding point-micros = pending + available + reserved
```

The monetary treasury ledger uses integer AUD micros and a versioned,
conservative `liabilityValue(pointMicros, valuationVersion)` function:

```text
funded AUD micros = initial funding + approved replenishments
encumbered AUD micros = liabilityValue(outstanding point-micros)
                      + fulfilment encumbrance
remaining AUD micros = funded - encumbered - realised reward cost
                     - incremental program loss
remaining AUD micros must never be negative
```

`incremental program loss` contains only loss not already included in realised
reward cost. Revaluation appends a `liability_revalue` treasury event; it never
rewrites prior values. Other immutable treasury events include `fund`,
`fulfilment_encumber`, `fulfilment_release`, `reward_cost_realise`, and
`program_loss_realise`. Every treasury event carries its currency, valuation
version where relevant, source reference, and idempotency key.

`earn_pending` increases outstanding point liability. `earn_release` moves
pending to available without changing it. `reverse` reduces outstanding point
liability unless the points have been spent, in which case it creates account
deficit and incremental program loss within the approved reserve. `reserve`
moves available to reserved. Partner submission does not extinguish point
liability. If submission makes partner cost unavoidable, it also creates a
monetary fulfilment encumbrance. `capture` occurs only when the reward becomes
`fulfilled`; it moves reserved points to spent while the partner cost moves from
fulfilment encumbrance to realised reward cost. `delivery_confirmed` closes the
user-visible order but does not change points again.

Partial refunds reverse the exact point-micros attributable to the refunded
share using the original rule version. If the relevant points have already been
redeemed, the pilot records a bounded deficit rather than clawing back a
delivered reward. Future earnings first clear the deficit. A risk-based release
delay and program loss reserve cap this exposure. Suspected abuse suspends new
redemptions pending review.

Remainder attribution is deterministic and auditable. Each account, rule
version, and earn source retains its exact rational entitlement and unallocated
residual. When combined residuals for the same account and rule version cross a
point-micro boundary, the ledger appends `remainder_allocate`. That event assigns
the new point-micro to the source whose contribution crossed the boundary and
references every contributing source and rational amount. Example:

```text
source A -> 600,000 point-micros + 0.4 point-micro residual
source B -> 500,000 point-micros + 0.7 point-micro residual
B crosses the boundary -> allocate 1 point-micro to B, citing A 0.4 + B 0.6
remaining residual -> B 0.1 point-micro
```

A reversal follows those allocation references. If the allocated point-micro is
unspent, compensating immutable events remove it and recompute the surviving
sources' residuals under the pinned rule version. If it has been spent, the
normal bounded-deficit and incremental-loss policy applies. No residual is
silently borrowed, discarded, or reassigned without a ledger event.

`operator_adjustment` requires an enumerated reason and compensating funding-pool
event. It cannot erase spent history or silently remove a deficit. Adjustments
above the configured threshold require two distinct operator approvals and are
bounded by the same liability ceiling as ordinary issuance.

The user-facing balance shows available whole points and honest progress toward
the next whole point. A transaction worth less than one point still contributes.

### 2. Earn engine

The initial earn rules are:

- a modest base rate for eligible provisionally settled activity, held pending
  through its configured refund and reconciliation window, then released;
- supplier-funded point boosts for declared acquisition, continuation, or return
  events;
- temporary AE promotions with explicit budgets and end dates;
- no points when the qualifying event is unfunded, unsettled, duplicated,
  self-dealing, reversed, or outside policy.

For this design, realised gross profit means transaction revenue less supplier
settlement, gateway and payment expense, variable inference or fulfilment cost,
and the configured refund reserve after reconciliation. The pilot base pool is a
fixed, capped marketing allocation that may be replenished only from measured
realised gross profit. Issuance never depends on hoped-for future margin.

Each funding pool has a currency, funded amount, point-liability valuation,
reserved amount, issued amount, remaining amount, start and end dates, and an
exhaustion policy. Qualification atomically reserves both point liability and
campaign budget using the source-event idempotency key. Concurrent attempts
cannot overspend the pool. An exhausted pool returns `earn_unavailable` before
the activity where pre-disclosure is required; it never silently promises points.

When points are disclosed before activity, AE first creates a short-lived earn
quote that reserves the maximum point liability against the funding pool. The
quote pins the earn-rule version, maximum qualifying amount, point cap, expiry,
and activity idempotency key. Provisional settlement captures the required
portion into `earn_pending`; unused reservation is released. Expired, cancelled,
or failed activity releases the whole quote. Activity without a valid quote may
still qualify only under a rule that explicitly does not promise points in
advance.

The base rate and point economics remain configurable until reward costs and
transaction distributions are measured. Marketing may advertise a published
earn rule, but AE must not promise an economically unsupported universal return.

### 3. Rewards catalogue

Each reward is a versioned offer with:

- reward title, image, description, category, and supplier;
- points price;
- AE’s fulfilment cost and commercial funding source;
- inventory or availability;
- jurisdiction, account, age, or eligibility constraints;
- delivery method and expected delivery time;
- expiry and material exclusions;
- refund, replacement, and failed-fulfilment policy;
- start and end dates.

The long-term catalogue may include gift cards, products, subscriptions,
software benefits, travel, experiences, upgrades, partner perks, donations, and
featured rewards. The pilot is deliberately narrower: digitally delivered,
fixed-cost rewards with simple replacement terms. Physical goods, travel
bookings, variable inventory, experiences, and points-plus-pay are later product
lines with separate payment, support, refund, and legal contracts. Operations
are explicitly excluded as the defining reward category.

A pinned goal references a reward revision. If that revision expires, changes
price, or loses inventory, the goal becomes `unavailable` and the user sees
replacement options; AE never silently swaps the reward. A redemption reserves
the exact inspected revision and price.

### 4. Redemption engine

Redemption is a separately authorized, idempotent state machine:

```text
selected
  -> reserved
  -> submitted
       |-> retrying -> submitted
       |-> fulfilled -> delivery_confirmed
       |-> failed -> cost_reversal_confirmed -> points_and_encumbrance_released
       |-> cancelled -> cost_reversal_confirmed -> points_and_encumbrance_released
       |-> manual_reconciliation -> fulfilled | failed
```

The `rewards.redeem` idempotency key covers the account, reward revision, points
price, and authority. Points reservation and redemption creation are atomic.
Retries reuse the same redemption reference. On submission, AE creates a
monetary fulfilment encumbrance whenever the partner order is chargeable or
non-cancellable. A failure or valid cancellation releases points and that
encumbrance only after the partner confirms non-delivery and a credit or cost
reversal. Otherwise both remain encumbered in `manual_reconciliation`. A timeout
also becomes visible `manual_reconciliation`; it never creates a second order
automatically.

AE owns first-line user support and point restoration. The reward partner owns
delivery under its contracted service level and replacement terms. The partner
receives only the minimum fulfilment data required after the user confirms
redemption. Where possible, the partner delivers a digital code to AE and AE
delivers it to the user without sharing personal data.

An agent may query the balance, catalogue, reward goal, and redemption status.
Human confirmation is the default for redemption. Delegated redemption requires
an explicit grant containing account, agent principal, permitted categories,
per-redemption limit, rolling-period limit, expiry, and confirmation-escalation
threshold. Grants are revocable immediately, every use is audited, and stale or
replayed authority is rejected.

Revocation blocks new reservations immediately and cancels reserved-but-not-yet-
submitted redemptions. Once a partner order is submitted, revocation cannot
pretend the external action is reversible: AE requests cancellation only when
the partner contract supports it, otherwise the order completes under the grant
that was valid at submission. The user sees this boundary when granting or
revoking authority.

One economic owner may bind multiple agents to the same account. Account
creation, owner verification, agent binding, related-account linkage, and
supplier-related-party declarations are platform identity responsibilities.
Earning excludes owner/supplier self-dealing, duplicate principals, linked
accounts acting as independent demand, and circular funded activity.

### 5. Reward goals and progress

The felt product is progress toward something selected by the user.

- Users can pin a reward or category goal.
- Receipts show points earned, spendable balance, pending progress, and distance
  to the goal.
- Agents receive the same fields in structured output and can mention meaningful
  progress without interrupting every transaction.
- Boosts show the exact qualifying behavior and bonus before the activity.
- No random payout, paid chance, mystery value, or manufactured urgency is used.

### 6. Supplier and reward-partner platform

Earn partners buy measurable demand by funding point boosts. Reward partners
provide catalogue inventory, negotiated fulfilment cost, sponsorship, or
exclusive value. AE earns through a mix of:

- supplier campaign and platform fees;
- the spread between the economic cost of points issued and rewards fulfilled;
- negotiated reward inventory and partner funding;
- breakage recognized only after an approved accounting policy and adequate
  evidence exist;
- increased transaction gross profit caused by incremental routed activity.

AE does not depend on taking an arbitrary rake from every transaction. It must
still measure total reward cost against incremental gross profit.

### 7. Agent-first surfaces

The machine surface should support:

- `points.balance`;
- `points.activity`;
- `points.earning_options`;
- `points.earn_quote.create`;
- `rewards.search`;
- `rewards.inspect`;
- `rewards.goal.get` and `rewards.goal.set`;
- `rewards.redeem` behind explicit authority;
- `rewards.redemption_status`.

Pilot contracts share `contractVersion`, `accountRef`, `authorityRef`,
`idempotencyKey` where mutating, and structured error codes.

- `points.balance` returns point-unit version plus pending, available, reserved,
  spent, and deficit point-micros as decimal strings and display whole points.
- `points.activity` is cursor-paginated and returns immutable ledger events.
- `points.earning_options` is read-only. It returns qualifying rules, formulas,
  estimated points, and current availability without reserving liability.
- `points.earn_quote.create` is mutating. It pins a rule version and atomically
  reserves the maximum point liability for pre-disclosed eligible activity.
- `rewards.search` returns only currently eligible reward revisions and supports
  category and maximum-point filters.
- `rewards.inspect` returns the pinned revision, exact price, delivery terms,
  exclusions, and availability.
- `rewards.redeem` requires reward reference, revision, expected point price,
  idempotency key, and authority reference; it returns redemption reference,
  reserved point-micros, and current status.
- `rewards.redemption_status` returns the state, attempt count, last transition,
  delivery status, and user action required.

Compact pilot wire contracts:

```text
PointMicros = signed integer encoded as a decimal string
Cursor = opaque string; omitted means first page; null nextCursor means end

points.balance({accountRef})
  -> {contractVersion, asOf, pending, spendable, reserved, spent, deficit,
      displayWholePoints}

points.activity({accountRef, cursor?, limit?})
  -> {events[], nextCursor: Cursor|null}; limit defaults to 50 and is capped

points.earning_options({accountRef, activityType, qualifyingAmount?, currency?})
  -> {options: [{ruleRef, ruleVersion, fundingSource,
      estimatedPointMicros|null, formula|null, unavailableReason|null}]}

points.earn_quote.create({accountRef, activityType, maximumQualifyingAmount?,
                          currency?, authorityRef, idempotencyKey})
  -> {earnQuoteRef, ruleRef, ruleVersion, reservedMaximumPointMicros,
      expiresAt}

rewards.search({accountRef, category?, maximumPointMicros?, cursor?, limit?})
  -> {rewardSummaries[], nextCursor: Cursor|null}

rewards.inspect({accountRef, rewardRef, rewardRevision})
  -> {reward, exactPointPrice, available, deliveryTerms, exclusions, expiresAt}

rewards.goal.get({accountRef})
  -> {goal: {rewardRef, rewardRevision, status, pointPrice, goalDistance}|null}

rewards.goal.set({accountRef, rewardRef|null, rewardRevision?, authorityRef,
                  idempotencyKey})
  -> {goal|null}; rewardRef=null clears the goal

rewards.redeem({accountRef, rewardRef, rewardRevision, expectedPointPrice,
                authorityRef, idempotencyKey})
  -> {redemptionRef, status, reservedPointMicros, userActionRequired|null}

rewards.redemption_status({accountRef, redemptionRef})
  -> {status, attemptCount, lastTransitionAt, deliveryStatus|null,
      userActionRequired|null}
```

All absent optional inputs are omitted rather than sent as empty strings. Every
mutating request authenticates the principal, checks account ownership and the
named authority, and deduplicates by idempotency key. A replay with identical
input returns the original result; a replay with different input returns
`idempotency_conflict`. Contract additions are backward-compatible within a
version; breaking field or semantic changes require a new contract version.

Named pilot errors include `account_not_authorized`, `insufficient_points`,
`points_deficit`, `reward_unavailable`, `reward_revision_stale`,
`reward_price_changed`, `funding_pool_exhausted`, `authority_expired`,
`redemption_limit_exceeded`, `idempotency_conflict`, `fulfilment_retrying`, and
`manual_reconciliation_required`. No error is represented as an empty success.

The agent sees point consequences before eligible activity and receives exact
readback after settlement. The human user receives a familiar rewards catalogue
and redemption experience. Agent-first does not mean forcing a human rewards
store into the invocation response.

## First Pilot

Run only after the x402 start line is proven.

### Gate 1: economic feasibility before implementation

Gate 1 cannot pass until the dual point/AUD conservation model, valuation
version, fulfilment encumbrance, and revaluation rules are defined and
sensitivity-tested.

Use observed AE transaction distributions and real reward-partner terms to model:

- eligible transactions and gross profit per active account;
- time and transaction count to each candidate reward threshold;
- wholesale reward cost, delivery cost, loss reserve, and liability ceiling;
- base earn pool and supplier-boost contribution;
- break-even incremental activity under low, expected, and high redemption;
- sensitivity to refunds, reward abuse, concentration, and partner failure.

Do not build the points pilot if a desirable fixed-cost digital reward cannot be
reached within the chosen cohort’s credible activity horizon while remaining
inside the funded liability ceiling.

### Gate 2: closed-pilot readiness

Before admitting participants, secure one fulfilment contract, three fixed-cost
digital rewards, fulfilment and reversal test results, support ownership, and an
approved loss-reserve cap. Implement account-linkage checks, redemption
suspension, manual review, per-account and program loss limits, and audit
monitoring. Obtain preliminary Australian legal, privacy, tax, and accounting
sign-off for closed-pilot terms, personal-data flow, gift-card fulfilment, and
points-liability treatment.

### Gate 3: experiment

1. Choose one fungible transaction category and at least three independent
   suppliers.
2. Establish one base earn rule funded from a bounded AE marketing allocation
   tied to realised gross profit.
3. Add one supplier-funded boost with a fixed budget and qualification rule.
4. Offer three digitally delivered, fixed-cost reward types through one real
   Australian fulfilment path.
5. Let users select a reward goal and show machine-readable progress.
6. Define eligibility from a pre-randomization lookback or a predetermined
   account criterion. Then randomize at the economic-account level before the
   first eligible pilot transaction. Treatment receives points; holdout receives
   identical prices, suppliers, quality policy, and service without points.
7. Exclude linked accounts, staff, suppliers, and ineligible accounts before
   assignment. Analyze every eligible randomized account on an intent-to-treat
   basis, including accounts with zero post-assignment activity. Prevent
   cross-cohort balance or campaign sharing.
8. Simulate the analysis window from pre-pilot account activity, the selected
   earn rate, and the reward threshold; lock its duration before randomization.
   Calculate required account and opportunity counts from the baseline activity
   rate and the minimum lift worth funding before launch.
9. Pre-register the estimator, one-sided or two-sided confidence requirement,
   minimum economically worthwhile lift, treatment of zero-activity accounts,
   attrition policy, fixed stopping rule, and correction for any repeated looks.
   Keep the primary metric fixed: incremental completed eligible transactions
   per randomized account, net of reversals.
10. Pass only when the lower confidence bound on incremental net gross profit
   per randomized account, after reward cost, delivery cost, program loss, and
   incremental support cost, is above zero and the point estimate exceeds the
   predeclared minimum worthwhile lift. Reward attainment and redemption are
   mechanism measures, not substitutes for this outcome.
11. After the primary window, run a predeclared reduced-subsidy or no-new-base-
   points follow-up window to measure whether incremental activity persists.

### Gate 4: public launch

Public availability requires closed-pilot economics, fulfilment reliability,
abuse controls, liability accounting, and Australian legal review to pass. No
public launch occurs merely because the software works.

## Success Criteria

- Incremental eligible transaction frequency and gross profit versus holdout.
- Percentage of eligible accounts reaching the first redeemable reward.
- Median time and transaction count to first redemption.
- Points issued, pending, available, reserved, reversed, deficit, and redeemed.
- Reward selection, goal-setting, redemption, and fulfilment completion rates.
- Reward cost per incrementally retained or reactivated account.
- Supplier campaign cost per qualified acquisition, continuation, and organic
  return.
- Reward-partner fulfilment cost, margin, failure rate, and delivery latency.
- Outstanding points liability, unredeemed balance, and inactivity distribution.
- Related-account, self-dealing, refund, duplication, and reward-mining rates.
- Concentration of earning, redemption, suppliers, and reward partners.

The pilot passes only if points create incremental productive activity after
reward cost and abuse, not merely high point issuance or catalogue browsing.

## Kill Conditions

- Users cannot reach a desirable reward within a credible usage horizon.
- Redemptions concentrate in rewards whose fulfilment cost makes the program
  structurally unprofitable.
- Activity lift disappears during the predeclared reduced-subsidy or no-new-
  base-points follow-up window.
- Suppliers will fund boosts only in exchange for undisclosed routing priority.
- Reward mining, self-dealing, refund abuse, or duplicated activity consumes the
  available margin.
- The account owner does not understand who earned the points, what they can buy,
  or why a balance changed.
- Fulfilment failures cannot restore points quickly and visibly.
- The economic feasibility model requires point funding or a reward threshold
  that makes the program feel fake or unreachable.

## Explicitly Not in Scope

- Naming or branding the points currency.
- Treating Operations as rewards.
- Cash withdrawal, a stored-value wallet, or a customer-controlled crypto asset.
- Tradable or transferable points.
- User-funded point purchases or top-ups.
- Pooled community funds, voting, governance, or token economics.
- Random drops or paid-chance mechanics.
- Intent auctions, route royalties, or a general workflow engine in the points
  pilot.
- Bulk point sales or external point conversion before partner economics and
  Australian legal treatment are established.
- Points expiry during the pilot.
- Points-plus-pay, physical goods, travel bookings, variable inventory, and
  experience fulfilment during the pilot.
- A generalized multi-partner framework beyond one earn-rule interface, one
  funding-pool boundary, and one reward-fulfilment adapter. Future extension
  points are documented, not prebuilt.

## Open Questions

1. Which Australian reward fulfilment partner can provide the first real
   catalogue, wholesale cost, delivery API, reversals, and support terms?
2. What point earn rate and first redemption threshold are reachable under the
   observed atomic transaction distribution without exceeding the funding cap?
3. Which three rewards produce sufficiently different motivation to test utility,
   aspiration, and perceived generosity?
4. After the no-expiry pilot, would an inactivity-based expiry rule create enough
   economic value to justify notice, fairness, and accounting complexity?
5. What redemption categories, values, and frequencies may an agent execute
   under delegated authority without human confirmation?
6. How will outstanding points and reward inventory be recognized and forecast
   in AE’s accounts?

## Dependencies

- Completion of the unfamiliar-agent x402 start-line transaction.
- Existing exact money, settlement, refund, budget, and Qualified Use primitives.
- A real Australian reward fulfilment path with commercial and failure terms.
- Abuse and related-account controls proportionate to reward value.
- Australian legal and accounting review of the implemented earn, liability,
  expiry, redemption, gift-card, and data-use flows.

Dependency sequence:

## Distribution Plan

The points system is part of the hosted Agentic Economy service. Agents access
earning, balances, goals, catalogue search, and authorized redemption through
the existing MCP, HTTP, CLI, `SKILL.md`, and machine-documentation entry paths.
Users access reward selection, account controls, and human-confirmed redemption
through the account interface.

There is one canonical versioned service contract. HTTP is its authoritative
wire form; MCP and CLI are thin adapters, while `SKILL.md` and machine
documentation are generated descriptions of the same contract. The pilot must
prove parity only through the minimum HTTP plus agent surface already used by
the x402 start line. Other wrappers may expose the feature only after generated
contract checks pass; they are not independent implementations.

## The Assignment

Obtain real commercial terms from one Australian reward catalogue or fulfilment
provider for three pilot rewards: wholesale cost, minimum volume, API or delivery
method, settlement timing, reversals, expiry, support responsibility, and
jurisdiction limits. Use those terms to calculate the first credible points earn
rate and redemption threshold. Do not design the pilot around retail reward
prices or imagined partner discounts.

## What I Noticed About How You Think

- You rejected “free useful Operations” immediately because the mechanism had
  been mistaken for the thing a person wants.
- You said “people like free shit,” which kept the design focused on incentives
  with felt value instead of internal platform credits.
- You stopped the naming exercise before the product shape was settled: “don’t
  name it for now, just call them points.”
- You kept the Treg clone as the foundation while insisting the business must
  compound above per-use rake economics.
