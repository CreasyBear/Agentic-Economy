## Decision

Aecon will explore a managed-agent service for businesses it onboards. The first
offer is not a self-serve agent builder or a general-purpose runtime. Aecon and
the customer define one recurring business outcome, Aecon configures and operates
one agent for it, and the customer pays for the managed service and its successful
work.

The service will preserve a clean boundary for externally hosted agents from the
start. An agent may run on Aecon-selected infrastructure or in a customer-selected
harness, but its Aecon identity, authority, connections, commercial activity and
evidence remain portable and independently governed.

Working product name: **Aecon Managed Agents**. This is descriptive language, not
a committed brand.

## Problem Statement

Businesses can prototype agents quickly but still struggle to operate them as
accountable workers. A useful production agent needs a stable identity, scoped
access to business systems, durable work, approvals, recovery, monitoring,
cost controls, evidence and a human escalation path. Today those responsibilities
are assembled from an agent framework, cloud runtime, connector product, secrets
store, workflow engine, evaluation stack and internal support process.

Aecon already intends to own many of the difficult control and transaction facts:
principals, accounts, delegated authority, connections, budgets, Orders,
Invocations, evidence, reconciliation and settlement. A managed-agent service can
turn those primitives into an outcome a business buys while generating a real,
installed source of demand for Aecon Operations.

The danger is becoming an undifferentiated agent-hosting platform or consultancy.
The product therefore starts with one repeatable managed outcome and treats the
runtime as a replaceable implementation detail.

## Demand Evidence

There is strong category evidence that businesses pay for managed agent outcomes,
but no direct evidence yet that they will buy this service from Aecon.

- OpenAI Presence is a managed enterprise service for deploying and continuously
  improving governed agents. Its process begins with a business outcome, then
  integrates systems, policies, testing, human escalation and controlled rollout.
  It is limited-GA and not self-serve.
  [OpenAI Presence](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001405)
- Intercom prices Fin by successful outcomes rather than attempts. Its current
  documentation lists $0.99 support resolutions and procedure handoffs, no charge
  for failed procedures, and higher prices for qualified sales outcomes.
  [Fin outcomes](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8205718-fin-ai-agent-outcomes)
- Vercel says it operates more than one hundred internal agents and has productized
  the recurring infrastructure as eve, Workflows, Sandbox and Connect. This proves
  the operational pattern exists, while also showing that generic runtime and
  connector infrastructure will be heavily competed.
  [Introducing eve](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-eve)
- Cloudflare now positions Durable Objects as a hosted agent substrate with durable
  identity, local SQL state, scheduling, recoverable execution, MCP, browser and
  optional sandbox tools.
  [Cloudflare Agents](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/)
- AWS AgentCore and Microsoft Foundry both separate agent code from managed runtime,
  identity, isolation and external tool access. Microsoft additionally gives hosted
  agents dedicated identities and inbound invocation, A2A and MCP endpoints.
  [AgentCore Runtime](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/harness-vs-runtime.html)
  · [Foundry hosted agents](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/hosted-agents)

This evidence supports the market category, not Aecon's initial workflow, customer
or willingness to pay. No managed-agent product work should outrun a paid design
partner.

## Status Quo

The assumed customer currently does some combination of the following:

1. A founder, operations lead or engineer prototypes an agent in a framework such
   as eve, AI SDK, Cloudflare Agents, LangGraph or a coding-agent harness.
2. The team separately provisions Slack, email, API or webhook entry points.
3. OAuth tokens and API keys live in environment variables or a connector vendor.
4. Prompts, business rules, tools and approval logic are mixed into one deployment.
5. Cron jobs, retries, state and human follow-up are implemented ad hoc.
6. Logs show model and tool calls but do not reconstruct who authorized a
   consequential action, under which account, with which terms and budget.
7. When the agent fails, staff inspect traces, manually repair work and decide
   whether a provider action or charge happened.

The cost is engineering time, operational interruption, security review, connector
maintenance, uncertain spend and a business owner who cannot tell whether the agent
is producing reliable work. These are informed assumptions until customer interviews
attach names, hours and dollars to them.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

The initial buyer is the accountable owner of a repeated operational workflow at a
20–200 person digital business that lacks an internal agent-platform team. The likely
title is Head of Operations, Customer Operations, Revenue Operations or Marketing
Operations. The exact person and workflow remain unproven.

The first sellable wedge is deliberately narrow:

- one business;
- one agent;
- one recurring workflow with an objective completion boundary;
- two to four connected systems;
- one primary channel, preferably Slack, email or an existing API;
- explicit approval points for irreversible or high-cost actions;
- a named human exception owner;
- a thirty-day supervised production pilot;
- setup fee plus a fixed pilot fee and pass-through usage.

Suitable first workflows are frequent, bounded, reversible and measurable. Examples
include research-to-brief, inbound support triage, lead enrichment and routing, content
operations, or vendor/data procurement. Aecon should not choose the category from this
document; the first customer willing to pay for a qualifying workflow chooses it.

## Product Boundary

Aecon Managed Agents is a managed outcome layer above the existing transaction kernel.

```text
ManagedServiceAgreement
        |
AgentAssignment  -- desired outcome, policy, SLA, escalation, price
        |
AgentTask / Episode
        |
Orders + Invocations + external actions
        |
TaskOutcome + evidence + service billing
```

- **ManagedServiceAgreement** records the customer, operator, service scope,
  commercial terms, support boundary, data handling, exit terms and liability.
- **AgentAssignment** is the durable mandate for one agent: outcome definition,
  allowed work, schedules, channels, budgets, policies, success tests and escalation.
- **AgentTask** is one bounded episode of work, whether triggered by a human, another
  agent, a webhook or a schedule.
- Existing **Operation → Offer → Resolution → Quote → Order → Invocation → Settlement**
  remains the atomic commercial ladder for capabilities the managed agent buys or
  sells. The managed agent itself is not automatically an Operation.
- If the managed agent is later exposed as a paid callable service, its declared
  capability may be published as an Operation and each external call follows the
  existing Quote, Order and Invocation contracts.

This addition exposes a real omission in the current maturity model without invalidating
it: long-lived mandates and managed service relationships sit above atomic commerce.

## Core Product Requirements

### 1. Business outcome contract

Every deployment begins with a written assignment containing:

- the job to be done and an objective completion boundary;
- accepted inputs, outputs and delivery channel;
- allowed, approval-required and forbidden actions;
- data sources, retention and permitted data use;
- cost, rate, concurrency and time limits;
- human escalation conditions and response expectations;
- acceptance tests, quality sampling and rollback conditions;
- service price, pass-through costs and failed-work treatment.

The service must refuse assignments whose outcome cannot be measured or whose failure
cannot be bounded during the pilot.

### 2. First-class identity and ownership

- The managed agent has a stable `Principal` independent of its runtime, model,
  credential or channel.
- The customer organization owns the active Account and remains the payer and
  beneficial owner by default.
- The agent operates through a scoped membership or Grant; it never impersonates the
  customer or an Aecon employee.
- Aecon is recorded as service operator. Every staff intervention preserves the
  original actor and separately records the operator, reason and authority.
- The design must also permit an autonomous agent to own its own Account directly.

### 3. Runtime independence

The product contract depends on an `AgentRuntime` port rather than one vendor.

Candidate adapters include Vercel eve/WorkflowAgent, a Cloudflare Durable Object agent,
AWS AgentCore, Microsoft Foundry, or a customer-hosted harness. The pilot may use the
runtime that reaches production fastest within the existing Vercel/Convex estate. A
Cloudflare Durable Object prototype is warranted for lightweight persistent agents,
but it is not a platform commitment.

A full sandbox is an escalation tier, not the default. Most managed agents should use
a small declared capability surface: model calls, selected MCP servers, HTTP tools,
web fetch, a browser API and bounded file/artifact storage. Arbitrary code execution,
untrusted repositories or operating-system dependencies require isolated sandboxed
compute with separate policy and pricing.

Convex remains the canonical writable source of truth for Aecon identity, authority,
commercial state and consequential evidence. Runtime-local state may optimize execution
but cannot become the only copy of business truth.

### 4. Recovery reconstructs authority

Persistence preserves continuity, not permission.

When an agent wakes, resumes or migrates, Aecon reconstructs:

```text
stable principal
+ accepted durable checkpoint
+ current capability snapshot
+ current authority and connection generations
+ current policy and assignment revision
```

The runtime must not deserialize an old credential, cached OAuth token or prior authority
decision and silently continue. Before every consequential step it re-resolves account
context, authority expiry, connection state, budget and current assignment policy using
server time. Admitted work retains its pinned commercial terms, but new consequences
require current authority.

### 5. Connections and secrets

- Connections are account resources, private by default and shared through explicit
  narrowing Grants.
- Provider tokens are retrieved just in time and never placed in prompts, checkpoints,
  logs or agent-visible state.
- The model sees tool descriptions and results, not raw connection URLs or secrets.
- Revocation stops new use immediately; ambiguous in-flight effects enter reconciliation.
- Prompt injection from documents, messages, MCP descriptions and tool results is
  treated as untrusted input, not authority.

Vercel Connect demonstrates the expected developer experience—short-lived runtime
credentials, webhook verification and per-installation authorization—while Aecon's
connection model must remain provider-neutral.
[Vercel Connect](https://vercel.com/connect)

### 6. Durable task lifecycle

Each task has a stable reference and explicit states such as:

```text
requested → admitted → running → input_required → running
                         |              |
                         v              v
                   reconciliation   cancelled
                         |
          completed | failed | refused
```

- Triggers and retries are idempotent.
- Side-effecting tool calls produce Attempts with effect-aware retry rules.
- An ambiguous irreversible effect is reconciled rather than blindly repeated.
- Human input and approval are durable, signed and replay-safe.
- The agent cannot mark its own task successful without the assignment's completion
  evidence.

### 7. Channels and inbound MCP

The same assignment may be reached through Slack, email, web, API, webhook, schedule,
A2A or MCP. Channels authenticate callers and normalize requests into the same task
lifecycle; they do not own business logic.

For inbound MCP, expose a remote OAuth-protected MCP server with a small stable surface:

- `agent.capabilities` — inspect the assignment's externally callable scope;
- `agent.request` — start or idempotently replay a task;
- `agent.status` — read durable state and permitted next actions;
- `agent.continue` — supply requested input or an approval receipt;
- `agent.cancel` — request cancellation when the current state permits it.

MCP transport is stateless. Each call carries authenticated principal/account context,
and the agent returns explicit task handles rather than relying on a hidden protocol
session. `input_required` and pollable task handles align with the current MCP direction,
but Aecon retains its own canonical task contract and adapters for supported protocol
versions.
[MCP 2026-07-28](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)

This makes an Aecon-managed agent callable from Codex, Claude, OpenCode, ChatGPT and
other MCP hosts without making MCP the agent's identity or memory.

### 8. Evaluation, supervision and change control

- A versioned evaluation set represents normal work, edge cases, forbidden actions,
  prompt injection and escalation conditions.
- New prompt, model, tool, policy and assignment revisions pass simulation before a
  canary rollout.
- Production quality is sampled against contract-valid task outcomes, not subjective
  traces alone.
- Kill switch, rollback and connection freeze are available per agent and Account.
- Staff repair actions are replayable commands, never direct impersonation.

### 9. Evidence and billing

The customer receives a task ledger showing request, actor, approvals, external actions,
Orders, Invocations, costs, outcome and any operator intervention without exposing
private reasoning or secrets.

Pilot pricing:

- one-time paid discovery and setup;
- fixed monthly managed-service fee;
- model, supplier, browser, sandbox and third-party costs passed through transparently;
- explicit service credits or non-billing for contract-invalid work.

Outcome pricing should be introduced only when Aecon can define, verify and reverse the
outcome objectively. Per-token pricing is an internal cost fact, not the customer value
metric.

### 10. Exit, transfer and portability

The customer can terminate the service, revoke every connection, export eligible task
history and evidence, and receive a portable Agent Definition containing instructions,
schemas, policy declarations and non-secret configuration. Historical financial and
audit truth remains retained as legally required.

Runtime migration changes the `Harness` or runtime binding, not the agent's principal,
Account relationships or historical attribution. Aecon must not create a hostage agent
whose identity disappears when a hosting contract ends.

## Constraints

- Do not build a horizontal self-serve agent platform before one managed workflow renews.
- Do not promise autonomous end-to-end operation; supervised exceptions are part of the
  initial service.
- Do not host arbitrary customer code by default.
- Do not let a checkpoint preserve stale authority, credentials or policy.
- Do not blend customer, agent, Aecon operator, payer, supplier and beneficiary roles.
- Do not duplicate Aecon's transaction truth inside a runtime vendor.
- Do not require businesses to replace their existing SaaS systems or human exception
  owners.
- Do not turn every internal agent action into a marketplace Operation. Operations remain
  reusable capability contracts, not arbitrary reasoning steps.
- Do not claim market demand from competitor launches or templates alone.

## Premises

1. Businesses buy a completed operational job, not a collection of agent-runtime
   primitives.
2. The first product is one paid managed workflow; reusable platform components are
   harvested from delivery rather than imagined upfront.
3. Aecon's differentiation is governed identity, authority, commercial execution and
   evidence across runtimes—not a proprietary reasoning loop.
4. Agent runtime and harness are replaceable bindings to a stable principal.
5. Durable state may survive while authority, tools, connections and policy expire.
6. Inbound MCP is a channel for invoking and continuing tasks, not a durable session,
   tenant boundary or agent identity.
7. Managed service agreements and long-lived assignments sit above, rather than replace,
   the atomic Operation/Offer/Quote/Order/Invocation ladder.
8. Human handoff is a production state, not a failure of autonomy.
9. Aecon staff operate through explicit, time-bounded authority and never impersonate
   the customer or agent.
10. No self-serve runtime investment is justified until a real customer pays, uses the
    agent in production and asks to renew.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Managed outcome service — selected

Aecon defines, deploys and operates one narrow agent with the customer. This is the
fastest route to paid demand evidence and forces every platform primitive to answer a
real operational need. Its risks are services-heavy delivery, customer-specific sprawl
and Aecon accepting more operational responsibility.

### Approach B: Bring-your-own-agent runtime

Customers deploy their own agent code while Aecon supplies durable hosting, connections,
identity, approvals and observability. This is the cleanest long-term infrastructure
product, but the market is already crowded by Vercel, Cloudflare, AWS and Microsoft, and
Aecon has no evidence that runtime is its buying trigger.

### Approach C: External-agent control plane

Agents run in customer-selected harnesses while Aecon supplies identity, authority,
connections, budgets, audit and commerce through HTTP/MCP. This best matches Aecon's
existing architectural moat and should remain the portability boundary, but sold alone
it asks customers to solve the runtime and outcome definition before they receive value.

## Recommended Approach

Choose Approach A as the product wedge and Approach C as the architecture boundary.

Aecon initially operates the agent and owns the service burden, but the agent's identity,
assignment, connections, authority and evidence are modeled independently from its
runtime. Repeated delivery reveals which parts deserve self-service. A customer can later
bring its own runtime or an externally hosted agent without re-platforming the commercial
and governance layer.

The first version may use Vercel eve because it aligns with Aecon's existing Vercel
estate and already packages channels, connections, durable work, approvals and sandbox
support. A bounded Cloudflare Durable Object prototype should compare cold-start cost,
state locality, hibernation, recovery and OpenCode compatibility. Neither provider becomes
canonical product architecture until measured pilot evidence selects it.

## Success Criteria

### Demand gate

- Three target businesses describe the same painful workflow with concrete hours or
  dollars attached.
- At least one signs a paid discovery/pilot agreement before generalized product work.
- The buyer names a budget owner, exception owner and renewal condition.

### Pilot gate

- One agent completes at least 100 real tasks over thirty days.
- At least 80% of eligible tasks finish contract-valid without Aecon staff intervention
  by the final pilot week.
- The workflow reduces measured human effort or cycle time by at least 50%.
- There are zero unauthorized consequential actions, secret exposures, cross-account
  accesses, duplicate irreversible effects or unowned unknown states.
- Every consequential action reconstructs actor, Account, authority, connection,
  approval, cost and outcome evidence.
- The customer chooses to renew at a price that produces positive contribution margin.

### Portability gate

- The same Agent Definition passes conformance on two runtime adapters without changing
  principal, Account or assignment identity.
- Revoking authority while a runtime sleeps prevents every new consequence after wake.
- Two independent MCP hosts can request, inspect, continue and cancel tasks using only
  published contracts and OAuth.

## Dependencies

- Canonical Principal, Account, Harness and Credential foundations from maturity Phase 1.
- Authority, connection and secret-management work from maturity Phase 2.
- Durable Invocation, Attempt, Outcome, idempotency, evidence and reconciliation contracts.
- A service-agreement and AgentAssignment model above the current commercial kernel.
- Runtime-adapter conformance tests and one selected pilot runtime.
- Legal, privacy and security terms for Aecon acting as a managed service operator.
- A named design partner and objective workflow acceptance test.

This feature should not interrupt the maturity sequence. The design becomes an input to
later agent distribution and operations phases, while customer discovery can begin now.

## Open Questions to Resolve Through the Paid Pilot

- Which single workflow has enough frequency, objective success and budget to be first?
- Does the buyer prefer a fixed managed-service fee, outcome pricing or a hybrid after
  seeing real task economics?
- Which state is genuinely portable across runtimes without carrying private chain of
  thought, vendor internals or stale authority?
- Which channel creates daily pull: Slack, email, API, webhook or inbound MCP?
- When does sandboxed code execution materially improve outcomes enough to justify its
  cost and risk?

These are experiment questions, not prerequisites for documenting the product shape.

## The Assignment

Within seven days, interview three businesses Aecon could realistically onboard. Ask
each to show—not describe—the repeated workflow they most want an agent to own. Record
the current steps, systems, weekly volume, failure cost, responsible human, approval
points and budget. Offer one fixed-scope paid thirty-day pilot; do not build the general
runtime until someone signs.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You moved from “external-facing agents via MCP” to “OpenCode inside a Durable Object”
  and then to “for businesses we onboard.” That sequence correctly noticed that a
  protocol and runtime are ingredients, while the business still needs a job to buy.
- Your sentence “persistence should preserve continuity without quietly preserving
  authority” is the most important architectural constraint in this feature. It keeps
  durable agents compatible with the maturity plan rather than creating a bypass around
  it.
- You are rightly attracted to the speed and cost of lightweight agent substrates. The
  discipline is to keep that choice replaceable until a paid workflow proves which
  runtime characteristics matter.

## Competitive Position

Aecon should not position against Vercel, Cloudflare, AWS or Microsoft as a better place
to run generic agent code. Those platforms sell runtime, cloud and developer primitives.
It should not initially position as another vertical support agent like Fin or Decagon
either.

The defensible position is:

> **Aecon puts a governed agent to work for your business, then gives that agent a
> portable identity, bounded authority and an economic network it can safely use.**

The managed service is the wedge. The cross-runtime agent control and commerce plane is
the long-term platform.
