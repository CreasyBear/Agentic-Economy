## PRODUCT & TRACTION

**What is the problem you are solving?**

> Software agents can now choose and pay for their own tools mid-task. x402 made the payment leg work: a request hits a 402, payment settles on Base, the resource is returned. The rail is solved.
>
> The purchase is not. A payment proves value moved. It does not prove who authorised the spend, which exact service was bought, on what terms, whether it was delivered, or who owes a refund when it was not. No enterprise risk function can approve an obligation with no stated scope, no bounded authority and no remedy. So agent spending stalls at the point of approval.
>
> The gap runs the other way too. Most capability an agent needs is human-delivered and has no machine-readable form at all, so software cannot buy it regardless of quality.
>
> aecon closes both. We define what a service produces, so it can be sold by a person today and by AI tomorrow under the same contract. We bind each purchase to a fixed price and the customer's policy before anything settles. We route the upstream leg over x402 on Base.

**Why are you working on this idea?**

Needs your own words. The honest origin, from what you have said before: you were using models for real commercial and engineering work and they kept producing confident, wrong facts, because they only know what was in their weights. The fix is not a better model, it is giving the work access to live, verified sources. That led to the tool-access problem, and then to the fact that the valuable sources are paid and nothing lets software buy them.

**What is your unique insight or advantage in the market you are building for?**

> The insight is that the definition of what a service produces has to exist before the work becomes machine-delivered, and that the same definition must survive the transition.
>
> When a capability is defined as a contracted unit with stated inputs, output, price, delivery condition and remedy, a firm can deliver it by hand today and with AI tomorrow and export the same shape. The price, the terms and the record do not change. That is what lets an Australian service business become AI-native without renegotiating what it sells, and it is the piece no one else is positioned on. Everyone else is either selling intelligence to agents or selling tool access to agents.
>
> The secondary advantage is supply depth in a market that is legally gated. We have mapped which Australian records and services are commercially used and have no programmatic access at all, from state land registries to environmental registers to grid capacity data. Those are work with statutory standing and no free substitute.

## WHY BASE

**Why do you want to join Base Batches?**

> Base Batches sits exactly where we are. Two of the program's focus areas, AI agents and payments, describe the same problem from different ends, and we are building the piece that joins them. The eight weeks and the investor day are useful, but the specific draw is the Base and Coinbase network around agent commerce. x402 is a Coinbase protocol. The supply we buy from is registered through the Coinbase Developer Platform. The teams solving the adjacent problems are in this cohort, not in a general accelerator.

**What part of your product is or will be onchain? What part uses Base?**

This is the most important answer in the form. Do not bury it.

> Settlement is onchain, and it is on Base specifically.
>
> Every purchase aecon mediates has two legs. The customer-facing leg is a fiat charge in Australian dollars. The upstream leg is a live x402 payment in USDC on Base, settled from corporate treasury to the service provider. That leg is onchain on every transaction.
>
> Base is also where the supply side lives. The services aecon buys from are x402 endpoints discoverable through the CDP registry, and the facilitators that verify and settle those payments run on Base. This is not a deployment choice we made among several; the protocol, the counterparties and the rail are all here.
>
> The economics require Base specifically. Agent purchases are micropayments, single-dollar lookups and sub-dollar data calls, thousands per customer per month. A rail with meaningful per-transaction cost makes those purchases uneconomic, which is precisely why enterprises have not been able to buy this way.
>
> What we are not doing is putting a token or a contract in front of the buyer. Australian businesses will not hold wallets or manage digital assets. The fiat layer is the adoption mechanism that routes enterprise demand onto Base; the settlement itself is onchain.
