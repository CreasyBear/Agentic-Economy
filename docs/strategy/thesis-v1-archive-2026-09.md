# The agent is now the app store

*A thesis on the agentic economy and what Australia needs in it. September 2026.*

## Summary

Software can now choose its own tools and pay for them. Anyone whose work an agent might need can sell it, and the thing agents will pay most for is authority: a signed statement that something complies, is owned, or is true. That is a new economy with a machine as the buyer, and every economy like it has needed a market before it could grow: a directory the buyer chooses from and a counter it pays at. The App Store was both. Agents have taken over the choosing. Nobody has built the counter. aecon is building it, in Australia first, because Australian law makes the machine economy untouchable for every Australian and the same law makes aecon necessary.

## 1. The shift

Two things changed in the last eighteen months, and each is ordinary on its own.

Software chooses. An agent working a task decides which tools it needs, finds one that fits, and uses it. Nobody selected that tool in advance.

Software pays. x402 lets a program meet a price on the web and settle it inside the same request. No account, no card on file, no person. By Visa's count in mid-2026 it had carried over a hundred million such payments since launching in May 2025. Stripe, Google, Visa and Mastercard have each announced their own version.

Together they mean software has become a buyer. It selects a supplier it has never met, pays, and moves on. Nothing in how people or businesses buy was built for that.

## 2. The economics

Selling a service used to need a sales motion, a contract, an invoice, and a customer willing to sit through all three. A machine buyer needs an address, a price, and a result. So the cost of becoming a seller falls toward zero, and the supply that appears is not software companies. It is anyone whose work an agent needs mid-task.

What agents will pay most for is not data. A model can absorb data. It is authority: the licensed surveyor's certificate, the accredited lab's result, the registered agent's lodgement, the standards body's compliance verdict. A model can tell an agent what the clearance distance should be. No council, insurer or grid operator will accept the model's word. Attestation is the one good in the machine economy that cannot be pirated or generated, and Australia has an unusual density of the small licensed firms that hold it.

On the buying side the constraint inverts. The cost of a purchase is no longer the price. It is the cost of the relationship. A single lookup from a supplier the agent found a moment ago may be exactly what the task needs and still be impossible to buy, because approving a new supplier takes a business days and costs more than the lookup. Lowering the price does not fix this. The fixed cost of trusting a stranger does not shrink with the transaction.

We believe this is the central economic fact of the agentic economy. Micropayments do not remove the need for an intermediary. They make one more valuable, because someone has to absorb the cost of trusting a stranger once so that thousands of small purchases can happen without it.

## 3. What a market is

Every market that let strangers trade at scale supplied the same five things. eBay, the App Store, Taobao, Upwork, the Chicago Board of Trade: the list does not change. Settlement the parties can trust. Reputation that follows the seller. A price fixed before the deal. A remedy when the thing bought does not arrive. Onboarding so light that supply appears on its own.

Once all five existed, supply moved fast. The App Store opened with five hundred applications and passed ten thousand within four months.

The agentic economy has two of the five. x402 settles. Any endpoint can sell. It has no price that holds across providers, no reputation, and no remedy. Those three are the market.

## 4. Who captures the value

Each time a new kind of buyer has met a new kind of seller, the value has gone to the party between them, and it has arrived years after the wire. The telegraph moved prices in 1844; the Board of Trade standardised futures in 1864 and took a share of every contract. Banks issued cards; Visa became the network. Advertising went programmatic and machines began buying from machines per impression. The exchanges in the middle took a large share of every dollar.

The Yellow Pages is the older and plainer version. Sellers paid to be listed because the book was in every house and readers opened it with intent. Readers paid nothing and never had to know a plumber existed. It died in five years when Google captured the intent, because the book never touched the plumber's invoice. The App Store did not die when Google arrived, because it owned the transaction as well as the listing.

That is the lesson. A directory alone loses to whoever owns the buyer's default search. A directory with a counter is a market.

## 5. What aecon is

aecon is the directory Australian agents choose from and the counter they pay at.

For sellers: list once, get verified, get found by agents anywhere, get paid in dollars on an ordinary invoice. No sales team, no wallet, nothing crypto on the books. Sellers are the customer. They pay for listing, verification and placement, and aecon takes a margin on settlement.

For buyers: every Australian, person or business, opens one account, funds it in dollars, sets what each agent may buy, and lets it buy. aecon fixes the price before the call, checks the mandate before selection, pays the provider on the wire, records what was bought and whether it arrived, and stands behind the sale within limits set in advance. Readers are the distribution. They pay nothing to look.

What aecon keeps is the record. Every purchase leaves evidence of which provider delivered, how fast, at what price. Across every provider and every call that evidence is reputation in the only form a machine buyer can use, and for a project that bought a dozen attestations from a dozen bodies it is the audit trail the insurer and the council will read. We believe the records are the asset that lasts. Technology can be copied. A record of how every provider actually performed compounds with each transaction and cannot be reconstructed by anyone who was not there.

## 6. Why Australia, and why now

Australians use AI more than anyone on earth per head, and Australian law locks every one of them out of the machine economy.

Under Australian tax law every spend of a stablecoin is a capital gains event with its own record, and the personal-use exemption is in practice unavailable for spending. A person whose agent buys a fifty-cent lookup with USDC owes the tax office a CGT calculation. A developer who sells a service for USDC has income at the day's rate, a capital gains event on conversion, GST on the supply, and an accountant who has never seen any of it. The wall runs both ways and it covers everyone.

The wall is not global. The United States removed it for regulated stablecoins in March 2026. The United Kingdom removes it in April 2027. Australia, Japan, India, France, Canada and New Zealand have not moved. So the largest agent markets are about to grow fastest, and Australians can neither buy from them nor sell to them.

Nobody has built the bridge. No company anywhere offers dollars in, crypto held by the company, seller of record, tax invoice out, for x402 services. The agent-payment companies pass settlement through to the merchant. The merchant-of-record companies take fiat only and do not touch agents. The intersection is empty.

aecon stands on the far side of the wall once, for the whole country. Buyers get access to the live global supply in dollars. Australian sellers get an export channel to the American and British agent markets and are paid in dollars. That is the onboarding. It drives both sides in, and it is compulsory by law rather than optional by convenience.

We believe the wall will fall. The tax office will be pressed to follow the UK and the US within a few years. When it does, the reason to join disappears and what remains is what was built while it stood: every Australian seller listed with records, every Australian buyer with an account, and the ranking that improves with every recorded delivery. The bridge acquires the country. The records keep it.

## 7. What others are building

The rails move money: Coinbase's x402, Stripe's machine payments protocol, Google's AP2, the card networks' agent tokens. Cards cannot carry this traffic; a five-cent transaction costs fifteen cents in fees, so agent-to-API payments will run on stablecoins and the wall matters exactly where the commerce is. The gateways make calls easier: one key, many tools. The registries list what exists.

All are necessary. None is the market, and none sells to an Australian. Each assumes the agent's owner and the provider will sort out price, trust and failure between themselves.

The one to watch is Stripe. It owns a merchant-of-record product and a stablecoin agent rail, and pure compliance bridges have historically been absorbed by exactly that kind of player within three to five years. Stripe will not curate a Perth building surveyor's listing or carry the remedy on her certificate. It could offer AUD invoicing on agent payments. That is the race, and it is why the records and the sellers matter more than the bridge.

## 8. What must be true

A thesis that cannot be wrong is not a thesis. This one fails if any of the following holds.

Agents rarely need anything outside the tools they were shipped with. Then there is no market to build.

People and businesses refuse to let software buy at all. Then every purchase stays human-gated.

The cost of trusting a new supplier cannot be amortised across buyers. Then the margin exceeds the value.

Australia exempts stablecoin spending before aecon has enough sellers and buyers to matter. Then the bridge closes before the market exists.

Stripe or a major agent platform offers the seller of record role with local invoicing before aecon has records worth keeping. Then the space closes from above.

Protocols come to carry enforceable identity, authority and remedy on their own. Then the market is built into the wire.

The fourth and fifth are the ones we watch. The signal for the fourth is a Treasury consultation on stablecoin tax. The signal for the fifth is a platform accepting liability for a tool it did not build. Neither has happened. That is the window.

## 9. The claim

For fifteen years the App Store did two jobs. It chose what software reached you, and it made the sale safe: one account, one bill, a refund when something broke. Agents have taken the first job. Nobody has taken the second, and in Australia the law makes it impossible for anyone to buy or sell without it.

Software has become a buyer. Australians are becoming sellers. Between them there is a wall and nothing else. aecon is the directory and the counter on the far side of it.

The agent is now the app store. aecon is the economy behind it.
