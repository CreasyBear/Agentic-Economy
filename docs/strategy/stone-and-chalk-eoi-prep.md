## Honest fit assessment

### Where aecon fits hard

- **AI guardrails and governance.** This is the strongest match on the whole form. aecon's spending policy, expiring quote and bounded authority are precisely the control layer that lets a regulated enterprise permit an agent to spend at all.
- **Governance and regulatory compliance**, a named priority area. aecon produces an auditable purchase record linking authority, counterparty, delivery and remedy. That is what a bank's risk function asks for.
- **Identity and insider threats**, a named priority area. aecon binds agent identity to an account and a policy. A credential is access and audit evidence, not a budget owner.
- **Customer protection**, a named priority area. Remedy, refund and unresolved-outcome handling under stated terms.
- **Safe and responsible AI**, a named priority area, and the first of the four support areas.

### Where aecon does not fit, stated plainly

The program is built to help a company **sell into** banks, insurers and super funds. aecon is not a product a bank buys to run inside itself. It is being built as a market and settlement layer that a bank would transact through.

That is a real gap and the application must not paper over it. The defensible argument is narrower and stronger: banks and super funds will be the strictest buyers of anything an agent purchases on their behalf, and they cannot allow agent spending without authority evidence, spend limits and an audit trail. aecon is building the layer that supplies that. Cuscal being a founding partner, and Solana being the services partner, both point the same direction.

### The traction problem

The program asks for "a proven AI product and commercial traction, with technology that is already being used by customers." The FAQ softens this: "You do not need to be fully enterprise-ready."

aecon is pre-launch. Production money is disabled pending legal, AML/CTF, tax and bank-account gates. The intensive accelerator is a stretch. The national digital track is open to companies beyond the selected cohort and is the realistic target.

**Apply anyway.** The partner list is the network aecon needs, the EOI is free, and a decline costs nothing but an hour.

**What is your offering and what problem does it solve?**

Agentic Economy is building the layer that turns a service capability into a contracted one that software can buy.

The core of it is a definition, not a constraint. For a given service, we define what the service produces: its required inputs, its output, its price, its delivery condition, its remedy, and the evidence that travels with the result. That definition is the contract. It does not govern how the work is performed, and it does not constrain what a model produces inside it. It states, in a form a program can read, what the buyer is purchasing.

Once that definition exists, a service can be sold before and after it becomes AI-powered. A firm delivering the work by hand exports a result in that shape today. When the same firm automates part of the work with AI, it exports the same shape. The buyer's agent sees one interface either way, and the price, the delivery condition and the record do not change. The contract survives the transition, which is what allows a business to become AI-native without disrupting the commercial relationships it already has.

That definition is what is currently missing. Most Australian capability is human-delivered, so it has no machine-readable form at all: software cannot buy it, and an agent cannot reach it. When those firms begin to automate, they inherit the same problem, because nothing was ever defined. Defining the output is the step that has to happen before any of this can be transacted by software.

The timing is set by three developments arriving together. Models can now select and invoke outside services during a task rather than only generating text. Payment rails now allow software to settle a transaction without a person. And the first agent platforms capable of purchasing are entering the market. The rail and the agent are being solved elsewhere, by others. The definition layer between them is not built, and it is the layer that determines which Australian services are reachable at all.

Enterprise adoption follows the same path. A bank, insurer or superannuation fund cannot procure a category of work that has no defined unit, but it can procure a capability with a stated input, output, price, delivery condition and remedy, carrying a spend limit and an audit record for each call. That is the difference between work that can be procured and work that remains a pilot.

Australia is an appropriate place to build this, and the field is genuinely early. Services account for approximately half of national output, and around 900,000 Australian employing businesses have fewer than twenty staff, most of them holding capability that cannot currently be purchased by software. One goods and services tax, one company register and one consumer law allow the compliant path from a defined capability to an invoiced purchase to be built once and applied nationally. No one has defined these output contracts at scale, and the definitions become the standard by which Australian service capability is bought, whichever agent or platform is doing the buying.

**How is AI part of your offering?**

AI sits on both sides of the transaction, and Agentic Economy sits between them.

On the demand side, AI has changed the buyer. A model working a task can now decide what it needs, find a supplier and invoke it, without a person choosing that supplier in advance. That behaviour is what makes a machine-readable definition of a service necessary at all.

On the supply side, AI changes how the work is delivered. A service defined as a contracted capability can be delivered by people today and by AI tomorrow, exporting the same output, at the same price, under the same delivery condition. That definition is what allows a firm to automate without renegotiating what it sells, and without the buyer seeing a different product.

Agentic Economy does not build or depend on a model. It does not generate output, reason about a task, or constrain what a model produces. It defines what a service produces, records what was purchased and under which authority, and holds that definition stable across both modes of delivery.
