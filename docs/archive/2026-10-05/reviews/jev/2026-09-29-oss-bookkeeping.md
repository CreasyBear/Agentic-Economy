# Jev and OSS bookkeeping decisions — 29 September 2026

## Slice A: TypeSafe System One / bounded decision APIs

**Finding:** Jev-specific **invoice and expense-processing OSS does exist**. The strongest implementation, [`tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices), actually codes receipt lines to illustrative VAT codes and decides supplier-invoice approval. [`carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage`](https://github.com/carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage) guards a consequential accounts-payable operation. Neither is an end-to-end, production bookkeeping/ledger system: the former has synthetic inputs, simplified Norwegian tax policy and no authentication; the latter's release operation and business data are mocked. Do not conclude from the absence of a complete ledger that finance-specific Jev source is absent.

### 1. Supplier invoice + expense VAT: `tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices`

**Repo:** https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices. **Code evidence:** [`models.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/models.py), [`questions.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/questions.py), [`expense.yaml`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/questions/expense.yaml), [`vendor.yaml`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/questions/vendor.yaml), [`jev.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/jev.py), [`app.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/app.py), [`rules/expense.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/rules/expense.py), [`rules/vendor.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/rules/vendor.py), [`rules/judgments.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/rules/judgments.py), [`bench.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/bench.py).

Real decision-shape excerpt from `src/jev_invoices/questions.py`:

```python
LINE_CRITERIA = {
    "lodging": "One or more nights of accommodation, such as a hotel room. A room with breakfast on one line counts as lodging. A meeting room does not.",
    "served_food": "Food or a non-alcoholic drink to eat or drink right away, such as a meal, coffee, room service, takeaway food, or snacks and soft drinks from a minibar.",
    "alcohol": "An alcoholic drink, such as beer, wine, cider or spirits, including from a minibar.",
```

- **State:** `ExpenseInvoiceIn.state()` serializes extracted invoice text, employee/vendor country, and expense purpose, excluding the already-extracted receipt lines (each line is embedded in its own question). `VendorInvoiceIn.state()` sends `invoice_text` and purchase-order descriptions; amounts, dates and bank-account equality are checked in Python, while earlier-invoice text is embedded in generated duplicate questions. No OCR; `/expense-invoices` and `/vendor-invoices` receive structured inputs. The [sample fixture](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/samples/vendor/clean-invoice.json) demonstrates vendor, PO, earlier invoice and expected decision.
- **Questions / candidates:** One `system_one(state=state, questions=questions)` SDK request per invoice; the SDK is `typesafe_sdk.AsyncTypeSafeClient`, not the JS package. Expense: three fixed `noul`s (hospitality, named diners, purpose matching), a four-option receipt-kind `choice`, a three-level purpose-specificity `score`, plus one **eight-option** purchase-kind `choice` per receipt line; 5 + up to 50 line questions. Vendor: a three-level line-specificity `score`, four fixed `noul`s (PO match, unordered items, bank change, pressure), a **six-option** document-kind `choice`, plus one duplicate `noul` per earlier invoice within 60 days (up to 50 supplied). `extra_questions` may be added but cannot collide with built-in IDs. These are judgments, **not** a choice from a chart of accounts.
- **Consumption / boundary:** `rules/expense.py` maps line choices to SAF-T VAT codes (e.g. `lodging`/`transport` → `13`, `served_food`/`alcohol` → `0`, `goods` → `1`); returns `needs_review`, flags and totals. `rules/vendor.py` computes exact amount/PO tolerance (2%), prior invoice number and bank-account checks, then returns `hold`, `review` or `approve`. `rules/judgments.py` marks **only read answers** uncertain: Noul between 0.2 and 0.8, Choice confidence below 0.6. The application stores separate raw `judgments`, derived `computed` and Jev metadata in SQLite/JSON; no actual payment or ledger posting.
- **Timeout / failure:** `jev.py` constructs the SDK client without an explicit timeout in this repository; SDK retries rate limits/server errors according to its defaults. Unavailable Jev maps to HTTP **502**; invalid questions to **422**; there is no synthetic approval or fallback categorization. Startup without a TypeSafe key fails. Not comparable to aecon's fast, fail-open search reranking without a product-specific boundary decision.
- **Reported measurement, not independently rerun:** [README's measured section](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/README.md#measured) describes a `jev-1.13.0` run on 20 held-out **synthetic** documents (12 expenses, 8 vendor invoices, each in English and Norwegian), with **16/20** correct derived outcomes in English and **14/20** in Norwegian; 136–137/143 individual answers on the correct side of thresholds, 5 uncertain, 1–2 wrong-side. Reported **0.23–0.41 s/document** (mean ~0.3 s); 1,000 concurrent receipt calls at 20 in flight took 17.4 s (median 0.28 s, p90 <0.32 s, one 10.7-s retry). [`bench.py`](https://github.com/tameernoor/aotn-jev-invoices/blob/main/src/jev_invoices/bench.py) implements scoring and latency accounting; no real-book accuracy estimate. One false negative coded a private lip balm as deductible goods: typed answers do not make tax decisions intrinsically correct.

### 2. Execution-time supplier-invoice gate: `carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage`

**Repo:** https://github.com/carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage. **Code evidence:** [`app/decision_gates.py`](https://github.com/carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage/blob/main/assets/invoice-triage-jev/app/decision_gates.py), [`app/jev.py`](https://github.com/carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage/blob/main/assets/invoice-triage-jev/app/jev.py), [release-guard regression cases](https://github.com/carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage/blob/main/assets/invoice-triage-jev/tests/test_decision_gates.py). This is the public companion of the [SAP Community technical article](https://community.sap.com/t5/technology-blog-posts-by-sap/joule-studio-with-jev-at-the-decision-boundary-for-autonomous-enterprise-ai/ba-p/14490897); the mock release only clears a synthetic payment block, **not a payment**.

Real decision-shape excerpt from `assets/invoice-triage-jev/app/jev.py`:

```python
    return await _ask_choice(
        state,
        instructions="Should this consequential tool call be allowed to run?",
        criteria={
            "allow": "Safe and justified — run as-is.",
            "confirm": "Run only after explicit human confirmation.",
            "review": "Hold for human review before running.",
            "deny": "Do not run — unsafe or against policy.",
        },
    )
```

- **State:** At the instant `release_payment_block` is called, the wrapper **re-reads** invoice detail and recent invoices for that vendor via sibling tools. It derives PO/goods-receipt status, price variance and same-vendor/same-amount duplicate signals; `tool_guard()` constructs `{tool, action, arguments_summary, side_effects, safeguards, policy, reversibility}`. Evidence fetch is best-effort (may produce a thinner state), a caveat when adapting it to real money.
- **Questions / candidates:** Direct `httpx` POST to `/v1/systemone` with model default `jev-latest`, **one Choice over four application-defined actions** `allow | confirm | review | deny` (plus a distinct three-way advisory completion Choice elsewhere). No `@typesafe-ai/sdk` dependency and no account/Tool candidate ranking.
- **Consumption / failure:** `allow` alone invokes the original release coroutine. `deny` returns `release_denied`; `review`, `confirm`, disabled Jev, missing/invalid answers or request failure return `release_pending_review` without invoking release. The hold is a status, **not** a wired human-approval workflow. Timeout defaults to **15 seconds**, configurable via `TYPESAFE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`/`JEV_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`. Tests cover explicit allow, other verdicts, invalid bodies, timeout and duplicate evidence with mocked transport; no measured live accuracy or latency published in the repository. The [README](https://github.com/carlosbasto/joule-studio-jev-invoice-triage/blob/main/README.md) identifies synthetic data and a mock release tool.

### TypeSafe first-party finance publication, distinct from OSS code

TypeSafe publishes an [Invoice Processing workflow evaluation](https://evals.typesafe.ai/invoice_processing.html) and [expense-claim example](https://evals.typesafe.ai/). The invoice evaluation's exact advertised **decision shape** is: input invoice, PO, contract, vendor master, prior invoices, correspondence, delivery and approvals; seven subject-specific rounds of `Noul`/`Choice`/`Score` questions; code computes sums/dates/account numbers/statuses; four stop conditions (fraud, already paid, wrong vendor/entity, non-invoice), then parallel hold/dispute/correction rule lists, then yes/no checks for approval, signature, short-pay, scheduling or pay. Outputs can coexist (`PAY`, `SCHEDULE`, `SHORT PAY`, `ROUTE FOR APPROVAL`, `HOLD FOR DOCUMENTS`, `REQUEST CORRECTED INVOICE`, `DISPUTE LINES`, `FRAUD REVIEW`, `DUPLICATE`): **not one nine-way Choice**. The page's chart HTML labels a Jev workflow point `61.8% · $0.0011 · 0.5 s` **against its consensus evaluation labels**, not real-book precision or an OSS benchmark. No published code path, timeout or fallback is shown on that page; do not mistake its workflow diagram for a fetchable bookkeeping repository. The overview's expense example asks whether a receipt is readable, classifies meal/travel/equipment and checks manager sign-off for a meal over $75 when claim description does not clearly match the receipt. The [official use-case map](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/use-case-map.md) covers financial-crime transaction narratives and investigator routing, but does not publish a bookkeeping application.
The evaluation page does not disclose the per-stage question count or candidate counts; it gives no timeout or fallback policy.

### Transfer to aecon's Jev-backed Tool selection/ranking (not tax-policy adoption)

1. **Separate semantic evidence from executable policy.** As `aotn-jev-invoices` keeps money/account comparisons in code, aecon can score Tool descriptions or provider claims with independent `noul`/`score` judgments but retain deterministic routeability, price and access gates in code. Persist which question actually influenced selection; don't let unused low-confidence scores cancel viable choices.
2. **Constrain each candidate set and include an explicit nonmatch path.** Financial document classes have `other`; invoice intake has `general`. For registry search, shortlist verified Tools and distinguish no suitable Tool from merely the top of a forced Choice. The source examples do not establish that Jev can discover unlisted Tools.
3. **Treat consequential execution separately from search ranking.** The SAP gate reconstructs fresh evidence and makes one four-way Choice at the action boundary; a `deny`/`review` is appropriate for irreversible AP state changes but should not be transplanted wholesale onto aecon's fail-open, non-mutating Tool rerank. Its 15-second timeout is also not a justification for aecon's 1.5-second search path.

**Evidence boundaries:** No cited repository is a production ledger or bank-feed-to-chart-of-accounts poster; none proves accuracy on real bookkeeping transactions. Expense VAT examples are Norwegian and illustrative, not Australian GST rules. No third-party source was copied into this repository.

### Source decision excerpts and operational detail

- **Actual AI:** The prompt's actual output contract is `"type": "existing"|"new"|"rule"` and an existing category ID; the rendered prompt contains actual category/group IDs and Actual rules. (Quote: [`prompt.hbs`](https://github.com/sakowicz/actual-ai/blob/master/src/templates/prompt.hbs): `Existing categories by group:` and `"type": "existing"|"new"|"rule"`.) The parser accepts JSON with required fields, bare IDs, and can fish a UUID from otherwise unparseable text; the processor dispatches to a strategy and tags malformed/errors as not guessed. Existing IDs are checked against current category entities; a missing ID is marked not guessed. Novel suggestions are grouped, optimized, reused if already present, then created by code; source comments say the LLM regularly repeats existing categories and can misstate whether a group is new. The transaction filter excludes already-categorized transactions and (unless rerun is enabled) prior not-guessed rows. This is richer than “LLM returns a category,” but its 120 s default and provider retry/rate-limit policy are unsuitable as-is for interactive Tool search.
- **Firefly toolbox:** [`clients/ai.ts`](https://github.com/Xenolphthalein/toolbox-for-firefly-iii/blob/main/src/server/clients/ai.ts) includes current category names in the prompt and requests `categoryName`, `confidence` (0–1), `reasoning`; it asks for `Uncategorized` when no fit. It normalizes amount with JS `parseFloat(...).toFixed(2)`, not an exact decimal type, and sends no currency field. JSON is parsed, but no runtime schema/range validation is done there. [`aiSuggestions.ts`](https://github.com/Xenolphthalein/toolbox-for-firefly-iii/blob/main/src/server/services/aiSuggestions.ts) exact-matches category name against Firefly's current list; a sentinel becomes `unableToClassify`, and an unknown name is logged and dropped. Suggestions and their confidence are returned; application is a separate route. The service defines `minConfidence: 0.3` but does not filter the shown category-suggestion path by it. Treat its confidence as display data, not a calibrated acceptance gate.
- **Invoice notebook:** Code defines `CostType(str, Enum)` and `Reply.category: CostType`; `Reply.confidence` is constrained to 1–100. The notebook reports 805/900 correct at baseline and says reducing ambiguous cost types from 50 to 40 led to 294/300 correct. It also reports that model confidence failed to flag many incorrect answers, including confidently wrong ones. Take the 98% as the notebook's synthetic/sample result, not evidence for production accounting or an established confidence threshold.
- **Invoice Maid:** Rules in `email_classifier.py` can decide obvious invoice/non-invoice cases or return `None` for the LLM; `scheduler.py` invokes the LLM for ambiguous messages and uses `EmailAnalysis.should_download` only when a link's `url_confidence >= 0.6`. `InvoiceCategory` is an enum (`vat_invoice`, `receipt`, `proforma`, `overseas_invoice`, `other`); Pydantic constrains confidence ranges, and a model validator enforces relationships such as non-invoice ⇒ reason required and no URL. Extraction prompt defines category tests and distinguishes category from VAT validity. Manual-upload code uses `Decimal`, a completeness/confidence gate (normally 0.6; transport e-tickets 0.5), invoice-number deduplication and a database uniqueness race path. The app deliberately retains low-confidence records as review outcomes rather than applying guesses. It is a source-rich example of a document workflow; its document taxonomy is not a GL account list.
- **Docket:** `classify.py` builds choices from registered schema descriptions plus `unknown`; `catalog.parse_type()` maps unregistered model output to `unknown`. Rules defer on close scores/ambiguous “invoice” headers; TF-IDF has a confidence floor; only then does the LLM run. If that call fails, code falls back to TF-IDF when available or returns `unknown` at confidence 0. `review_reasons.py` routes low classification confidence/unknown type to review. This is the honest **(d)** example: the common path is deterministic, not LLM classification. Its benchmark calls the first Portuguese receipt run wrong (11/50 receipt, 39/50 invoice) and documents a rule change requiring invoice structure, after which 49/50 were receipts. The same report warns that 2 of 13 scans were wrong but passed review, so validation/review is not a proof of correctness.
- **Actual-GPT:** `prompt.tmpl` receives the category names and a table of historical categorisations; `ACTUALGPT_HISTORICAL_DAYS_FOR_PROMPT` configures the date range (the README example sets 90 days). `app.js` says output only the chosen category or `###UNKNOWN###`, exact-matches names and handles the sentinel, but if a non-sentinel name does not match it obtains `null` and still calls `updateTransaction`; there is no parser/schema guard at this boundary. It also concatenates transactions into a Markdown table without escaping source content, so merchant/notes text can distort the model context. This source does not establish robust abstention for malformed or invented labels.
- **Local Actual categorizer:** `main.ts` prompts with current category names and amount/payee/notes, asking for only the selected option; it omits date and hardcodes `$`. It has one hand-coded spelling repair, then case/trim-normalizes the returned text to look up the category ID and logs rather than updates if no match. `llm.ts` parses each streamed Ollama line as JSON but has no visible timeout, transport-status check, or retry. No data supports its accuracy or currency correctness.
- **Expense labeler:** `instructions.json` supplies closed lists, and the prompt requests one category and deduction fields. `expense_labeler.py` enables JSON-object mode and `json.loads`, but does not validate required keys, types or returned labels against those lists. The system prompt explicitly invites “creatively, out of the box” deductions—an unsafe incentive in a tax setting. On any exception, retry count can reach 99 with capped exponential sleeps; exhaustion returns `None`, after which the original CSV row is written without generated AI fields. This is a cautionary pattern, not a production recipe.

### Mapping to aecon Jev at a 1,500 ms interactive budget

aecon's [`jev-client.ts`](../../../../../src/modules/decision-engine/jev-client.ts) gives interactive calls a 1,500 ms timeout and disables retries for `discovery`; it validates answer type/range and rejects invalid `choice` keys. [`tool-reranker.ts`](../../../../../src/modules/registry/tool-reranker.ts) already implements the directly reusable bounded shape: deterministic retrieval first, bounded candidate prefix, a single `noul` per candidate in one System One call, deterministic descending sort, explicit match threshold, and original/lexical results on operational failure.

| Jev primitive | Bounded Tool-selection use | 1.5 s discipline |
|---|---|---|
| `noul` | Best fit for “does this candidate directly perform the requested bounded contribution and meet each explicit constraint?” One binary relevance judgement per retrieved candidate; code ranks returned probabilities and keeps the retrieval index as tie-breaker. Add “none suitable” through the derived no-match threshold rather than choosing a forced winner. | Make one parallel/batched System One request for the existing capped shortlist, never one sequential request per Tool. Pre-filter routeability/eligibility deterministically and keep candidate evidence compact. Return lexical/original results if unavailable; no retries or fallback LLM inside the interactive deadline. |
| `choice` | Use for one small, mutually exclusive presentation/action decision (e.g. `describe`, `search`, `compare`, `clarify`) or exactly-one Tool selection when that is truly the requested behavior. Include an explicit `none`/`clarify` option; preserve multiple relevant Tools for search/compare rather than collapsing them to a winner. | Share the same call as candidate relevance where useful. Apply local confidence threshold and check choice membership; never let an untrusted candidate become authority to execute it. |
| `score` | Use only when a candidate needs an ordinal rubric (e.g. unrelated / adjacent / direct / exact fit); code maps the bounded returned ordinal to ordering or deterministic thresholds. It is not a reason to ask the model for a free-form account name or a supposedly calibrated confidence. | One per candidate in a bounded batch if ordinal distinctions genuinely alter ranking. Prefer `noul` for direct relevance because it matches aecon's existing reranker and avoids inventing unused ranking semantics. |

The gateway enforces that `noul` lies in `[0,1]`, `score` is within the configured criteria indices, and `choice` is one of the supplied keys (`jev-client.ts`). The answer value is a judgment signal—not operational proof of callable status, price, access, or truth; those remain code/data gates. Nothing in the OSS benchmarks establishes that these workloads fit Jev's 1.5 s budget; the budget mapping above is a proposed fit to aecon's current call pattern, not a measured guarantee.

### Ranked top five patterns worth porting into aecon Tool selection

1. **Bounded candidate-by-candidate `noul`, then code ranks (strongest fit).** Reuse aecon's current pattern, corroborated by the accounting shape of one judgement per transaction/invoice: retrieval constructs the candidate set; Jev only judges fit; code sorts with stable ties and uses explicit thresholds.
2. **No forced winner; abstain on no fit or unavailable evidence.** Take Docket's explicit `unknown` and Actual AI's not-guessed state as the useful pattern, but express it as aecon's `noul` threshold/clarify outcome and preserve lexical results rather than rejecting a search.
3. **Keep the candidate vocabulary authoritative and resolve to IDs in code.** Actual Budget category IDs, Firefly's exact category-name match, Docket's registered-schema `parse_type`, and Invoice Maid's enum all show why generated names must not directly mutate a system.
4. **Use deterministic gates before model work; use model only for semantic ambiguity.** Docket's rules → TF-IDF → LLM cascade and Invoice Maid's rule-first email classifier reduce model work; aecon should analogously perform deterministic directory retrieval and eligibility before ranking.
5. **Treat proposed novelty as suggestion, not authority.** Actual AI groups/deduplicates new-category proposals before code creates them; for aecon, a model may rank known Tools but must not invent a callable Tool or override routeability/pricing facts.

**Fetch failures (dropped, not analyzed):** `realityinspector/llm_expense_labeler` and `mr-karan/gullak` returned GitHub 404/Not Found on repository/source fetch attempts. No behavior claims are made about either.
