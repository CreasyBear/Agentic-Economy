# Tool market routing and data strategy

**Date:** 25 September 2026
**Status:** proposed platform design strategy; product direction in [PRODUCT.md](../../../../PRODUCT.md) is active, implementation and public contracts remain subject to their owning decisions
**Decision owner:** Joel Chan
**Scope:** discovery, Provider distribution, AI and Tool routing, information release, Customer and Agent controls, Quote/Call, records and recovery

**Data-handling authority:** [Platform data privacy design](../../../designs/platform-data-privacy.md) owns recipient, first-release, access, retention and Australian privacy requirements. The privacy analysis below is research context and routing rationale; use that canonical design for platform behavior and acceptance.

## 1. Decision in one sentence

Make an outside capability as easy for an Agent to find and use as an OpenRouter server tool, while AE remains the market and commercial boundary that shows the Customer who receives data, what one Call costs, what happened, and what can be done next.

This advances both core journeys in [PRODUCT.md](../../../../PRODUCT.md#core-market-journeys): the buyer gets a useful contribution with fewer decisions and the Provider reaches relevant demand. Managed x402 purchasing is the entry release. Broader specialist supply and better matching are the product direction, not claims about what is live today. The Agent keeps its existing harness; AE does not own the larger objective or become a general model router.

**Decision now:** stay with that direction and use established products as quality benchmarks. The additional purchase step must earn its place through clear price, authority, data recipient, record and remedy. A routine authorised Call should feel like one continuous task.

## 2. Why the strategy is needed

[OpenRouter's Tools catalog](https://openrouter.ai/tools) places common server tools inside a model request, and its [server-tools guide](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/server-tools) describes the resulting execution loop. Its [Tool Search](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/server-tools/tool-search) selects among developer-supplied function definitions; the application executes those functions. Public documentation reviewed on this date does not show an open, self-service market where any business publishes a paid, non-inference service under a general buyer purchase and remedy contract. That is a public-documentation observation, not a claim about private integrations or future releases.

OpenRouter wins the default path for enabled, common functions: the Agent may never need to look elsewhere. AE must therefore prove value at the capability gap, especially where fit, Provider choice, terms, local commercial treatment or recovery matter. OpenRouter is also a possible distribution channel: an application can expose an AE-backed function while AE continues to own the Quote and Call. AE's own chat currently uses OpenRouter, making it an operational dependency whose actual routing and privacy settings need separate inspection.

The accepted product target remains one chain:

```text
capability gap -> search and compare -> Quote -> Call -> result or uncertainty
    -> remedy if required -> Purchase status -> Outcome record -> Agent continues
```

Discovery itself can transmit a bounded query. For managed x402, **a price inspection can send proposed input to the selected Provider before Quote issuance**. The first information-release boundary may therefore occur before the first commercial commitment. Treat it as a real release, with the same recipient and authority discipline.

**The common architecture is a scoped contract, not a universal “private” switch.** A Customer/organisation sets standing limits; a project or Agent inherits and may narrow them; a request selects within the eligible set. The chosen endpoint/hosting mode determines actual recipients and processing location. Training, abuse logs, application state, gateway logs, cache, files and external tools are independently described. The execution record captures actual attempts. The clearest counterexamples are Australian storage without Australian processing in OpenAI, Azure geography storage with broader Global processing, and Cloudflare's default gateway logging. These are platform design facts, not a recommendation to adopt every enterprise feature at launch.

The existing [platform cohesion RFC](2026-09-23-platform-cohesion-rfc.md) owns detailed page and navigation design; this strategy defines the cross-platform facts those pages must carry. Its proposed signed-in **owner-chat per-spend confirmation** remains a separate money-authority interaction. It is not a repeated privacy approval for every otherwise authorised Agent Call.

## 4. Authority, defaults and data claims

**Customer limits** are hard ceilings on spend, allowed Providers, data classes, locations or effects where AE has enough information to enforce them. **Delegated Agent limits** can narrow that authority. **Agent defaults** save ranking and filtering preferences within the ceiling. **This search's filters** express the immediate need. Hard eligibility is the intersection of all applicable limits; preferences rank only eligible Tools. Search results echo the effective rules and their origin. If no Tool qualifies, explain the blocking rule and permit revision of an Agent preference or search filter; only the Customer changes its hard ceiling. Fallback never silently expands a recipient set.

“Show me” governs which Tools are considered. “Share with” governs which input leaves AE and when. The user should be able to answer: **what do I see; what stays in my Account; what does the selected Provider see; what appears in activity, payment or public settlement records?** Keep the first answer short at the decision point and offer full detail on inspection. An authorised routine path has no extra privacy click; a scope expansion stops before release.

Separate **Provider claim**, **supporting evidence**, **AE observation** and **unknown**. No training, no retention, location and onward recipients are separate assertions with source, version and freshness. A strict Customer rule excludes missing or stale claims. A permissive Customer may choose a Tool with a truthful unknown label. Do not infer that a generic input field called `query` is public content, or that a private payment input is exposed on a public chain merely because settlement has public facts.

For OpenRouter-mediated AI matching, govern the inference endpoint, optional server tools/plugins, cache/files and observability destinations separately. OpenRouter's [data collection](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/data-collection), [ZDR](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr), [in-region routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/in-region-routing), [logging](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/input-output-logging) and [Broadcast](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/broadcast) documentation describe different boundaries. Inference ZDR does not establish a Tool Provider's retention policy; regional routing does not establish Australian residency. A route that can retry against another endpoint must filter every eligible endpoint under the effective Customer rule.

### Routing rules to implement

1. **Separate the two routes.** AI matching routes bounded query/chat content through AE's model gateway to an inference endpoint. Its gateway, hosting operator and model brand may be different parties. Tool procurement routes one proposed input to the selected Tool Provider for a price probe and Call. Give each route its own eligibility and disclosure decision; neither inherits the other's ZDR or retention claim.
2. **Filter, then rank.** Before either network send, compute the eligible endpoints from Customer limits, Agent authority, request constraints and current Provider evidence. If the set is empty, return a reason. Optimise price, latency and fit only within that set. Preferences can change ranking; they cannot make an ineligible endpoint eligible.
3. **Make fallback recipient-safe.** AI matching may retry another endpoint only if *every* possible recipient satisfies the same hard rule. OpenRouter's `provider.only`, `provider.data_collection`, `provider.zdr`, exact endpoint slugs and guardrails are useful enforcement inputs; AE still owns the Customer-level decision and must verify the effective account configuration. Base Provider slugs can include multiple regions. A Tool Call has no automatic cross-Provider fallback after Quote.
4. **Bind the Tool route once chosen.** Search may compare Providers. Quote inspection selects one Tool version, Provider, destination, terms, input digest and price ceiling. Recheck those facts before the unpaid probe and before Call dispatch. A changed destination or term requires a new Quote. Redirects may not widen the chosen recipient.
5. **Minimise each release.** Search receives a short capability need, not the whole customer file. The unpaid x402 probe sends only what the endpoint needs for its 402 price response; if the endpoint requires full input, label that fact and apply standing release authority before probing. Tool credentials remain scoped server-side rather than being placed in ordinary input fields.
6. **Return evidence of the route.** Search explains applied constraints and exclusions. Quote reports any price-probe disclosure that already occurred. `call.status` reports actual attempts, recipient, money and delivery separately. An unsuccessful or timed-out request must say whether release is known, absent or uncertain.

These rules leave room for a future non-x402 Provider and multiple model gateways: the platform standard is the **eligibility, binding and evidence contract**, while each transport supplies its own adapter. A new routing service or policy database is unnecessary until an actual second caller needs it.

## 5. Execution contract and API direction

The existing Tool, Quote, Call, Account and Spending policy concepts are the design seams. No new market object or generic privacy engine is proposed. The following is a **semantic target**, not an approved wire or storage schema.

| Boundary | Requires | Ensures / invariant | On violation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search or external discovery | Bounded query, effective disclosure route and Customer rules. | Only the stated query is sent to an eligible recipient; filters and reasons are returned with provenance. | Exclude route or explain no match before sending prohibited data. |
| Pre-Quote x402 inspection | Authenticated authority, selected Provider/destination, bounded and classified proposed fields, compatible release rule. | Only those fields go to that Provider to inspect the price. Inspection is recorded as a disclosure event, not a purchase. | Refuse before network send and offer an allowed Tool or safer input. |
| Quote | Current Tool and Provider identity, input digest, authority, supported price, material terms and claim versions. | One inspectable decision binds price, parties, recipient, input, effects and terms. | Unknown or incompatible facts produce an explicit refusal or a clearly labelled choice where Customer policy permits. |
| Call | Current Quote and spending authority; unchanged bound material. | One accepted purchase/effect identity; no silent Provider substitution. Duplicate or concurrent attempts follow that identity. | Refuse stale material before dispatch/charge. After possible dispatch, return status and reconcile rather than suggest a fresh purchase. |
| Receipt / activity | Call identity and observed dispatch, delivery and money facts. | State actual recipient attempts, amount, result or uncertainty, and safe next action. Keep content access separate from transaction metadata. | Mark unknown facts unknown; support remedy without inventing delivery or settlement. |

The future machine contract should carry **effective search filters and exclusion reasons**, **first-release recipient and field list**, **Quote material data claims with provenance and version**, and **Call actual route and purchase status**. Human views can compress these facts. Do not freeze public field names, a universal retention taxonomy or a new database table from this strategy alone. Confirm the first-release contract against real x402 GET and POST Tools and representative Provider terms first.

### Proposed API standard on existing actions

| Existing action | Proposed additive contract | Rule for the caller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `registry.tools.search` | Accept hard filters separately from ranking preferences or saved Agent defaults. Echo effective constraints, their owner, exclusion reasons and source freshness. Return a short data-use summary with provenance for each match. | Send a short need; inspect `noMatch`/`unavailable` separately; never assume a hidden Tool means the query was hidden from AI or directory recipients. |
| `registry.tools.describe` | Expose the Tool's declared data-use claims, evidence freshness and a **price-inspection release preview**: selected recipient, transport, and input field paths that would be sent if the caller Quotes. Label unknowns. | Present the release line before asking for a Quote when buyer attention is needed. The backend must recompute it from the actual input; a listing preview is not authorisation. |
| `tool.quote` | Keep `toolRef` and `input`; evaluate release policy before the first external probe. On success, include bound Provider/destination and material claim versions, exact price, plus a disclosure record for any completed price probe. On refusal, return a stable reason and safe next action without sending blocked data. Define a stable request identity so a client retry does not cause repeated probes; verify whether the existing source-write key suffices before adding a public `idempotencyKey`. | A Quote may have disclosed input already. Do not display it as a purely local read. An authorised Agent can still complete it in one API call under standing policy. |
| `tool.call` | Retain the current `quoteRef` + `idempotencyKey` input. Revalidate bound route, terms and authority; return one Call identity and state. Never accept a new Provider or revised payload in this action. | Retry with the same key and inspect `call.status` after an uncertain response. |
| `call.status` / receipt | Report observed Provider attempt(s), release certainty, delivery, Charge, Purchase status and recovery action as distinct facts. Keep full payload access separately scoped. | A timeout after possible dispatch is an existing Call to inspect or reconcile, not an invitation to buy again. |

Example of the **information categories**, not proposed JSON field names: a search response says “three matches; filtered by Customer: Provider A excluded; by Agent: price over A$5 excluded.” Tool detail says “checking price sends `/document` to Provider B.” Quote says “Provider B received `/document` for price inspection; A$3.20 to call; terms version 4.” Receipt says “Provider B was contacted; Call outcome uncertain; no second Call permitted until status is resolved.” Each statement must be generated from server facts. A `tool.quote` response should not need to echo the full normalized confidential input or include it again in a retry continuation merely to tell the Agent what to do; the current response and some continuations do so, and require contract review before change.

**Error semantics:** malformed or unauthenticated requests use the existing RFC 9457 HTTP problem convention. Expected business refusals remain typed action results with machine-readable codes. Distinguish `policy_blocked_before_send`, `no_eligible_recipient`, `provider_terms_unknown`, `probe_unavailable`, `quote_stale` and `dispatch_uncertain` in the future refusal/status vocabulary rather than collapsing them into “unavailable.” A pre-send refusal has no Provider release or Charge; a post-send timeout has at least a basic guarantee: the Call identity and the uncertainty remain inspectable. Do not mark it safe to retry as a new purchase. These are target semantics; the current enum and HTTP mappings need an explicit compatibility review before any public change.

### Security and confidentiality at the platform boundaries

| Boundary | Required control | Observable proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input and first release | Classify the submitted values independently of Tool schema defaults; minimise the query or Call fields; apply the effective release rule before AI matching, directory search, price probe or paid dispatch. | A disallowed secret sent in a generic `query` property never reaches an external recipient, including the unpaid x402 probe. |
| Routing and endpoint | Bind the selected Provider to the actual HTTPS destination; control redirects, host/DNS changes, credential scope and private-network access. Apply a strict rule to every possible fallback endpoint. | A route or terms change between Quote and Call is refused before dispatch; a fallback cannot reach a disallowed recipient. |
| Content and telemetry | Keep full inputs, outputs and attachments out of generic transaction logs, analytics and external traces by default. Specify access, retention and deletion for Quote input, Call content, cache, files and backups separately from minimal financial evidence. | A receipt and support trace remain useful without exposing content to an analytics destination; deletion can be demonstrated across each retained copy. |
| Provider claims and contract | Record which terms govern Provider retention, training, location and onward recipients; do not inherit an inference Provider's policy for a separate Tool Provider. Treat missing or stale claims as unknown. | A changed declaration or unsupported claim cannot retain a previously valid strict-policy Quote. |
| External output and uncertain effect | Treat Tool output as untrusted content when it returns to an Agent; it cannot grant authority or trigger a new purchase by instruction. Preserve one Call identity after possible dispatch. | A prompt-injection result cannot alter spending policy; timeout recovery does not create a second charge or effect. |

OpenRouter's [provider selection](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection) distinguishes provider data-collection routing from its [ZDR](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr) controls, and the latter explicitly does not govern enabled tools/plugins. Its [response cache](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/response-caching), [files](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/files-api) and [Broadcast](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/broadcast) have separate content paths. AE's own purchase record and any Tool Provider terms therefore require independent treatment. OpenRouter's [Terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms) and AE's Provider agreements need legal review before calling customer inputs contractually confidential; this document makes no such legal conclusion.

## 7. Tradeoffs, triggers and unresolved decisions

**Cost accepted:** truthful data claims, first-release enforcement and an actual-route receipt add Provider evidence work and may reduce eligible supply under strict Customer rules. The benefit is that the buyer can safely use standing authority and later explain the purchase. Missing supply should be visible as a demand signal rather than concealed by relaxing policy.

**Cuts:** no second inference router, general agent runtime, per-Tool app-installation system, universal ZDR badge, Australia-only supply rule, speculative privacy engine, AWS-style subscription flow or new full Trust Center for this entry work. Use existing Tool/Quote/Call seams and extend them when a real caller requires it.

**Market triggers to revisit:** OpenRouter opens broad paid third-party Tool publication and execution; it assumes buyer-facing Seller, terms and remedies for arbitrary services; AE buyers succeed once but do not return; repeated unmet needs produce Provider demand; or buyers ask to invoke AE Tools from OpenRouter-backed apps. Compare live completed tasks and economics at each trigger. [OpenRouter's announced proposed combination with Stripe](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/openrouter-is-joining-stripe/) increases the importance of monitoring, but the announcement did not itself prove a released AE-equivalent service on this research date.

**Decisions still open before public contract work:** the Customer classes eligible at launch; acceptable Provider evidence for retention/training/location claims; handling of sensitive or credential input when x402 Provider terms are unknown; documented AE payload retention and deletion periods; effective deployed OpenRouter settings and agreements; and precise payment-lane facts visible on a public network. These need source, operational and legal review at the relevant release boundary. [OAIC APP 8](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-8-app-8-cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information) and [APP 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information) are relevant Australian primary guidance; this strategy does not declare a particular transaction legally compliant.

### Design integrity review

The design thesis is the first sentence. The constraint that improves the design is AE's existing one-Call Quote/Call boundary: it provides a natural place to bind the Provider, data terms and price without a parallel consent or transaction system. The biggest risk is a false claim of privacy from incomplete Tool metadata or uninspected live routing. The second is extra approval friction that prevents a routine permitted Call. The third is a receipt that claims delivery or recipient certainty after an uncertain dispatch. Scope is deliberately concentrated at first release, Quote and Call; later evidence and enterprise controls follow observed demand. **Integrity: mixed until the source and hosted checks in section 6 pass.** This is a document-only review; no live user interview or end-to-end production test is claimed.
